Anthropic releases Claude Opus 5 with no data retention and stronger image-to-HTML

News time: 2026-07-25T04:25:14.756354+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Anthropic has launched Claude Opus 5, its latest flagship large language model, positioning it as the enterprise-ready successor to Opus 4.8. Unlike Anthropic’s other high-performance model Fable—which requires 30-day data retention for general access—Opus 5 maintains Anthropic’s longstanding enterprise privacy promise: no mandatory data retention for standard use. This distinction makes Opus 5 immediately viable for regulated industries like finance, healthcare, and government, where strict data governance is non-negotiable.

The model also advances multimodal capabilities in practical ways. Early adopters report notably improved performance on image-to-HTML conversion—turning UI screenshots or design mockups into clean, semantic HTML/CSS code that closely follows the source layout. One tester found Opus 5 outperformed Fable 5 on fidelity to design intent, suggesting tighter alignment between visual input and structural output. This isn’t just theoretical: real-world workflows—from rapid prototyping to legacy site modernization—are already leveraging the capability.

Stylistically, Opus 5 preserves the linguistic hallmarks of earlier Claude models—phrases like “carry the argument,” “worth stating plainly,” and recurring syntactic patterns—offering continuity for teams accustomed to Claude’s voice. In contrast, Fable diverges more sharply in tone and structure, making Opus 5 a smoother upgrade path for existing Claude users. Pricing remains unchanged at $5 per million input tokens and $25 per million output tokens, matching Opus 4.8.

Benchmarks show Opus 5 scores 43.3% on Anthropic’s internal FrontierBench v0.1 (mini-SWE-agent variant), reflecting strong reasoning and coding ability—but the real differentiator lies in operational trust and task-specific utility. As AI deployment shifts from experimentation to production, features like zero data retention, predictable behavior, and reliable multimodal output matter more than marginal gains in abstract benchmark scores.
