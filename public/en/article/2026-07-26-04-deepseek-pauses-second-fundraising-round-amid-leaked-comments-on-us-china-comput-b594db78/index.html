DeepSeek pauses second fundraising round amid leaked comments on US-China compute gap

News time: 2026-07-26T04:37:42.119893+00:00 · Source: hackernews

DeepSeek, the Hangzhou-based AI lab behind the widely adopted open-weight models DeepSeek-V2 and DeepSeek-Coder, has suspended its second major fundraising round. The decision follows widespread online circulation of remarks made by founder Liang Wenfeng during a closed investor meeting on July 22, 2026 — particularly his candid assessment of China’s persistent compute infrastructure gap relative to the United States.

According to multiple sources familiar with the matter, DeepSeek informed prospective investors it was halting the round “days after” Liang’s comments went viral. Though the lab did not issue an official statement, Bloomberg reported the pause as confirmed by insiders. The leaked transcript — originally hosted on GitHub but later removed or force-pushed — included Liang’s observation that while Chinese models like DeepSeek, Qwen, and ERNIE now match US frontier performance within ~2.7%, they do so using significantly less compute and at far lower cost — raising questions about the long-term viability of the US’s trillion-dollar scaling strategy.

This pause is not a sign of financial distress: DeepSeek remains well-capitalized, with strong adoption of its open-weight models across enterprise and developer communities. Rather, it reflects strategic recalibration. Liang reportedly emphasized that chasing raw compute scale — without commensurate gains in efficiency, sustainability, or real-world utility — risks misallocating resources. His remarks sparked debate over whether AI advancement is converging toward commoditized, cost-efficient infrastructure rather than ever-larger monolithic models.

The episode highlights how geopolitical realities are reshaping private AI investment. With the US outspending China on AI by 23× (per 2026 Digital InAsia analysis), yet the performance gap narrowing rapidly, investors are re-evaluating assumptions about scalability, ROI, and national competitiveness. DeepSeek’s pause may signal a broader shift: from capital-intensive ‘arms race’ narratives toward leaner, open-weight development grounded in practical deployment and reproducible training methods.
