NeurIPS authors ask if linking figures in rebuttals is safe despite rules

News time: 2026-07-26T04:37:42.565558+00:00 · Source: reddit

A machine learning researcher preparing a NeurIPS rebuttal has raised a common but delicate question on Reddit: can they link to external plots or figures—even though NeurIPS guidelines explicitly prohibit links in rebuttals? The author explains that reviewers requested additional experiments, and presenting those results in tabular form feels inadequate. Visualizations would make the findings clearer and more persuasive—but embedding images directly isn’t possible in OpenReview’s current Markdown environment, and inline HTML or image uploads are unsupported.

NeurIPS’ official stance is unambiguous: rebuttals must be concise, text-based clarifications—not opportunities to add new evidence or supplementary material. According to the 2025 FAQ, rebuttals exist solely to address reviewer questions and clarify misunderstandings during the discussion phase. That means no new datasets, no new baselines, and certainly no external dependencies like URLs. Yet in practice, many authors face pressure to communicate complex results quickly and accessibly—especially when space, formatting limits, or tooling constraints get in the way.

The technical root of the problem lies in OpenReview’s limited Markdown support. While the platform allows basic formatting and LaTeX math, it deliberately disables image embedding and strips inline HTML for security and consistency reasons. As documented in OpenReview’s official guides, authors cannot insert figures natively—even with workarounds like base64 encoding or third-party hosting. This forces trade-offs: either cram dense experimental outcomes into narrow tables (risking misinterpretation), or violate the letter of the rules by linking out (risking reviewer annoyance or procedural penalties).

The poster’s concern reflects broader friction in academic ML publishing: infrastructure hasn’t kept pace with communication needs. They note, pointedly, that OpenReview should support modern Markdown features—including figure embeds—to align with how researchers actually share insights. Their question isn’t about gaming the system—it’s about doing right by clarity, rigor, and fairness under current constraints. For now, the safest path remains summarizing key trends in text or compact tables, while flagging limitations transparently. But as rebuttals grow more central to NeurIPS decisions, this gap between policy and practice may soon demand official attention.
