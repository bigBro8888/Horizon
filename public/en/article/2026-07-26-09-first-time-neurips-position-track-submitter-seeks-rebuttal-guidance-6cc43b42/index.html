First-time NeurIPS Position Track submitter seeks rebuttal guidance

News time: 2026-07-26T04:37:42.565848+00:00 · Source: reddit

A first-time NeurIPS Position Paper Track submitter recently posted on Reddit seeking clarity about the rebuttal process — a relatable moment for many early-career researchers navigating top-tier ML conferences for the first time. The author received mixed but largely constructive reviews (scores of 3, 3, 5, and 7) and a meta-review that included actionable revision suggestions — phrasing like “a revision should include…” — which often signals conditional interest from the Area Chairs (ACs). That hopeful tone prompted practical questions: Do reviewers revise scores after reading rebuttals? Who reads the rebuttal — reviewers, ACs, or both? And what tone and structure actually help?

Unlike standard NeurIPS research tracks, the Position Paper Track is explicitly designed to spark debate around emerging ideas, norms, or directions — not to report completed technical work. Launched experimentally in 2025 and returning for NeurIPS 2026, it prioritizes argumentation, vision, and community impact over empirical validation. This distinction matters: rebuttals here aren’t about defending methodology or fixing bugs, but about clarifying stance, acknowledging limitations, and showing how revisions will sharpen the paper’s conceptual contribution.

The rebuttal period itself is brief — typically one week — and strictly limited in length (often 1,000 words or fewer). Reviewers do not rescore papers during this phase; instead, they may update comments or flag concerns. Final decisions rest with the ACs, who weigh the original reviews, the rebuttal, and any updated reviewer input. A strong rebuttal acknowledges feedback honestly, avoids defensiveness, cites specific line numbers or sections when agreeing, and explains how changes will be implemented — not just whether they’ll be made.

For newcomers, the stakes feel high, but the process is more collaborative than adversarial. As NeurIPS’ own guidance emphasizes, rebuttals are an opportunity to correct misunderstandings, clarify intent, and demonstrate responsiveness — not to win an argument. The fact that this question surfaced publicly reflects how little official documentation exists specifically for the Position Track’s rebuttal norms, underscoring a real gap in onboarding support for junior researchers entering this newer, discussion-oriented forum.
