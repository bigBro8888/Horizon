Qwen3.5-4B with reasoning hits 87% on Swedish medical exams

News time: 2026-07-27T04:49:50.899849+00:00 · Source: reddit

A recent experimental report shows that compact, open-weight language models are now approaching the performance of top-tier proprietary medical AI systems—specifically on Swedish-language medical licensing exams. Using the MedQA-SWE benchmark, researcher /u/AccomplishedCat4770 found that Qwen3.5-4B—without any domain-specific fine-tuning—achieves 77% accuracy out of the box. When equipped with chain-of-thought reasoning (enabled via standard prompting), its score jumps to 87%, narrowly missing the reported 88% achieved by o3, a high-performance medical AI model released in 2025.

This result is notable not just for its accuracy, but for its efficiency: Qwen3.5-4B is a 4-billion-parameter model that runs readily on consumer-grade hardware and requires no closed infrastructure or API access. For comparison, MedGemma-1.5-4B needed supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on historical exam data to reach only 60%—a passing threshold but far from competitive. Meanwhile, Gemma4-E4B matched Qwen3.5-4B’s zero-shot baseline (77%), but did not benefit as strongly from reasoning interventions.

A key technical insight involves managing reasoning length. The author observed that unrestricted reasoning traces often devolved into repetitive formatting loops—filling context windows without yielding answers. To address this, they applied S-GRPO’s ‘early exit’ intervention: inserting a fixed phrase (e.g., ‘Answer:’) after a predetermined number of tokens to force termination of the reasoning step and prompt direct answer generation. This simple heuristic improved reliability more than the full S-GRPO reinforcement learning pipeline—which showed only marginal gains, likely due to limited training resources.

Another surprising finding was Qwen3.5-4B’s cross-lingual reasoning behavior: despite Swedish prompts and answer options, the model consistently generated internal reasoning steps in English. Given that Swedish comprises roughly 1% of typical LLM pretraining corpora, this suggests strong transfer capability—not just in vocabulary, but in structured clinical logic. The full methodology, including reproducible code and ablation studies, is publicly available on GitHub and documented in a detailed blog post.
