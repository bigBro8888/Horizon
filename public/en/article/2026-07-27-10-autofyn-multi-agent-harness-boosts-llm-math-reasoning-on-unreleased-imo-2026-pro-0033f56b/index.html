AutoFyn multi-agent harness boosts LLM math reasoning on unreleased IMO 2026 problems

News time: 2026-07-27T04:49:50.899921+00:00 · Source: reddit

A new benchmark study has tested leading large language models on the unreleased International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) 2026 problems — a rare, contamination-free testbed for high-level mathematical reasoning. Unlike standard benchmarks drawn from public datasets, these six official problems were not available before the competition, ensuring no model could have seen them during training. The evaluation focused not just on raw model capability but on how orchestration strategies — especially multi-agent systems — affect performance on deeply structured, proof-heavy tasks.

The results reveal a clear hierarchy: frontier proprietary models like Sol and Fable achieved near-perfect scores regardless of the inference framework used. In contrast, strong but non-frontier models — including Claude Sonnet, Claude Opus, and the open-weight GLM — performed poorly when run through basic web interfaces. Their scores improved meaningfully with provider-specific tooling (e.g., Claude’s built-in code interpreter), and rose further with AutoFyn, a custom-built, modular multi-agent harness developed by SignalPilot Labs. AutoFyn coordinates specialized agents for retrieval, verification, stepwise reasoning, and self-correction — effectively turning monolithic inference into a collaborative problem-solving process.

Yet even with AutoFyn, none of the non-frontier models matched the top-tier performers. On the hardest problem (P3), every sub-frontier model — across all harnesses and even after 20 hours of computation — failed at the same critical insight: a non-obvious algebraic reduction that unlocked the full solution. This highlights a persistent gap: current orchestration can refine execution but cannot reliably generate missing conceptual leaps. Human graders (former IMO medalists) and a separate frontier model cross-verified all solutions, catching hallucinated proofs — such as Sonnet’s false claim on P3 — underscoring that correctness remains fragile in formal domains.

The study also affirms the value of IMO-style problems as intelligence proxies: they demand rigorous logic, long-horizon planning, and domain-aware abstraction — qualities hard to fake or overfit. While AutoFyn doesn’t close the capability gap, it narrows it significantly for capable-but-not-state-of-the-art models, suggesting that intelligent system design may be as important as model scaling in real-world reasoning applications.
