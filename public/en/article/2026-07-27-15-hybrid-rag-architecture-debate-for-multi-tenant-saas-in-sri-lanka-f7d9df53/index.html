Hybrid RAG architecture debate for multi-tenant SaaS in Sri Lanka

News time: 2026-07-27T04:49:50.900072+00:00 · Source: reddit

A developer building a document-focused, multi-tenant SaaS platform in Sri Lanka is grappling with a common yet subtle challenge in production RAG systems: how to reliably answer user questions when their private documents are sparse—without compromising data isolation or citation integrity. The platform must support tenant-specific retrieval over uploaded sensitive documents while also grounding responses in a trusted, curated global knowledge base—like local regulations, industry standards, or public domain resources relevant to Sri Lankan users.

Two architectural paths are under active consideration. Option 1 uses a managed commercial LLM (e.g., OpenAI or Anthropic via Azure AI Foundry or Amazon Bedrock) as the base model, layered with two parallel RAG pipelines: one scoped to each tenant’s private documents, and another querying a centrally maintained, version-controlled global knowledge base. This keeps fine-tuning out of the loop and leverages proven infrastructure for scalability and auditability.

Option 2 proposes fine-tuning an open-source LLM on Sri Lankan or domain-specific public data before applying tenant-isolated RAG. While this could improve domain fluency, it introduces significant operational overhead: ongoing retraining, evaluation drift, hardware costs, and regulatory uncertainty around model weights trained on jurisdictionally sensitive material. The developer rightly notes they lack fine-tuning experience—and that cost and maintenance complexity weigh heavily against it.

What makes this question especially grounded is its attention to real-world constraints: strict data residency requirements, citation traceability for compliance, and the need to scale to thousands of tenants without cross-tenant leakage. Hybrid retrieval—combining keyword, vector, and potentially graph-based search across both private and public corpora—is emerging as the pragmatic middle ground. Tools like RAGFlow and Azure AI Foundry’s agent orchestration layer now support precisely this kind of two-stage, context-aware retrieval, where source attribution is preserved and routing logic enforces tenant boundaries.

Ultimately, the developer’s instinct toward Option 1 aligns with current best practices for regulated, multi-tenant SaaS. Rather than baking domain knowledge into model weights, it’s safer and more maintainable to curate authoritative external sources and retrieve from them dynamically—ensuring answers stay up-to-date, auditable, and legally defensible.
