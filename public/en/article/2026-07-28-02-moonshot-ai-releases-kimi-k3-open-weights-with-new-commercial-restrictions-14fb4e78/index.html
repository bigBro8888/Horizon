Moonshot AI releases Kimi-K3 open weights with new commercial restrictions

News time: 2026-07-28T04:24:57.591869+00:00 · Source: rss

Moonshot AI has made a landmark release: the model weights for Kimi-K3, a 2.8 trillion parameter large language model — one of the largest publicly available LLMs to date. Hosted on Hugging Face, the full weight set occupies 1.56 terabytes, underscoring both its scale and computational ambition. Unlike earlier models that remained proprietary or partially disclosed, Kimi-K3’s weights are freely downloadable and usable for inference and fine-tuning — a major step for the open-weights ecosystem.

But this openness comes with notable strings attached. Moonshot explicitly avoids calling Kimi-K3 'open source' — instead using the precise term 'open weights'. That distinction matters: while users can run and adapt the model, they do not receive training code, data curation details, or reproducible training recipes. More critically, the license introduces tiered commercial constraints. For companies operating 'Model as a Service' (MaaS) businesses with over $20 million in annual revenue, a separate agreement with Moonshot is mandatory before any commercial use — a departure from standard permissive licenses like MIT.

This builds on Moonshot’s prior licensing experiment with Kimi-K2, which required prominent UI attribution for services exceeding $20M monthly revenue or 100M monthly active users. The K3 license drops the 'modified MIT' branding entirely and tightens enforcement around infrastructure-scale deployment. Still, Moonshot deserves credit for transparency: it neither mislabels the license nor hides its conditions. Meanwhile, third-party platforms like OpenRouter have already integrated Kimi-K3 across seven providers — matching Moonshot’s own pricing of $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens.

The release highlights a growing tension in AI governance: how to balance broad accessibility with sustainable business models. As model size balloons and inference costs rise, purely permissive licensing becomes economically challenging for startups. Yet restrictive terms risk fragmenting the open-weights community and complicating compliance for developers and enterprises alike. Kimi-K3 doesn’t resolve that tension — but it forces the conversation into sharper focus.
