New userscript embeds Hacker News discussions as resizable sidebar

News time: 2026-07-29T04:27:46.549073+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Hacker News is beloved for its high-signal links and insightful technical discussions—but reading both the article and its comments has long required juggling two browser tabs. A new open-source userscript called HNewhere eliminates that friction by embedding HN discussions directly into the article page as a collapsible, resizable sidebar.

The script works in two smart ways. First, when you click a link from Hacker News (e.g., on news.ycombinator.com), it opens the target article with the HN discussion panel already loaded on the right. Second, if you land on any external article—say, a blog post or documentation page—that’s previously been shared on HN, HNewhere automatically detects it and adds a subtle toggle button in the top-right corner to summon the discussion panel on demand.

Built as a lightweight userscript—not a full browser extension—it runs via managers like Tampermonkey or Violentmonkey, requires no login or API keys, and respects privacy: it fetches comment data from Algolia’s public HN search API (hn.algolia.com) without storing or transmitting user credentials. The UI is responsive and works on desktop, though some mobile users report needing horizontal scrolling to access the minimize control—a known edge case being refined.

What sets HNewhere apart isn’t just convenience—it’s thoughtful UX alignment. Unlike tab switching or split-view browser features (which require manual activation and don’t persist context), this script preserves the original article layout while surfacing relevant commentary in situ. Users praised its minimalism and configurability, noting how easily it integrates into existing workflows—especially for developers who routinely cross-reference articles with their HN critiques, sometimes even finding counterarguments that reshape their understanding of the topic.

Still, the tool sparks deeper reflection: one commenter observed that relying on side panels may signal an unmet need in modern web UI paradigms—where content and discourse remain artificially siloed. That tension makes HNewhere more than a productivity hack; it’s a small but telling experiment in rethinking how we read and discuss ideas online.
