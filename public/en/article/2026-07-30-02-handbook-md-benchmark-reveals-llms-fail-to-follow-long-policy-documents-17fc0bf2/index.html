Handbook.md benchmark reveals LLMs fail to follow long policy documents

News time: 2026-07-30T04:17:07.064067+00:00 · Source: hackernews

A new benchmark called Handbook.md has exposed a critical weakness in today’s most advanced language models: their inability to consistently follow lengthy, detailed policy documents—even when those documents are explicitly provided in context. Developed by Liudas Panavas and colleagues, the benchmark evaluates how well LLMs adhere to real-world employee handbooks, compliance guidelines, and operational policies across 65 carefully designed tasks. Each task requires the model to reason over hundreds or thousands of tokens of procedural text—such as IT security protocols, HR onboarding steps, or customer service escalation rules—and then act accordingly. The results are stark: no current model, including top-tier commercial and open-weight models, achieves reliable adherence above 60% accuracy, with many dropping below 30% on longer or more nuanced instructions.

This isn’t just a matter of token count—it’s a systemic failure in instruction retention and contextual grounding. Unlike short prompts where models excel at following directives, long-policy scenarios demand sustained attention to hierarchical constraints, conditional logic, and cross-referenced clauses. The paper shows that models often ignore earlier sections, conflate contradictory rules, or invent plausible-but-unauthorized actions when key constraints fall outside their effective working window. These failures mirror known architectural bottlenecks: KV cache compression, quantization artifacts, and limited attention scope—all of which degrade fidelity over long contexts.

The implications extend far beyond academic benchmarks. As AI agents increasingly operate in regulated domains—healthcare workflows, financial reporting, or enterprise automation—reliance on static handbook-style governance is proving dangerously optimistic. Real-world users report similar breakdowns: one engineer described how Claude routinely bypassed explicit ‘no-code-commenting’ rules from a CLAUDE.md file after ~10 minutes of interaction, yet instantly complied when the same rule was repeated mid-task. This suggests that prompt-level reinforcement—not document-level ingestion—is what actually governs behavior.

Handbook.md doesn’t argue that long-context models are broken—it argues they’re being misapplied. The benchmark reframes governance not as a one-time context dump, but as an ongoing, interactive, and often stateful process. For developers building agentic systems, the takeaway is clear: don’t assume your model ‘reads the manual.’ Instead, design for continuous alignment—via tool-calling guardrails, runtime validation, or iterative clarification—rather than hoping a 20,000-token policy doc will magically steer behavior.
