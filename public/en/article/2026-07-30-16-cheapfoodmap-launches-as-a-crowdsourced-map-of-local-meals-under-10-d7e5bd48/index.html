CheapFoodMap launches as a crowdsourced map of local meals under $10

News time: 2026-07-30T04:17:07.064101+00:00 · Source: hackernews

CheapFoodMap is a new, publicly built tool that maps independently owned restaurants offering full meals for $10 or less — no franchises, no chains. Created by a developer who was laid off after 18 years, the project emerged from a self-imposed 100-day challenge to build something useful in public. Inspired by Korea’s 거지맵 (Beggar’s Map) — a student-driven platform listing meals under 7,000 won — CheapFoodMap adapts that ethos to U.S. cities with strict local-only and price-verified criteria.

The site currently features over 1,200 verified meal options across 15 U.S. cities, with heaviest coverage in Texas (where the creator lives in Dallas). Initial entries were seeded from Google Reviews: only businesses rated 4.2 stars or higher with at least 500 reviews qualified, and each listed meal was manually confirmed to cost ≤$10. That dual filter — popularity and affordability — helps surface genuinely good value, not just cheap food.

What sets CheapFoodMap apart is its intentional design constraints: it excludes corporate franchises, rejects sponsored placements, and prioritizes community verification over algorithmic scraping. But sustainability remains an open question. As one commenter noted, GasBuddy succeeded partly because gas stations themselves updated prices — a built-in incentive model CheapFoodMap lacks. The creator openly asks for feedback on how to keep pricing fresh amid inflation, and how users decide whether to trust a $10 claim on a crowd-sourced site.

Beyond utility, the project sparks nuanced discussion about economic reality: truckers, large families, and hourly workers weigh in on what ‘affordable’ really means — not just dollar amounts, but portion size, nutritional value, and accessibility. One user pointed out that a family of eight might consider a $20 total bill ‘under $10 per person’ — revealing how context reshapes price perception. CheapFoodMap isn’t just mapping meals; it’s mapping lived economics.
