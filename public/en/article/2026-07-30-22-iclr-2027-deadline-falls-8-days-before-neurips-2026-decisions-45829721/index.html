ICLR 2027 deadline falls 8 days before NeurIPS 2026 decisions

News time: 2026-07-30T04:17:07.284765+00:00 · Source: reddit

The machine learning research community is raising concerns over a newly announced scheduling conflict between two flagship conferences: ICLR 2027 and NeurIPS 2026. According to the official timeline, ICLR 2027’s full paper submission deadline is set for September 16, 2026 — just eight days before NeurIPS 2026 is scheduled to release its acceptance decisions on September 24. This tight window creates a practical dilemma for authors who submitted to NeurIPS first and may have received constructive feedback or even learned of a borderline rejection that could inform meaningful revisions.

Unlike workshops or smaller venues, both ICLR and NeurIPS are highly selective, peer-reviewed conferences where timing directly affects strategic submission choices. Many researchers traditionally use NeurIPS reviews — when available — to strengthen papers before resubmitting to ICLR, especially given their overlapping scopes in deep learning and foundational ML. With decisions arriving only after ICLR’s deadline, authors lose that opportunity entirely this cycle.

The issue isn’t merely logistical; it touches on fairness and equity. Papers that improved significantly during the NeurIPS review period — perhaps through additional experiments, clearer exposition, or rebuttal-driven refinements — now face an artificial barrier to re-entry. Likewise, authors who received ambiguous or contested rejections at NeurIPS won’t have time to address concerns before ICLR closes submissions.

While conference scheduling is inherently complex — involving venue availability, reviewer recruitment cycles, and editorial calendars — this misalignment stands out because it contradicts longstanding informal norms in the ML community. There’s no public explanation from the ICLR 2027 organizing committee about why the deadline was set so early, nor any indication of flexibility (e.g., a late-breaking or revision track). For now, researchers must adapt by either submitting earlier drafts to ICLR or skipping the cycle altogether — both suboptimal outcomes for scientific rigor and inclusivity.
