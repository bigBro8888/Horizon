University student seeks adults who used AI for friendship or romance

News time: 2026-07-30T04:17:07.285307+00:00 · Source: reddit

Julia Bain, an undergraduate honours student at the University of the Sunshine Coast in Queensland, Australia, is conducting qualitative research into how people form emotional connections with artificial intelligence. Her project focuses specifically on adults who have recently engaged with AI systems—such as Replika, Character.AI, or other companion chatbots—not just for information or productivity, but for companionship, friendship, or romantic interaction. To gather firsthand insights, she has launched an anonymous online survey targeting individuals aged 18 and older who have had such experiences within the past six months.

The survey takes approximately 25 to 30 minutes to complete and asks open-ended and reflective questions about users’ motivations, emotional responses, perceived authenticity of AI interactions, and boundaries they set—or struggle to set—with AI partners. It does not collect personally identifiable information, and all data is handled in accordance with ethics approval S262259 from the university’s Human Research Ethics Committee. Participants are encouraged to share their honest, unfiltered perspectives—there are no right or wrong answers.

This work sits at the intersection of human-computer interaction (HCI), AI ethics, and social psychology. As emotionally engaging AI tools become more widespread and sophisticated, researchers are increasingly concerned about how these technologies shape expectations around intimacy, trust, and relationship norms—especially among younger or socially isolated users. Bain’s thesis contributes to a small but growing body of academic inquiry that treats AI companions not merely as tools, but as relational agents whose use reveals deeper patterns in human social behaviour.

While the survey itself is still recruiting participants—and no findings have been published yet—the initiative reflects a timely shift in academic attention: away from purely technical benchmarks toward lived human experience with AI. It also underscores how undergraduate research can meaningfully engage with urgent, real-world questions about technology and society. For those interested, the full project information sheet and survey link are publicly available, and Bain welcomes questions via email or Reddit comments.
