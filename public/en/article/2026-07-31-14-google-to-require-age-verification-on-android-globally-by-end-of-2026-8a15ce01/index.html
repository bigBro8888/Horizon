Google to require age verification on Android globally by end of 2026

News time: 2026-07-31T04:36:00.263566+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Google has announced it will expand mandatory age verification across Android devices worldwide by the end of 2026 — a move that goes far beyond its earlier, limited pilots in select markets. The rollout centers on the newly launched Age Signals API, part of Android’s broader Digital Credentials framework, which allows apps to request and verify age-related attributes without collecting or storing full identity documents. Unlike traditional ID checks, the system relies on cryptographic digital credentials issued by trusted providers — such as government-issued digital IDs or verified third-party attestations — processed locally on-device via Android’s Credential Manager.

This policy shift responds to mounting regulatory pressure, especially from the EU’s Digital Services Act (DSA) and the UK’s Age Appropriate Design Code, both of which demand robust age assurance for services likely accessed by minors. Google says the goal is to help developers deliver safer, age-appropriate experiences — like restricting access to mature content or limiting ad targeting — while minimizing data collection. Still, the company acknowledges that implementation rests largely with app developers: only apps that integrate the Age Signals API will benefit from standardized, privacy-preserving age signals.

Critics argue the real-world impact may diverge sharply from these intentions. Several commenters point out that mandatory age checks often trigger forced account creation — undermining anonymity and increasing friction for casual users. Others warn that even anonymized age signals could feed into ad-tech pipelines, enabling more precise demographic targeting. As one developer noted, advertisers have long paid premiums for verified age and gender data; integrating such signals at the OS level risks normalizing deeper user profiling under the guise of child safety.

The rollout also raises structural concerns about platform lock-in. Once users verify their age through Google’s ecosystem — tied to Google Wallet or Android’s credential infrastructure — switching to alternative platforms becomes harder, not easier. Meanwhile, enforcement remains uneven: apps that ignore the API, like Telegram or certain sideloaded tools, won’t be subject to the same controls, creating fragmented protection. In short, this isn’t just a technical upgrade — it’s a pivotal test of whether centralized age assurance can balance safety, privacy, and competition without reinforcing digital monopolies.
