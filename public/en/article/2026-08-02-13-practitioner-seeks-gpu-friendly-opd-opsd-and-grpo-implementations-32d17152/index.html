Practitioner seeks GPU-friendly OPD OPSD and GRPO implementations

News time: 2026-08-02T04:34:49.685237+00:00 · Source: reddit

A machine learning practitioner has posted on Reddit asking for accessible, open-source implementations of three emerging LLM alignment techniques—On-Policy Distillation (OPD), On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD), and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)—that can run on consumer-grade hardware like the NVIDIA RTX 4090 or upcoming 5090. The request reflects a growing pain point in the LLM community: many cutting-edge alignment methods are described in academic papers but lack ready-to-run, memory-efficient codebases. Without access to multi-GPU clusters or cloud credits, learners and independent researchers struggle to empirically compare how these algorithms behave—especially in terms of training stability, sample efficiency, and final alignment quality.

OPD and OPSD represent alternatives to traditional reinforcement learning. Unlike standard RL approaches that optimize a scalar reward signal via policy gradients, OPD distills from a teacher model using per-token supervision while sampling on-policy—i.e., generating outputs from the current student model itself. This avoids exposure bias common in supervised fine-tuning. OPSD takes this further by eliminating the need for a separate teacher: the same model acts as both teacher and student, refining its own outputs through iterative self-distillation. GRPO, meanwhile, is a PPO variant designed specifically for LLMs—it estimates advantages using group-normalized rewards instead of a value network, making it more stable in sparse-reward settings and easier to implement without critic overhead.

The poster emphasizes practical constraints: they want not just theory, but working configurations—specific small language models (SLMs), datasets, and hyperparameters—that fit under ~24 GB VRAM. This suggests interest in lightweight yet pedagogically meaningful setups, such as fine-tuning Phi-3, Qwen2.5-0.5B, or TinyLlama on math reasoning or instruction-following tasks using public datasets like UltraFeedback or Self-Rewarding Language Models (SRLM) subsets.

What makes this request especially valuable is its grounding in real-world accessibility. It highlights a gap between algorithmic innovation and implementation maturity—and invites the community to share battle-tested, documented repos. If answered well, it could lower the barrier for newcomers to experiment with post-training alignment beyond basic SFT or DPO, fostering deeper intuition about trade-offs between reward modeling, distillation fidelity, and gradient variance.
