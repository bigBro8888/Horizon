First-time solo author weighs EMNLP vs AACL commitment after borderline ARR review

News time: 2026-08-02T04:34:49.685180+00:00 · Source: reddit

A first-time solo author is facing a pivotal early-career decision: whether to commit their paper to EMNLP or AACL following the ACL Rolling Review (ARR) process for May 2026 submissions. The paper received three reviews with scores that improved after rebuttal — R1 moved from 2.5 to 3 on overall assessment, R2 similarly rose to 3, and R3 held steady at 4 — resulting in a final meta-review score of 3.5, labeled 'Borderline Conference.' The meta-review was broadly positive, praising the paper’s empirical rigor and practical relevance, and noting that the authors successfully addressed major concerns during rebuttal. Interestingly, the Area Chair reportedly leaned toward 3.5 largely due to presentation and readability issues — not technical flaws — and those concerns have since been resolved.

This dilemma reflects a broader shift in how NLP researchers navigate conference commitments. Unlike traditional submission cycles, ARR decouples review from venue selection: papers are reviewed once centrally, then authors choose where to commit based on scores and fit. That means decisions like this one — between EMNLP (the flagship conference of the ACL’s North American chapter) and AACL (the umbrella conference for Asian chapters, launched in 2022) — now hinge less on raw acceptance odds and more on strategic alignment: audience, visibility, career stage, and perceived prestige.

Both venues accept papers into Main or Findings tracks, with Main typically reserved for higher-impact work. Given the 3/3/4 reviewer scores and 3.5 meta-score, the author is likely competitive for either EMNLP Findings or AACL Main — but EMNLP Main remains uncertain. Community norms suggest EMNLP Main tends to favor papers with stronger novelty or broader implications, while AACL Main has shown greater openness to solid, well-executed empirical work — especially from researchers outside traditional hubs.

For early-career scholars, the choice carries weight beyond logistics. Committing to EMNLP may offer wider name recognition in Western academic networks; AACL could signal regional engagement or provide stronger mentorship opportunities through its growing community. Neither decision is objectively 'wrong' — but each shapes visibility, networking, and future submission strategy. Ultimately, the author’s best move may be less about prestige rankings and more about where their work resonates most — and where they want to build relationships.
