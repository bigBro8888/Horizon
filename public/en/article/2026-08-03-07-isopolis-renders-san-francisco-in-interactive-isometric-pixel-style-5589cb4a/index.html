Isopolis renders San Francisco in interactive isometric pixel style

News time: 2026-08-03T04:44:33.719967+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Isopolis is a striking new web-based visualization that transforms San Francisco into a vibrant, navigable isometric pixel map — not hand-drawn, but algorithmically reconstructed from real-world geospatial data. Launched as a 'Show HN' project, the site (sf.isopolis.city) invites users to pan, zoom, and explore the city’s terrain, buildings, and streets in a stylized 3D perspective reminiscent of classic simulation or strategy games. What makes it unusual isn’t just its aesthetic — it’s how it’s built: by scraping Google’s publicly accessible Photorealistic 3D Tiles, then re-rendering them using Three.js with isometric projection and pixel-art-inspired simplification.

The technical pipeline is both pragmatic and clever. Rather than modeling buildings from scratch or relying on imperfect open datasets like OpenStreetMap’s 3D extrusions, creator Lawrence Chen turned to Google’s high-resolution photogrammetric mesh — a dataset originally designed for immersive navigation in Maps and Earth. He supplemented it with freely available USGS LiDAR elevation data to ensure accurate height representation, especially where building footprints or textures were ambiguous. A custom scraper, reportedly assisted by Claude Code, streams tile-by-tile geometry and imagery, which Three.js then composites into a lightweight, GPU-accelerated isometric scene.

Community feedback highlights both admiration and scrutiny. Developers praised the game-dev-level execution — one commenter noted how deceptively hard authentic isometric mapping is, especially when preserving spatial coherence across dense urban terrain. Others pointed out amusing artifacts: AI-generated hallucinations like roads morphing into rivers or phantom square ponds in the Tenderloin district — likely stemming from inconsistencies in Google’s underlying 3D reconstruction or texture stitching. Still, these quirks haven’t diminished enthusiasm; several users suggested practical extensions, like integrating Isopolis into navigation apps or using it for urban planning education.

Beyond its charm, Isopolis demonstrates how public geospatial infrastructure — from Google’s commercial 3D tiles to US government–funded LiDAR archives — can be repurposed creatively by independent developers. It sits at the intersection of open data advocacy, WebGL performance optimization, and retro-futurist cartography — proving that ‘realistic’ and ‘stylized’ aren’t mutually exclusive in modern web mapping.
