NeurIPS 2026 rebuttals submitted early appear invisible to reviewers

News time: 2026-08-03T04:44:33.975124+00:00 · Source: reddit

A NeurIPS 2026 author has raised alarm after submitting their rebuttal several days before the official discussion period began — only to receive no acknowledgment from any of their four reviewers or the assigned Area Chair (AC). The rebuttal was posted via the official OpenReview 'Rebuttal' button on or before July 26, 2026, ahead of the scheduled discussion window opening on July 27 AoE. When the discussion period officially launched, neither the authors nor reviewers involved reported receiving email notifications for those early submissions — suggesting the system failed to trigger alerts entirely.

This isn’t an isolated anecdote. The same author notes that several co-reviewers on this cycle also failed to see rebuttals for papers they were assigned to, despite those authors having used the correct interface. Attempts to surface the issue — including posting visible meta-comments, sending reviewer reminders, and emailing the Program Chairs — yielded no response. With less than 24 hours remaining in the discussion window, the team faces a real risk of their rebuttal being overlooked entirely, potentially undermining their chances at an oral or spotlight presentation.

The timing confusion appears rooted in NeurIPS 2026’s unusually compressed and ambiguously communicated schedule. Multiple external reports confirm widespread frustration over conflicting deadlines: one source describes a 'negative eight-day window' for revision, while others cite track-specific variations and last-minute clarifications. Unlike prior years, where rebuttal windows opened with clear system-wide notifications and AC oversight, this year’s rollout seems to have decoupled submission functionality from notification logic — allowing authors to click 'Submit Rebuttal' without alerting anyone downstream.

For a conference as influential as NeurIPS — where acceptance hinges heavily on how convincingly authors address reviewer concerns — missing rebuttals could distort outcomes, disadvantage diligent submitters, and erode trust in procedural fairness. While the Program Chairs have not yet issued an official statement, the incident highlights systemic fragility in large-scale AI conference infrastructure, especially as review workflows grow more automated and distributed. It also underscores how minor UI or scheduling misalignments can cascade into serious equity issues across thousands of submissions.
