ComfyUI adds native support for MiniMax H3 open-weight video model

News time: 2026-08-04T04:28:35.934974+00:00 · Source: hackernews

ComfyUI — the popular node-based visual workflow tool for generative AI — has integrated MiniMax’s H3 video generation model with day-zero support, marking one of the fastest adoptions of a major new multimodal foundation model. Unlike previous video models that required cumbersome API wrappers or cloud-only inference, this integration brings full local execution to desktop GPUs, including native audio generation, 2K resolution output (up to 15 seconds), and seamless frame-to-frame coherence — all without external dependencies.

The breakthrough hinges on a novel optimization strategy: researchers discovered that roughly 40% of H3’s modulation weights — parameters responsible for dynamic feature adaptation across frames — could be safely pruned and replaced with compact, functionally equivalent lookup tables. This technique slashes the model’s memory footprint by 66%, shrinking it from 123.6 GB in full precision to just 42.5 GB in its smallest variant. Combined with ComfyUI’s dynamic VRAM offloading, the optimized H3 now runs locally on consumer-grade hardware like the RTX 3060 (12 GB VRAM), previously unthinkable for a 2K video model.

Early adopters report mixed but encouraging performance. One user running on an RTX 4070 Ti Super (16 GB VRAM) generated a 10-second, 480p clip in about 10 minutes — slow by real-time standards, but fast enough for iterative creative work. Others highlight impressive fidelity in motion-critical scenes like mouse movement and beverage close-ups, though some artifacts remain in complex physics-driven sequences (e.g., spinning wheel stunts). Several commenters note how well AI-generated wide shots pair with traditional rendering for close-ups — hinting at hybrid production pipelines already emerging in practice.

What makes this especially notable is that MiniMax released H3 as an open-weight model — not just open API access, but fully downloadable, inspectable, and modifiable weights. That openness, paired with ComfyUI’s modular architecture, enables community-driven optimizations like this pruning + lookup table approach. While questions remain about scalability to LLMs and real-world benchmark consistency, the integration signals a shift: high-fidelity multimodal video generation is no longer confined to data centers — it’s becoming a local, editable, and composable creative tool.
