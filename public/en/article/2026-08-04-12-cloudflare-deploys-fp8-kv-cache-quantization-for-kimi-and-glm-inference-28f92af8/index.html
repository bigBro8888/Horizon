Cloudflare deploys FP8 KV cache quantization for Kimi and GLM inference

News time: 2026-08-04T04:28:35.934925+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Cloudflare has rolled out FP8 quantization for the key-value (KV) cache in large language model inference—specifically for open models like Kimi K2.6 and GLM—marking a practical step toward more efficient, scalable, and cost-effective AI serving. In a detailed technical blog post, Cloudflare explains how compressing the KV cache from higher-precision formats (like BF16) down to FP8 slashes memory usage by up to 50%, cuts latency, and allows more concurrent requests per GPU. This isn’t weight quantization—it’s targeted at the dynamic, per-request KV tensors that grow with sequence length and dominate memory bandwidth during decoding.

The optimization works by storing K and V tensors in FP8 format while keeping attention computation in BF16, with dequantization fused directly into the attention kernel. That avoids runtime overhead and preserves numerical stability better than naive INT8 approaches. Cloudflare reports measurable gains: faster token generation, longer context support, and reduced GPU memory pressure—especially valuable for developers deploying open LLMs on constrained infrastructure.

However, the rollout comes with caveats. Validation so far covers only Kimi K2.6—not GLM variants, Qwen, or Llama-family models—raising questions about generalizability across architectures. Community feedback highlights this gap, noting that KV quantization sensitivity varies significantly between model families. Also unaddressed is zero-data-retention (ZDR) policy: users must trust Cloudflare not to log or inspect prompts and responses, as no ZDR guarantee is offered. Pricing remains opaque—visible only inside the dashboard—and alternative quantization schemes like nf4 (used in bitsandbytes) aren’t evaluated despite known advantages for certain workloads.

Still, Cloudflare’s transparency stands out. Unlike many inference providers who treat KV optimizations as black-box internals, they openly document the technique, trade-offs, and limitations. For developers weighing inference latency against fidelity—or building privacy-sensitive agents—the FP8 KV cache offers a compelling middle ground: not as aggressive as INT4, but more efficient than full-precision caches. As vLLM and other runtimes mature FP8 support, this move may accelerate industry-wide adoption of memory-aware attention optimizations.
