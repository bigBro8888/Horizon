NeurIPS reviewers urged to raise scores when rebuttals fully address concerns

News time: 2026-08-04T04:28:36.366071+00:00 · Source: reddit

A NeurIPS reviewer has issued a pointed call for greater consistency and fairness in the conference’s peer review process — specifically urging fellow reviewers to adjust their scores upward when authors successfully address all stated concerns during the rebuttal phase. The post, shared on Reddit’s r/MachineLearning subreddit under the handle /u/undesirable_12, challenges a common but problematic pattern: reviewers acknowledging that their technical or methodological objections were fully resolved in the rebuttal, yet refusing to revise their original score because they ‘don’t vibe with’ the paper’s direction, style, or conceptual framing.

This plea arrives amid ongoing efforts by NeurIPS to strengthen review integrity. As highlighted in official 2025 and 2026 reviewer guidelines, the conference has recently introduced a revised scoring system and launched a ‘responsible reviewing’ initiative focused on quality, timeliness, and accountability. The rebuttal period — a tightly scheduled window (e.g., July 24–30 AoE for NeurIPS 2025) — is designed not as a formality, but as a substantive dialogue where authors clarify, correct, or contextualize their work in response to reviewer feedback.

The core argument hinges on distinguishing between objective critique and subjective preference. A valid concern — such as missing baselines, unclear assumptions, or unreported ablation studies — becomes moot once thoroughly addressed with evidence or reasoning. Holding onto an initial low score despite resolution conflates scientific rigor with personal taste, potentially disadvantaging novel or interdisciplinary work whose value may not align with a reviewer’s immediate intuition. As the author notes, the strength of scientific progress lies precisely in enabling exploration of ideas whose significance isn’t universally apparent at first glance.

While NeurIPS does not mandate score changes post-rebuttal, its guidelines emphasize that reviews should reflect the final state of the submission after rebuttal. This includes updating assessments based on new information — a principle echoed in the 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks Chairs’ reflection blog, which stressed that scoring must evolve with the evidence presented. The Reddit post thus functions less as a policy proposal and more as a community-driven reminder of existing norms — one gaining traction in light of growing awareness about review bias and reproducibility pressures in ML.
