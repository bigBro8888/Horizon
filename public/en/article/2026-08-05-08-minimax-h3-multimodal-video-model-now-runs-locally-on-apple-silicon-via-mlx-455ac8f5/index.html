MiniMax-H3 multimodal video model now runs locally on Apple Silicon via MLX

News time: 2026-08-05T04:26:40.718684+00:00 · Source: rss

MiniMax-H3 — a newly released, open-weight omni-modal generative model capable of producing 15-second video clips with native stereo audio — has been successfully ported to MLX, Apple’s open-source array framework optimized for M-series chips. The port, maintained by PipeNetwork as minimax-h3-mlx, enables developers to run the full multimodal pipeline — accepting text, images, audio, or video inputs — directly on Apple Silicon Macs without cloud dependencies.

The model was launched by MiniMax on July 31, 2026, under the name Hailuo 3.0, and described as a single transformer architecture that jointly processes text, image, video, and audio. Unlike prior text-to-video models that generate silent video and require separate audio synthesis, H3 outputs synchronized video+audio in one pass — up to 2K resolution at 24 fps. Its open-weight release marked a notable shift toward accessible, high-fidelity multimodal generation.

Developer Simon Willison documented a working local setup on an M5 Max MacBook Pro. After downloading ~115 GB of model files — including the FL2VA vision-audio-video backbone and an 8-bit quantized MLX-compatible checkpoint — he generated a 15-second clip from the prompt "a rainbow colored skunk leaps over a mossy log in a supermarket". The result, while visually coherent, included unintelligible speech-like audio — a known limitation when audio prompts are omitted. As noted in MiniMax’s official prompting guide, precise audio control requires explicit instructions (e.g., "cheerful background music", "no speech") alongside the visual description.

Execution took just under 45 minutes on the M5 Max, leveraging MLX’s unified memory architecture to avoid costly CPU-GPU data copies. This contrasts sharply with GPU-bound frameworks like PyTorch on macOS, where such workloads often stall or fail entirely. Though still computationally heavy, the port demonstrates that complex multimodal inference is becoming feasible on consumer laptops — not just data centers. For developers exploring on-device AI, this represents both a milestone and a practical starting point: a fully functional, documented, and open implementation of state-of-the-art video generation.
