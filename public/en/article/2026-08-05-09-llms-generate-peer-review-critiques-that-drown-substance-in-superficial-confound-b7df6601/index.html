LLMs generate peer review critiques that drown substance in superficial confounder lists

News time: 2026-08-05T04:26:41.461176+00:00 · Source: reddit

A growing number of researchers are using large language models (LLMs) to draft or augment peer reviews — but a recent, widely resonant critique warns this practice risks degrading review quality rather than improving it. Drawing from firsthand experience both as reviewer and author, the post identifies three interrelated flaws: an obsessive focus on uncontrolled variables, overly abstract novelty criticisms, and shallow technical comparisons. These aren’t minor quirks — they reflect structural limitations in how LLMs reason about scientific methodology and contextual judgment.

The first issue centers on confounders. LLMs excel at listing possible uncontrolled variables — rainfall, soil microbes, wind patterns — even in well-designed experiments. But real peer review isn’t about enumerating every imaginable factor; it’s about assessing whether any plausible, impactful variable could meaningfully undermine the paper’s central claim. An LLM cannot reliably distinguish between a trivial residual uncertainty and a genuine threat to validity. When reviewers copy such outputs verbatim, authors waste rebuttal time addressing technically valid but practically irrelevant concerns — shifting labor, not insight.

Second, LLMs often criticize novelty at the level of entire research fields (“not sufficiently different from Transformers”) instead of pinpointing specific prior work. Without naming a concrete paper, architecture, or learning objective, such feedback is unfalsifiable and impossible to address. Similarly, LLMs overestimate methodological similarity when two approaches share buzzwords like “attention” or “architecture,” ignoring deep differences in training objectives, assumptions, or implementation. The result is a review that sounds thorough but lacks technical grounding.

Ultimately, the problem isn’t falsehoods — it’s unrestrained plausibility. LLMs generate endless, superficially coherent critiques without weighing relevance, severity, or evidentiary burden. Human reviewers must act as filters: prioritizing only those concerns that could materially affect conclusions, and anchoring each point in precise technical reasoning. Outsourcing judgment to an LLM doesn’t scale rigor — it outsources scrutiny to the authors.
