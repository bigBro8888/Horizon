NeurIPS rebuttal phase sees unusually low author and reviewer engagement

News time: 2026-08-05T04:26:41.460831+00:00 · Source: reddit

A NeurIPS reviewer recently raised alarms about an unusually quiet rebuttal period—where both authors and reviewers appeared to disengage en masse. In their batch of four papers, only one author submitted a formal rebuttal; one withdrew; and two remained completely silent, with no response or withdrawal notice—even though one had borderline review scores. Strikingly, the reviewer was the sole person to reply to the single rebuttal submitted. This pattern goes beyond isolated cases: the poster notes widespread reports of reviewers going silent after submitting initial reviews, and authors vanishing after receiving feedback—sometimes without even withdrawing their submissions.

This isn’t just about bad timing or individual oversight. The silence suggests deeper structural strains in NeurIPS’s peer-review pipeline. With record-breaking submission volumes year after year—NeurIPS 2023 received over 11,000 papers—the system may be hitting diminishing returns on participation incentives. Authors face mounting pressure to submit broadly across venues, while reviewers shoulder increasing loads with little recognition or reward. The result? A growing number may treat the rebuttal phase as optional—or skip it entirely.

The implications extend beyond fairness or efficiency. When rebuttals go unanswered, reviewers lose crucial context for final decisions, and program committees lack evidence to resolve scoring discrepancies or assess clarification requests. Silent withdrawals also complicate meta-review logistics and inflate administrative overhead. More troublingly, this erosion of mutual accountability risks normalizing passive participation—undermining the collaborative, iterative ethos that peer review is meant to uphold.

While the poster wonders whether they simply drew an anomalous batch, the resonance of their observation across Reddit’s machine learning community suggests otherwise. Without intervention—such as clearer expectations, better incentives, or streamlined workflows—the trend could accelerate, further straining trust in one of AI’s most prestigious venues.
