ProvenMetal launches US-based PCB assembly with multi-day turnaround

News time: 2026-08-07T04:02:59.122238+00:00 · Source: hackernews

ProvenMetal, a Y Combinator S26 startup founded by Will and Johnny, has launched a new service that delivers fully assembled printed circuit boards (PCBs) from domestic US facilities in days—not weeks. Unlike traditional contract manufacturers (CMs), which often require customers to source components separately, manage quoting delays, and wait for design-for-manufacturability (DFM) reviews, ProvenMetal automates the front-end of the process: quoting, BOM procurement, DFM feedback, and coordination across bare-board fabricators and assembly partners.

The company’s motivation is deeply rooted in national infrastructure concerns. In 2000, the US produced 30% of the world’s PCBs; today that share has plummeted to just 4%, while China now accounts for 55%. Much of what remains in the US consists of small, family-run CMs using decades-old workflows—labor-intensive, slow to quote, and ill-equipped for modern hardware startups’ rapid iteration needs.

ProvenMetal’s early attempts to build an end-to-end garage-based manufacturing line revealed a key insight: assembly isn’t the bottleneck—it’s the fragmented, manual front office. So instead of scaling low-volume fabrication, they pivoted to orchestration. Their system automatically sources components from US and global distributors, integrates with KiCAD and Altium via plugins to pull BOMs early in design, and pre-orders long-lead parts. They store components in their San Francisco HQ, kit boards, and route them through a vetted network of domestic partners for bare-board fab and assembly.

This model sidesteps the capital intensity of owning factories while tackling real pain points: multi-day email loops with CMs, unpredictable part availability, and opaque timelines. Customers upload design files and get fast quotes, automated DFM feedback, and consolidated delivery—all without managing separate vendors or component logistics. Still, early community feedback highlights unresolved questions: layer count limits, FlexPCB support, pricing transparency, and whether speed comes at a steep cost—especially compared to Shenzhen’s $10–$20 per-board turnkey pricing for simple designs.
