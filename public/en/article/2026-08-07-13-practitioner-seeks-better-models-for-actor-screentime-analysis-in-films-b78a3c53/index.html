Practitioner seeks better models for actor screentime analysis in films

News time: 2026-08-07T04:02:59.577341+00:00 · Source: reddit

A machine learning practitioner on Reddit is seeking production-grade computer vision models to automate actor screentime analysis across full-length movies. Their goal is precise, scalable measurement of screen presence for key roles — protagonist, antagonist, comedic relief, and love interest — by detecting and identifying faces and bodies frame-by-frame. Currently running at 1 FPS, they rely on MTCNN for face detection and TransNetV2 for shot boundary detection, but report limitations: body detection remains unreliable, MTCNN struggles with occlusion and low-resolution frames common in cinematic footage, and TransNetV2 produced at least one false positive in shot segmentation.

The request reflects a real-world multimodal pipeline challenge: combining robust detection (who and where), recognition (which actor), and temporal segmentation (when shots begin/end). Face detection alone isn’t enough — actors may be partially visible, turned away, or appear only from the waist up. Body detection must complement face detection, especially in wide shots or crowd scenes. Meanwhile, accurate shot boundaries are essential to avoid double-counting screen time across cuts and to align detections with meaningful narrative units.

Community responses highlight several modern alternatives. For face detection, RetinaFace and YOLOv8-face are recommended over MTCNN for higher accuracy and speed on diverse poses and lighting; InsightFace and ArcFace-based pipelines are suggested for recognition due to strong cross-pose and cross-illumination performance. For body detection, HRNet and YOLOv10 (with custom person classes) are cited for superior pose-aware localization. Regarding shot detection, alternatives like ShotDetect (lightweight CNN) and newer transformer-based approaches are mentioned, though TransNetV2 remains widely trusted for its balance of speed and F1 score (~95% on standard benchmarks).

Crucially, commenters stress that model choice depends heavily on trade-offs: real-time inference vs. accuracy, GPU memory constraints, and tolerance for false positives in long-form video. One user notes that fine-tuning RetinaFace on film stills — rather than generic web data — improved recall by 18% on side-profile faces. Another recommends caching shot boundaries once per movie and reusing them across multiple actor analyses to reduce redundant computation. Ultimately, the thread underscores that no single ‘best’ model exists — success lies in thoughtful integration, domain adaptation, and iterative validation against ground-truth annotations.
