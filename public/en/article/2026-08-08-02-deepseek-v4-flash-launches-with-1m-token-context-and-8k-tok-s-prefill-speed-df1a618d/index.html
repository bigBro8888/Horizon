DeepSeek V4 Flash launches with 1M-token context and 8k tok/s prefill speed

News time: 2026-08-08T02:59:47.555782+00:00 · Source: hackernews

DeepSeek V4 Flash, released on July 31, 2024, marks a significant milestone for open-weight large language models optimized for real-world deployment. Unlike earlier iterations, this version delivers exceptional throughput—up to 8,000 tokens per second during prefill and roughly 250 tokens per second during autoregressive decoding—on consumer-grade hardware like dual RTX Pro 6000 Blackwell GPUs. Its performance isn’t just theoretical: developers report running six concurrent inference streams on modest setups while spending under $5 per day, thanks to its MoE architecture and aggressive efficiency tuning.

At its core, DeepSeek V4 Flash is a 284-billion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model with only 13 billion parameters active per token—a design that balances scale with practicality. It supports a massive 1-million-token context window, enabled by a novel hybrid attention mechanism combining Compressed Sparse Attention (CSA) and Heavily Compressed Attention (HCA). This architecture slashes KV cache memory usage to just 10% and reduces FLOPs to 27% of what DeepSeek-V3.2 requires for single-token inference at ultra-long contexts.

The model shines in developer-centric tasks: debugging code, analyzing uploaded documents, and powering local AI agents. Early adopters praise its reliability in tool-calling workflows and responsiveness in IDE-integrated assistants—even when handling complex Rust or data analysis prompts. While some users report rare hallucinations or loop behavior in specific agent configurations (e.g., Pi), these appear isolated and not systemic across tested environments.

What sets V4 Flash apart isn’t just raw speed—it’s the convergence of openness, affordability, and production readiness. As an open-weight model licensed for commercial use, it invites fine-tuning, redistribution, and integration into private infrastructure without vendor lock-in. With benchmarks confirming strong coding and reasoning scores—and real-world usage validating low-latency, high-throughput operation—it bridges the gap between research-scale models and deployable engineering tools.
