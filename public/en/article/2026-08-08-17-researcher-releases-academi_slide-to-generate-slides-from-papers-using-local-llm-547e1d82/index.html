Researcher releases academi_slide to generate slides from papers using local LLMs

News time: 2026-08-08T02:59:47.614409+00:00 · Source: reddit

A researcher frustrated by the time-consuming task of turning academic papers into presentation decks has built academi_slide — an open-source tool that automates slide creation entirely on the user’s machine. Unlike cloud-based AI summarizers, academi_slide never sends documents online. Instead, it processes PDFs or plain-text research papers locally, extracting key elements like sections, tables, figures, metrics, and citations before feeding them into a customizable large language model.

The tool supports multiple local inference backends, including Ollama and llama.cpp — two widely adopted frameworks for running open-weight LLMs offline. Users can choose models like Llama 3.1 or others compatible with GGUF format, adjusting prompts and deck structure to match their field or audience. It also handles multilingual input and output, letting users generate slides in English, Spanish, French, or other languages without relying on external translation APIs.

Beyond raw text generation, academi_slide structures outputs as ready-to-use slide decks (in Markdown or PowerPoint-compatible formats) and includes a concise executive summary. The entire pipeline — from parsing to slide layout — runs in minutes on modest hardware, such as a laptop with 16 GB RAM and a modern CPU or integrated GPU. Its modular design allows developers to swap components: custom parsers, prompt templates, or even export targets like Beamer or Reveal.js.

The creator, u/nickemlop, emphasizes privacy and reproducibility: unpublished manuscripts, sensitive clinical data, or proprietary technical reports stay entirely offline. While still in early development, the tool already integrates with common academic workflows — supporting arXiv-style PDFs, LaTeX-generated outputs, and citation-aware summarization. Feedback from early adopters on Reddit highlights interest in tighter integration with Zotero and Jupyter notebooks, suggesting strong potential for expansion beyond standalone use.
