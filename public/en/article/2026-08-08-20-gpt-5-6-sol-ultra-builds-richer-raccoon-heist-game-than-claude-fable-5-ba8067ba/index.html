GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra builds richer raccoon heist game than Claude Fable 5

News time: 2026-08-08T02:59:47.116644+00:00 · Source: rss

Simon Willison recently ran a controlled experiment to compare how two cutting-edge large language models handle game generation from the same prompt: Claude Fable 5 versus GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra running inside Codex Desktop with aggressive sub-agent orchestration. The task was to implement 'Raccoon Heist' — a whimsical premise originally conceived in 2022 using GPT-3 and DALL-E — as a fully playable browser-based game.

Fable 5 produced a functional but minimalist version: a single raccoon collecting coins and fish in a backyard. In contrast, GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra delivered a far more elaborate narrative and gameplay loop — placing the player inside a museum, rescuing two fellow raccoons, stacking them into a tower, and stealing a golden sardine from a display case. Willison dubbed the result Moonlight & Mayhem, highlighting its stronger thematic cohesion and mechanical depth.

The Sol Ultra version wasn’t flawless: it introduced a bizarre visual bug where each raccoon’s head was overlaid with an enormous black sphere — essentially a giant eyeball — four times the size of its body. Crucially, Codex failed to detect or fix this during its 52-minute generation process, even while reviewing screenshots. Willison resolved it manually with two simple follow-up prompts: 'Why do the raccoons have huge black spheres on them?' and 'Fix it.' The resulting patch is publicly available in the GitHub repository.

Beyond output quality, the experiment revealed meaningful differences in architecture and cost. Codex Desktop used GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra’s sub-agent mode — where specialized internal agents handle rendering, logic, asset generation (via gpt-image-2), and code synthesis in parallel. According to AgentsView, the session would have cost $23.28 at full API rates, consuming over 33 million tokens (mostly cached). This reflects both the model’s high computational demand and its efficiency gains: Sol Ultra achieved higher fidelity with less time and fewer output tokens than Fable 5, per OpenAI’s published benchmarks.
