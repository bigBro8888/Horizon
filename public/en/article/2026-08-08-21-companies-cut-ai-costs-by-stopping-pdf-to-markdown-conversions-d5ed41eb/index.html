Companies cut AI costs by stopping PDF-to-markdown conversions

News time: 2026-08-08T02:59:47.116673+00:00 · Source: rss

A quiet but costly AI habit is spreading across enterprises: non-engineers routinely feeding PDFs into large language models (LLMs) — often by converting them to Markdown first — without realizing how dramatically this inflates token usage and cloud bills. A leaked internal Accenture discussion, reported by 404 Media in June 2024, revealed that it’s not developers but business analysts, consultants, and subject-matter experts driving the bulk of token consumption. Justice Kwak, Accenture’s agentic AI strategy lead, confirmed their internal data shows non-technical staff are responsible for disproportionate token burn — especially through workflows like PDF ingestion.

Stuart Henderson, Accenture’s client group lead, joked about a particularly wasteful pattern: turning PDFs into images, then feeding those images to an LLM for OCR and Markdown conversion. His quip wasn’t just humor — it pointed to a real technical trap. When LLMs process PDFs directly (especially scanned or image-heavy ones), many APIs silently convert each page into an image and run multimodal inference — a process that consumes orders of magnitude more tokens than plain text. Even text-based PDFs often contain hidden formatting, fonts, and metadata that bloat token counts unnecessarily.

The fix isn’t theoretical: open-source tools like Microsoft’s MarkItDown can extract clean, semantic Markdown from PDFs before sending them to an LLM — cutting token usage by up to 80%, according to benchmarks. A 25-page academic paper might cost 15,000 tokens as a raw PDF but only 7,000–8,000 tokens once properly converted. Since most commercial LLM APIs charge per token — with rates ranging from $0.01 to $0.03 per 1,000 tokens — these inefficiencies translate directly into six-figure annual overspending for large teams.

This isn’t just about frugality. It reflects a broader maturation in enterprise AI adoption: moving from experimental 'prompt-and-pray' workflows to disciplined AI operations (AIOps). Companies are now auditing token spend like cloud compute, assigning 'token stewards', and embedding preprocessing guardrails into internal AI tooling. As one engineer put it: 'We used to optimize for speed. Now we optimize for tokens — because they’re the new CPU cycles.'
