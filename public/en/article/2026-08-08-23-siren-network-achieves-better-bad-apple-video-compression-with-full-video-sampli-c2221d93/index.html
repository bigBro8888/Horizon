SIREN network achieves better Bad Apple video compression with full-video sampling

News time: 2026-08-08T02:59:47.614176+00:00 · Source: reddit

A recent reimplementation of a SIREN-based neural network demonstrates measurable gains in compressing the iconic 'Bad Apple' video — a widely used benchmark in implicit neural representation research. Rather than training on subsampled frames, the author modified the data sampling strategy to include pixel values from every frame across the entire video sequence. This change significantly improved reconstruction fidelity, even though the underlying architecture remained identical to the original: a 4-layer, 512-width sine-activated MLP with 792,257 parameters.

The improvement highlights how data pipeline design — often overlooked in neural field experiments — can meaningfully affect performance without altering model capacity or training objectives. The author notes that this full-video approach trades off temporal generalization for spatial accuracy: while static frames reconstruct more faithfully, intermediate frames generated by interpolation remain visually nonsensical, revealing that the network memorizes rather than models motion. This confirms a known limitation of vanilla coordinate-based INRs — they lack explicit mechanisms to capture dynamics or optical flow.

To probe alternatives, the author experimented with two extensions: first, training at full native framerate (24 fps), which increased temporal burden and degraded image quality compared to the subsampled version; second, pairing the SIREN with a separate autoencoder to pre-compress frames — resulting in a smaller overall model but lower visual fidelity. Both attempts underscore a core tension in neural video compression: balancing parameter efficiency, reconstruction quality, and true motion understanding.

The work concludes with a concrete suggestion for future progress: integrating an explicit optical flow modeling layer. Such a component could decouple motion estimation from appearance representation — a step toward generalizable, editable, and temporally coherent neural video representations. Code is publicly available in a GitHub gist, enabling replication and further experimentation. While not a breakthrough in architecture, this effort exemplifies rigorous, incremental engineering grounded in empirical observation — precisely the kind of careful iteration needed to advance practical neural compression.
