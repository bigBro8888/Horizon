John Gruber compares technical blogging to live music performance

News time: 2026-08-08T02:59:47.116532+00:00 · Source: rss

In a concise but resonant reflection, John Gruber—longtime writer of the influential blog Daring Fireball—offers a vivid metaphor for how he approaches technical writing. Rather than treating every blog post as a polished, studio-recorded album, he sees most of his work as live musical performance: deliberate, practiced, and responsive to an audience in real time. This mindset prioritizes consistency, timing, and presence over perfection—aiming to ‘hit every note, in time’ while maintaining flow from one piece to the next.

Gruber acknowledges that some posts do rise to the level of ‘album-quality’ work—deeply researched, meticulously edited, and intended to endure. But he stresses these are intentional exceptions, not the default. Trying to make every post a ‘hall-of-famer,’ he says, would paralyze output entirely. The distinction isn’t about quality dropping—it’s about reallocating effort strategically: professionalism in execution, not uniform intensity across all outputs.

This philosophy speaks directly to the pressures many technical writers face today: the expectation to publish frequently and flawlessly, often while juggling engineering or product responsibilities. Gruber’s framing reframes ‘good enough’ not as compromise, but as discipline—a calibrated balance between craft, audience engagement, and sustainable output. It also subtly challenges the cult of the ‘evergreen mega-post,’ suggesting that rhythm and reliability can be just as valuable as singular, monumental pieces.

What makes this especially relevant is Gruber’s stature in tech communication. For over two decades, his writing has shaped how developers, designers, and product people think about Apple, web standards, and software ethics—not through academic rigor alone, but through clarity, voice, and consistency. His analogy doesn’t dismiss depth; it situates it within a broader practice where presence, pacing, and professionalism form the foundation.
