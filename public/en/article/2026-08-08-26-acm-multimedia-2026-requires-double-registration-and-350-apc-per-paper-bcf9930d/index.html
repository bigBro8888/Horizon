ACM Multimedia 2026 requires double registration and $350 APC per paper

News time: 2026-08-08T02:59:47.614335+00:00 · Source: reddit

A researcher preparing to present two accepted workshop papers at ACM Multimedia 2026 has voiced mounting frustration over newly imposed administrative and financial requirements. In a Reddit post, they describe being told by organizers that each accepted paper—whether in the main conference or a co-located workshop—must be linked to a separate author registration. Even though the same person authored both papers, the system mandates two distinct registrations using two different email addresses, as duplicate emails are rejected. This means one person must pay for two full or workshop-only registrations—despite attending only once.

Compounding the burden is ACM’s full transition to open access, effective January 1, 2026. For the first time, every accepted paper now incurs a mandatory Article Processing Charge (APC): $350 for non-members, $250 for ACM members. Unlike past years—where proceedings were bundled into registration fees—the 2026 model decouples publication from attendance. A full in-person registration costs $950 ($850 for members), while a workshop-only registration is $500. To present two workshop papers, the researcher calculates a minimum out-of-pocket cost of $1,850: ACM membership ($99) + main conference registration ($850) + one workshop registration ($500) + two APCs ($250 × 2).

This pricing structure raises concerns about accessibility, especially for early-career researchers, students, and those without institutional funding. The requirement to register twice under different emails also introduces logistical friction—forcing authors to manage multiple accounts, risk credential confusion, and potentially violate institutional email policies. While ACM cites its broader open-access mandate and subsidy-backed APC pricing for 2026 as efforts to increase equity and sustainability, critics argue the implementation lacks flexibility for multi-paper authors and fails to reflect real-world academic workflows.

The issue isn’t isolated to ACM Multimedia—it reflects a wider shift across ACM-sponsored conferences. As open access becomes universal, the financial and procedural weight is shifting from registration-based models to per-paper charges. Whether this leads to more equitable dissemination—or simply erects new barriers for under-resourced researchers—remains an urgent question for the community.
