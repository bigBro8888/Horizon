NeurIPS 2026 workshop calls for real-time conversational AI research

News time: 2026-08-09T03:06:46.885643+00:00 · Source: reddit

The Real-Time Conversational Agents (RTCA) workshop at NeurIPS 2026—set for December 11–12 in Sydney—has officially opened submissions, with a hard deadline of August 29, 2026 (Anywhere on Earth time). This is not just another AI workshop: it targets a critical bottleneck in today’s conversational systems—namely, the gap between offline-optimized models and live, human-paced interaction. While voice assistants, embodied avatars, and full-duplex agents are increasingly deployed, most academic benchmarks still rely on batched, non-streaming evaluation. As a result, real-world agents often feel stiff: turns lag or overlap awkwardly, prosody lacks emotional nuance, backchannels like ‘uh-huh’ or gaze shifts are missing, and interruptions break flow.

The RTCA workshop explicitly centers three interlocking challenges: generating speech, language, and video under strict latency constraints; achieving interactional naturalness—not just fluent utterances, but coordinated timing, turn-taking, grounding, and paralinguistic cues; and evaluating systems while they’re running, not after the fact. Standard metrics like WER or BLEU fail here—so the organizers welcome perceptual studies, interactive Turing-style tests, and new turn-taking or perceived-latency measures. Topics span streaming ASR/TTS, incremental LLM decoding, multimodal alignment under partial observation, real-time avatar generation, and safety-aware design for live agents—including consent, identity preservation, and deepfake mitigation.

Submissions fall into three tracks: full papers (up to 8 pages), short papers (up to 4 pages, including position or critique pieces), and demo papers (up to 2 pages, required for inclusion in the live on-stage Conversational Agents Showcase). All are double-blind, non-archival—authors retain full rights—and undergo single-round review without rebuttal. Confirmed speakers include Dimitris Samaras on visual behavior in dialogue and Evonne Ng on conversational avatar dynamics. The CFP remains open to community input: the organizers explicitly invite feedback on scope gaps and welcome suggestions to refine topics before finalization.

This workshop arrives at a pivotal moment. Industry deployments—from Meta’s seamless interaction efforts to SpeechifyAI’s real-time voice agents—are outpacing academic frameworks for measuring and designing truly interactive systems. By establishing shared vocabulary, benchmarks, and evaluation paradigms focused squarely on live interaction, RTCA aims to catalyze a shift from static, utterance-level modeling toward dynamic, socially grounded conversational intelligence.
