Researchers design and test 16 fully AI-generated viable bacteriophage genomes

News time: 2026-08-10T03:17:52.370019+00:00 · Source: reddit

In a landmark advance for synthetic biology, researchers have successfully designed and experimentally validated 16 fully functional bacteriophage genomes using cutting-edge genome-scale language models—Evo 1 and Evo 2. Unlike prior AI tools that focused on proteins or short regulatory sequences, these models operate at the whole-genome level, learning patterns from millions of prokaryotic and phage genomes. Starting from the well-studied ΦX174—a small, single-stranded DNA phage that infects Escherichia coli—the team prompted the models to generate novel genomic sequences preserving essential genetic architecture and host tropism.

The generated genomes were synthesized in the lab, assembled into infectious viral particles, and tested for viability in bacterial cultures. Remarkably, 16 of the AI-designed phages produced clear plaques on E. coli, confirming replication-competent infection. Phylogenetic analysis showed these phages were evolutionarily distinct from natural ΦX174 relatives—some differing by over 30% at the nucleotide level—demonstrating true generative novelty, not just minor edits.

This work breaks new ground not only in scale but in validation: it is the first time whole-genome sequences generated de novo by a language model have been shown to be biologically functional in wet-lab experiments. Previous efforts with biological LLMs—like those predicting protein folding (AlphaFold) or gene expression—stopped short of full-genome synthesis and phenotypic testing. Evo’s StripedHyena architecture, combined with state-space modeling, enabled long-context reasoning across megabase-scale sequences, a capability critical for maintaining coordinated gene regulation and structural integrity across an entire viral genome.

The implications extend beyond phage therapy. Viable AI-designed phages open paths for precision antimicrobials, programmable delivery vectors, and modular biofoundries. Crucially, this success establishes a feedback loop: experimental data from these 16 phages can now refine future model training, accelerating the co-evolution of AI design and biological testing. As one Reddit commenter noted, this isn’t just ‘in silico’ speculation—it’s wet-lab-verified generative biology.
