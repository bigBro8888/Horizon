Needle2 launches as 14MB agentic LLM for phones and robots

News time: 2026-08-11T03:10:09.019925+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Cactus Compute has released Needle2 — a 14MB, 45-million-parameter, 2-bit quantized large language model explicitly designed for real-time, on-device tool use in resource-constrained environments. Unlike most edge LLMs that prioritize text generation, Needle2 is built from the ground up as an agentic model: its core function is mapping natural language instructions to structured tool calls (e.g., 'lock front door' → lock_door(door="front door")) or schema-driven extraction (e.g., summarizing an email into predefined JSON fields). It runs entirely in 28MB of RAM as a single binary — no dependencies, no GPU drivers, no Python runtime required — making it deployable even on microcontrollers and $150 Android phones without NPUs.

The model achieves remarkable throughput for its size: 500 tokens/sec on a Raspberry Pi 5, 300–700 tokens/sec on budget Samsung A-series phones, and up to 1,500 tokens/sec on high-end VR headsets like the Apple Vision Pro. Crucially, it does this while spending only ~70 MFLOPs per token — 7× to 85× less than comparably capable small LLMs — translating directly to longer battery life and always-on responsiveness. Its architecture draws from Simple Attention Networks (SANs), a recent attention-only transformer variant described in a new arXiv paper, which eliminates feed-forward layers to reduce parameter count and compute without sacrificing functional accuracy on structured tasks.

Needle2 isn’t trying to replace ChatGPT on your phone. Instead, it redefines what ‘intelligence’ means at the edge: narrow, deterministic, confidence-aware, and tightly coupled to device APIs. Every output includes a learned confidence score; if below threshold, the system can gracefully escalate to cloud models like DeepSeek-v4-Flash. Developers can fine-tune Needle2 on custom tool sets in minutes using an automated data-generation pipeline — just provide a few examples, and the framework synthesizes training data. This makes it practical for OEMs to embed domain-specific intelligence into smart home hubs, wearables, or educational robots without ML engineering teams.

The release reflects a broader shift in AI deployment strategy: moving beyond ‘edge vs cloud’ toward hierarchical intelligence, where ultra-light models handle routine, low-stakes actions locally, while heavier models intervene only when needed. As Cactus notes, over 21 billion IoT devices exist globally — but fewer than 1.5 billion are laptops or desktops. Needle2 targets the other 19.5 billion: the phones, thermostats, robot arms, and hearing aids that lack GPUs but still need responsive, private, and reliable AI.
