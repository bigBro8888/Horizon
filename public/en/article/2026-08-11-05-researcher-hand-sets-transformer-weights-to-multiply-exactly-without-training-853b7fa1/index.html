Researcher hand-sets transformer weights to multiply exactly without training

News time: 2026-08-11T03:10:09.506378+00:00 · Source: reddit

Transformers are widely known for struggling with exact arithmetic—especially multi-digit multiplication—even after extensive training. But what if the problem isn’t capability, but how we use them? A researcher named Rob decided to test that idea by bypassing training entirely: instead of letting gradient descent discover patterns, he manually engineered the weights of a Phi-3 transformer to encode multiplication algorithms directly. Using his custom compiler Torchwright, he translated textbook grade-school multiplication—and three alternative approaches (hardware-style bit manipulation, scratchpad-based stepwise computation, and brute-force lookup)—into executable weight matrices. The result? A fully untrained transformer that computes 3-digit × 3-digit multiplication with 100% accuracy across all 3 million valid inputs.

This isn’t just a parlor trick. The project challenges a core assumption in deep learning: that neural networks must learn functions from data. Here, the network’s behavior is fully specified a priori, like writing assembly code into memory. Each version trades off architectural resources differently—some use more layers, others more attention heads or generated tokens—but all compute the same mathematical function deterministically. The researcher published Hugging Face checkpoints supporting up to 12-digit × 12-digit multiplication, inviting others to inspect, modify, or extend the circuits.

To underscore how unusual this performance is, the author benchmarked six state-of-the-art LLMs—including frontier models—on the same multiplication tasks. Their accuracy plummeted as operand length increased: at seven digits, five models scored zero correct answers out of 500 test cases. In contrast, the hand-crafted model maintained perfect accuracy—not because it’s larger or better trained, but because multiplication is literally wired into its weights. This highlights a sharp distinction between statistical approximation and algorithmic implementation in neural networks.

The implications reach beyond arithmetic. By treating transformers as programmable substrates rather than black-box learners, this work opens new paths in mechanistic interpretability—where researchers reverse-engineer circuits inside models—and neurosymbolic AI, which seeks to blend symbolic reasoning with neural flexibility. It suggests that some ‘failures’ of LLMs may reflect misaligned inductive biases or suboptimal training paradigms—not fundamental limitations of the architecture itself.
