Forcing friendliness on LLM outputs harms clarity and reliability

News time: 2026-08-11T03:10:09.020021+00:00 · Source: hackernews

A provocative new essay titled 'Humanising LLM Outputs Is Dumb' challenges a widespread design habit in AI applications: deliberately softening, personalising, or emotionally framing language model responses. The author argues that adding conversational flourishes — like greetings, empathetic phrasing, or enthusiastic tone — doesn’t make interactions more helpful. Instead, it introduces noise, increases cognitive load, and erodes precision — especially in technical, analytical, or safety-critical contexts where users need unambiguous, factual answers.

This critique targets not just chatbot small talk but deeper UX patterns: prompts engineered to elicit ‘friendly’ replies, interface designs that mimic human rapport, and corporate guidelines demanding ‘approachable’ AI voices. The essay contends that such efforts misrepresent what LLMs are — statistical pattern matchers, not sentient collaborators — and distract from their real utility: fast, scalable, objective information processing.

Several commenters on Hacker News echo this concern. One engineer shares a custom prompt that explicitly bans first-person pronouns, emojis, and emotional language, insisting on ‘concise, factual, and complete’ engineering-style output. Another compares over-humanised text to dense literary prose — so stylistically thick that comprehension collapses after a few sentences. A third points out a key technical caveat: forcing stylistic constraints may actually increase hallucination risk, as the model fills gaps with invented, plausible-sounding fluff rather than admitting uncertainty.

The debate reflects a broader tension in AI deployment: between making systems feel familiar and keeping them functionally trustworthy. As LLMs move into code review, medical summarisation, and legal analysis, the cost of ‘friendly’ ambiguity rises sharply. Clarity, consistency, and auditability matter more than warmth — especially when no human is double-checking every sentence.
