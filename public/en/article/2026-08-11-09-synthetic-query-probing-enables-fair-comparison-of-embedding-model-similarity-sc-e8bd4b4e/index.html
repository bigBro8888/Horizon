Synthetic Query Probing enables fair comparison of embedding model similarity scores

News time: 2026-08-11T03:10:09.506550+00:00 · Source: reddit

Choosing the right embedding model for retrieval—whether swapping from OpenAI’s text-embedding-ada-002 to Amazon Titan or evaluating newer open-weight models—is rarely straightforward. A common pain point is that similarity scores from different models aren’t directly comparable: a score of 0.72 from one model may mean something entirely different from 0.72 in another, due to differences in vector dimensionality, training objectives, normalization, and scoring functions. This makes threshold tuning brittle, cross-model A/B testing unreliable, and model migration risky.

Synthetic Query Probing (SQP) tackles this head-on—not by aligning embeddings themselves, but by comparing similarity spaces. The method generates consistent synthetic query–chunk pairs (e.g., a short question paired with a relevant text snippet), then computes similarity scores for each pair across multiple embedding models. Because the inputs are identical and controlled, the resulting score distributions reveal how each model ranks relevance—not just how its vectors sit in space.

The authors demonstrate that SQP uncovers meaningful relationships: Titan models of varying dimensions show near-linear score mappings, while Titan and Ada exhibit strong non-linear relationships and disjoint score ranges (e.g., Titan scores often cluster between 0.6–0.95, whereas Ada spans 0.3–0.8). These patterns allow learning transfer functions—simple regression models—that map scores from one model to another’s scale, enabling apples-to-apples thresholding and retrieval evaluation.

Beyond engineering pragmatism, SQP offers conceptual clarity: it shifts focus from incomparable geometric spaces to observable, task-relevant behavior—how models score matches. As retrieval systems increasingly rely on heterogeneous embedding backends (e.g., hybrid RAG pipelines), SQP provides a lightweight, reference-free, and scalable way to benchmark, calibrate, and interpret model outputs without requiring ground-truth labels or shared training data.
