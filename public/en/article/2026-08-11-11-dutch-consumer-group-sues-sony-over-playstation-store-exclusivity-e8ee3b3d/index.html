Dutch consumer group sues Sony over PlayStation Store exclusivity

News time: 2026-08-11T03:10:09.019978+00:00 · Source: hackernews

A Dutch consumer collective, Massa Schade Consument, has launched a class-action lawsuit against Sony Interactive Entertainment in the European Union. The suit alleges that Sony abuses its dominant market position by restricting all digital PlayStation game and in-game content purchases exclusively to its own PlayStation Store — effectively shutting out third-party retailers from selling download codes or digital entitlements. According to the plaintiffs, this practice violates EU competition law, specifically Article 102 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), which prohibits dominant firms from engaging in conduct that harms consumers or distorts fair competition.

The lawsuit stems from Sony’s decision two years ago to stop authorizing third-party retailers — such as Bol.com, MediaMarkt, or GameStop — to sell PlayStation digital game codes. Previously, consumers could buy downloadable game vouchers from physical or online stores outside Sony’s ecosystem; now, every digital transaction must flow through Sony’s platform. Plaintiffs argue this not only eliminates consumer choice but also enables Sony to set higher prices without competitive pressure — a claim supported by comparative data showing digital versions often cost more than physical discs, despite lower distribution costs.

Legal experts note that while platform owners like Sony have legitimate rights to control their ecosystems, EU antitrust rules draw a line when dominance translates into anti-competitive foreclosure. The case does not challenge game exclusivity (e.g., Spider-Man being PS-only), but rather Sony’s restriction on how consumers access digital content within the PlayStation platform. This distinction is central: it’s not about cross-platform availability, but about intra-platform competition — whether users should be able to buy the same digital game from multiple authorized sellers on the same console.

The outcome could set a precedent for how digital marketplaces are regulated across the EU, especially amid growing scrutiny under the Digital Markets Act (DMA). Though the DMA targets gatekeeper platforms like Apple and Google, this lawsuit relies on older but still active antitrust tools — showing that legacy competition law remains relevant even as new digital regulations evolve. For gamers, the stakes include pricing transparency, resale rights, and long-term control over purchased digital assets — issues increasingly tied to broader debates about digital ownership in an era where physical media is fading.
