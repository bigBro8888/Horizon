CVPR 2026 paper promised dataset but never released it

News time: 2026-08-11T03:10:09.506439+00:00 · Source: reddit

A researcher has raised a formal concern on Reddit about a CVPR 2026 paper whose central claimed contribution—a new dataset—has never been made publicly available. The paper was accepted and published, and even includes a GitHub link in its text; however, that repository remains empty, with no signs of ever having hosted the promised data. The complainant states they attempted to contact the authors directly but received no response—and argues that the failure to release the dataset violates CVPR’s explicit policy for papers claiming dataset contributions.

According to official CVPR 2026 guidelines, any paper selecting "Datasets / Benchmarks" as its contribution type must make that dataset publicly available by the camera-ready deadline. This is not optional: it’s a binding condition for inclusion in the proceedings when such a claim is made. The policy clearly distinguishes between using private data (permissible) and claiming a dataset as a contribution (which obligates public release). In this case, the paper appears to have crossed that line without fulfilling the requirement.

The issue strikes at the heart of reproducibility and accountability in computer vision research. CVPR is one of the most prestigious venues in AI, and its credibility depends on enforceable standards—not just aspirational suggestions. While code submission remains voluntary, dataset release is mandatory if claimed, and reviewers are expected to verify feasibility during review. Yet this case suggests a gap: either the review process missed the lack of readiness, or post-acceptance enforcement mechanisms failed.

There is no centralized public complaint portal for CVPR, which leaves researchers uncertain about proper channels. The official CVPR website directs authors and readers to contact the Program Chairs or Ethics Chair for serious concerns—but those contacts are rarely publicized or easily discoverable. This ambiguity may discourage reporting, allowing problematic cases to go unaddressed. As machine learning increasingly relies on shared benchmarks, consistent enforcement of dataset promises becomes essential—not just for fairness, but for scientific progress.
