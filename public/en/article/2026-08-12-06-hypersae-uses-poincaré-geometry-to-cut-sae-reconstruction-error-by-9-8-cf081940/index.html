HyperSAE uses Poincaré geometry to cut SAE reconstruction error by 9.8%

News time: 2026-08-12T03:28:47.168134+00:00 · Source: reddit

HyperSAE is a new PyTorch library that rethinks how sparse autoencoders (SAEs) represent concepts inside large language models. Instead of placing dictionary atoms in flat Euclidean space — where volume grows polynomially with radius — HyperSAE embeds them in the Poincaré ball, a model of hyperbolic geometry where volume expands exponentially with distance from the origin. This design directly matches the branching, tree-like structure of real-world knowledge and LLM-learned features, which also grow exponentially with depth.

The core insight is geometric: standard SAEs suffer from feature collisions and dead latents when dictionary sizes exceed ~16,000, because Euclidean space can’t accommodate hierarchical expansion without crowding near the boundary. HyperSAE avoids this by projecting dictionary weights into hyperbolic space only during training, while keeping the forward pass fully Euclidean. That means no inference slowdown, no changes to causal steering (still just vector addition), and full compatibility with existing SAE tooling.

A key innovation is the entailment cone loss, which encourages parent concepts to sit near the center of the Poincaré ball and child concepts toward the boundary — mirroring how entailment relationships naturally unfold in hyperbolic space. Combined with TriPartite loss (reconstruction + L1 sparsity + entailment), HyperSAE achieves 9.8% lower mean squared error and reduces dead latents from 3.8% to just 0.2% on Gemma-2-2B Layer 13 trained on FineWeb-Edu. It also slightly improves downstream task recovery (CE loss recovery +3.4 percentage points) and maintains perfect GPQA Diamond accuracy.

The library ships with production-ready features: co-activation queue tracking for dynamic sparsity monitoring, a single-class trainer interface, and full PyTorch integration. Its decoupled dual-speed architecture — Euclidean at inference, hyperbolic only at training — makes it a drop-in upgrade for mechanistic interpretability workflows. As researchers push SAEs toward larger dictionaries and finer-grained feature discovery, HyperSAE offers a principled geometric fix to a long-standing capacity bottleneck.
