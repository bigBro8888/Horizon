AI researcher seeks planning methods for merge puzzle with previewed randomness and afterstates

News time: 2026-08-12T03:28:47.168749+00:00 · Source: reddit

A researcher developing AI for a custom single-player merge puzzle is asking the machine learning community for algorithmic guidance—specifically, how to best plan and learn policies under three distinctive conditions: stochastic chance events that are previewed one cycle ahead, dynamics governed by afterstates (not just next-states), and strict stack-based mechanics with cascading merges. The game resembles 2048 in its action → afterstate → random event flow, but adds complexity: six vertical stacks capped at seven tiles each, actions that move entire contiguous runs of identical top tiles between columns, and deterministic merging cascades that can eliminate tiles valued at 9 to score points.

Crucially, every fourth player action triggers a deterministic drop of six new tiles—one per column—whose values are fully revealed before the fourth action is chosen. This preview breaks standard MDP assumptions: the agent observes part of the future randomness and must condition its decision on it. The random tile values depend on the highest merged tile so far (capped at 7), and empirical observations suggest non-i.i.d. behavior—e.g., alternating streaks of simple (1–2 distinct values) and complex (mixed) drops—hinting at latent temporal structure.

The optimization objective is throughput-oriented: maximize total 9-merges within a fixed 30-minute window (~1,800 actions), where death resets the game but permits immediate restart. This makes it closer to an average-reward reinforcement learning problem than a finite-horizon episodic one. Human players achieve ~115 merges in timed mode; top AI agents show steep learning curves—48 actions for the first 9, then ~18.7 per subsequent 9—highlighting the importance of both cold-start efficiency and mature-board stability.

The current architecture uses a column-permutation-equivariant neural network fed with a 394-feature state encoding: board layout (6×7×9 one-hot), cycle phase, preview values (when present), value cap, and history of empty-column counts over recent cycles. Planning relies on an exact simulator and prioritizes value estimation over long horizons (e.g., time-to-next-9, death risk). Yet open questions remain: how to best exploit preview information in tree search, whether afterstate Q-learning or policy gradient variants converge faster here, and how to allocate limited compute budget across lookahead depth, rollout breadth, and value network updates.
