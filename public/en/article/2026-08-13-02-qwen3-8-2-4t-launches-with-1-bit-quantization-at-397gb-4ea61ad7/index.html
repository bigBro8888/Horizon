Qwen3.8-2.4T launches with 1-bit quantization at 397GB

News time: 2026-08-13T03:32:13.942478+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Alibaba’s Qwen team has released Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B, a groundbreaking open-weight Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) large language model with 2.4 trillion total parameters — yet only 95 billion active per forward pass. What makes it truly notable is not just its scale, but how accessible it is: the model ships with a 1-bit quantized version weighing just 397 GB, enabling inference on high-end consumer workstations equipped with ~1 TB of RAM and multiple high-bandwidth GPUs. This contrasts sharply with typical trillion-parameter models, which usually require multi-node GPU clusters or specialized datacenter hardware.

The model’s performance benchmarks place it near top-tier proprietary systems — specifically between Anthropic’s Claude Opus 4.8 and Fable 5 in reasoning and coding tasks — despite its aggressive compression. Official model cards confirm FP8 and BF16 variants are also available, though the full BF16 version consumes a staggering 4.9 TB of memory. That means the 1-bit version achieves roughly 8% of the memory footprint of the lossless variant while retaining competitive capability — a rare trade-off in today’s LLM landscape.

Unlike earlier MoE releases, Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B is designed for practical deployment, not just research. Its architecture supports standard inference tooling, and Alibaba provides clear licensing terms: free for internal use or organizations earning under $50 million annually, with restrictions above that threshold. However, users note key omissions — notably no built-in vision support or 1-million-token context window, features reserved for the closed-source Qwen3.8-Max variant.

Community response has been deeply technical and measured. Some point out that the lack of quantization-aware training (QAT) for lower-bit variants like Q4 means third parties must invest significant effort to produce production-ready quantizations. Others highlight that while DeepSeek V4-Pro (1.6T-A49B) and Kimi K3 are close competitors, Qwen3.8-2.4T’s 1-bit option uniquely lowers the hardware barrier — even if raw throughput remains constrained. Ultimately, this release signals a shift: massive MoE models are no longer confined to cloud giants’ datacenters.
