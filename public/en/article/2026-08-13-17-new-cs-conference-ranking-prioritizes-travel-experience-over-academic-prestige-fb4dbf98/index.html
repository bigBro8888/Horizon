New CS conference ranking prioritizes travel experience over academic prestige

News time: 2026-08-13T03:32:13.923732+00:00 · Source: reddit

What if the 'best' computer science conference wasn’t the one with the highest CORE rating—but the one where you’d actually enjoy spending a week? That’s the premise behind Honest CS Rankings, a satirical yet fully functional website launched by Reddit user /u/JohnAZoidberg77. Rather than measuring research impact or peer-review rigor, the site ranks over 540 upcoming CORE-ranked conferences by real-world destination quality: local weather during the actual conference month (sourced from historical climate data), national safety (using the Global Peace Index), cost of living (World Bank price-level data), and subjective 'city vibe'—a blend of walkability, cultural energy, and accessibility.

The tool responds to a quiet but widespread pain point in academia: researchers often accept paper rejections with resignation, but agonize over whether to attend a conference based on location—not just for leisure, but for practical reasons like flight costs, visa hurdles, safety concerns, or even seasonal allergies. The site lets users filter by subfield (e.g., ML, systems, HCI), CORE rank (A*, A, B), or open submission deadlines—and even set a home city to sort by travel distance, helping teams decide whether to chase that funded long-haul trip to Lisbon or opt for a low-cost, low-stress regional event in Montreal.

Beyond ranking, it offers pragmatic features: exportable iCalendar (.ics) files for deadline tracking, shareable deep links for co-authors, and an 'Upsets' tab highlighting A*-ranked conferences held in objectively poor destinations—places with extreme heat, high crime, or exorbitant costs. As the creator notes, these venues may look great on a CV but terrible on a credit card statement or travel insurance form. Missing entries (like ICML/ICLR 2027 or COLM) are due to unannounced dates or lack of CORE ranking—not omissions.

While clearly tongue-in-cheek in tone, the project reflects growing researcher skepticism toward traditional metrics. CORE rankings, though widely cited, are updated infrequently and rely heavily on committee judgment—not empirical data about researcher well-being. Honest CS Rankings doesn’t replace those systems; instead, it adds a missing dimension: human-centered logistics. In an era where academic burnout and inequitable travel burdens are increasingly discussed, this tool quietly asks a radical question: what if 'impact' also includes how sustainably—and joyfully—we do our work?
