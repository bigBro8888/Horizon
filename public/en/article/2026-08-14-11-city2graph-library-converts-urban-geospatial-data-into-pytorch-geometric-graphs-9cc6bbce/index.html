City2Graph library converts urban geospatial data into PyTorch Geometric graphs

News time: 2026-08-14T03:28:57.936847+00:00 · Source: reddit

City2Graph is a new open-source Python library designed to bridge the gap between real-world urban data and modern graph machine learning. Unlike traditional GIS workflows that treat cities as collections of isolated layers—buildings, roads, transit stops—the library models them as interconnected, heterogeneous graphs where nodes and edges carry distinct semantic types (e.g., 'building', 'bus_stop', 'street_segment') and rich geometric attributes. This reflects how cities actually function: not as flat tables, but as layered, relational systems where proximity, movement, infrastructure, and morphology jointly shape behavior.

The library supports four core urban domains out of the box. Morphology graphs extract building footprints and street networks from OpenStreetMap or Overture Maps, tessellating urban fabric into spatially aware node-edge structures. Transportation modeling ingests GTFS (General Transit Feed Specification) and GBFS (General Bikeshare Feed Specification) feeds via DuckDB—enabling efficient aggregation into stop-to-stop transit graphs with accurate spatial alignment. Mobility analysis handles origin-destination matrices and flow data (e.g., bike-sharing trips or pedestrian counts) as weighted spatial graphs. Finally, proximity modeling offers flexible graph construction methods—including KNN, Delaunay triangulation, and contiguity rules (queen/rook)—under Euclidean, Manhattan, or network distance metrics.

What sets City2Graph apart is its strict fidelity to geometry across representations. It enables lossless round-trip conversions between GeoDataFrames (for geospatial operations), NetworkX or rustworkx (for graph algorithms), and PyTorch Geometric’s HeteroData (for GNN training). Every node retains its original coordinates and metadata; every edge preserves its topological meaning. The design is grounded in a peer-reviewed paper published in Computers, Environment and Urban Systems, co-authored by researchers from institutions including the University of Liverpool and Universitat Pompeu Fabra.

For urban AI researchers and GeoAI practitioners, City2Graph eliminates much of the tedious, error-prone preprocessing previously required to feed city-scale data into graph neural networks. Instead of writing custom parsers for GTFS or stitching together OSM geometries manually, users can now generate production-ready heterogeneous graphs in just a few lines of code. The library is actively maintained, welcomes contributions, and invites community input on future data source integrations—from mobility APIs to census boundaries or real-time sensor streams.
