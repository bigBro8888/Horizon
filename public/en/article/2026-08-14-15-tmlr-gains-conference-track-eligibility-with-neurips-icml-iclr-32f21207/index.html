TMLR gains conference-track eligibility with NeurIPS ICML ICLR

News time: 2026-08-14T03:28:57.936893+00:00 · Source: reddit

A recent announcement confirms that papers published in the Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR) are now eligible for presentation at three of machine learning’s most prestigious annual conferences: NeurIPS, ICML, and ICLR. This joint journal-to-conference track—formally launched in October 2025—marks a significant step in bridging the traditional gap between journals and top-tier conferences in ML. Unlike conventional journals, TMLR was designed from the outset to support open, iterative, and community-driven review: submissions are rolling, review timelines are shorter than typical journals, and editorial interaction is continuous rather than batched.

The move reflects growing recognition that high-quality ML research doesn’t always fit neatly into rigid conference cycles or traditional journal formats. TMLR’s model—hosted on OpenReview—emphasizes transparency, flexibility, and sustained dialogue among authors, reviewers, and editors. While it lacks the decades-long track record of the Journal of Machine Learning Research (JMLR), founded in 2000, TMLR has quickly attracted strong submissions, especially from researchers valuing reproducibility, revision depth, and visibility outside the hyper-competitive single-deadline conference system.

Still, prestige perception remains nuanced. In academic evaluations—especially for tenure, hiring, or grants—conference publications (particularly NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR) often carry more weight than journal articles, even peer-reviewed ones. Yet TMLR’s new pathway to conference presentation blurs that line: selected papers will be formally presented alongside conference papers, gaining equivalent visibility and citation potential. This doesn’t mean TMLR replaces those conferences—it complements them by offering an alternative route for rigorous, well-vetted work that may benefit from longer development cycles.

For early-career researchers, the implication is practical: publishing in TMLR is no longer just a ‘journal option’ but a strategic choice with dual-channel impact—journal publication plus possible conference spotlight. As one Reddit commenter noted, the quality spectrum in TMLR mirrors that of mid-tier conferences: some papers will be highly influential, others less so—but all undergo transparent, multi-round review. Ultimately, TMLR’s evolution signals a broader shift toward hybrid publishing models in ML, where rigor, openness, and dissemination matter as much as venue hierarchy.
