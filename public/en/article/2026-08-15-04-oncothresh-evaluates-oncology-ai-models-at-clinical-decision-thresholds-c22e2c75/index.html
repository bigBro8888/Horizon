oncothresh evaluates oncology AI models at clinical decision thresholds

News time: 2026-08-15T02:18:21.768345+00:00 · Source: reddit

In oncology AI, a persistent gap has existed between how models are typically evaluated and how they’re actually used in practice. Standard metrics like AUC, ICC, or MAE measure global performance—averaged across all possible thresholds—but clinicians don’t make decisions that way. Instead, they rely on fixed, clinically defined cutoffs: for example, flagging a tumor as PD-L1–positive if expression exceeds 1%, or recommending biopsy when tumor cellularity crosses 30%. oncothresh bridges this gap by shifting evaluation from aggregate statistics to decision-relevant metrics calculated precisely at those predefined thresholds.

The library computes sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, and number-needed-to-test at the exact threshold a clinician would use—not just once, but with bootstrap confidence intervals to quantify uncertainty. It also generates threshold-sensitivity curves, performs boundary-weighted calibration (a technique adapted from medical image segmentation to better calibrate predictions near critical decision boundaries), and conducts decision-curve analysis (DCA) to compute net clinical benefit—a measure increasingly required in regulatory submissions and health economics evaluations. These features are tailored for pathology tasks like Ki-67 scoring, TMB estimation, and PD-L1 quantification, where continuous model outputs must be converted into binary clinical actions.

Complementing the Python library is oncothresh-web: a lightweight, self-hosted no-code dashboard. Users upload a CSV containing model predictions and ground-truth labels, select their clinical threshold, and instantly receive interactive plots—including calibration curves, DCA plots, and confidence-interval–annotated metrics—plus a downloadable PDF report. Built with Docker Compose, it runs entirely offline on local hardware, addressing data privacy and regulatory concerns common in healthcare settings.

Though still in early v0.1 release, oncothresh reflects a growing consensus in clinical AI: evaluation must mirror real-world use. Its design prioritizes interpretability, statistical rigor at the point of care, and accessibility for both computational pathologists and clinical stakeholders who lack coding expertise. As regulatory frameworks like the FDA’s AI/ML Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) guidance emphasize clinical validation at intended use conditions, tools like oncothresh may become essential infrastructure—not just for researchers, but for hospitals deploying AI in routine diagnostics.
