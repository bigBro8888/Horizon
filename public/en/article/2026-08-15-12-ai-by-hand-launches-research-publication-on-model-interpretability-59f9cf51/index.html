AI by Hand launches research publication on model interpretability

News time: 2026-08-15T02:18:21.278670+00:00 · Source: hackernews

AI by Hand is a new research publication from By Hand Research, a group founded by Professor Tom Yeh, dedicated to making AI models understandable—not just usable. Rather than focusing on performance benchmarks or application wrappers, it drills down into the mathematical foundations and algorithmic logic behind deep learning and large language models. Each article walks through derivations, code implementations, and conceptual trade-offs with pedagogical clarity—treating interpretability as something built from first principles, not bolted on after training.

The publication targets researchers, educators, and engineers who feel increasingly disconnected from how modern AI systems actually work. As models grow larger and more opaque, many practitioners rely on high-level APIs without grasping gradient flows, weight updates, or attention mechanics at the tensor level. AI by Hand counters this trend by publishing long-form technical essays—and hosting live seminars—for subscribers. Access to the full research library, including archived articles and seminar recordings, is reserved for members.

This effort sits alongside a growing ecosystem of 'from-scratch' learning resources. Commenters on Hacker News pointed to complementary projects like llm-from-scratch, ml-by-hand, and No Starch Press’s Deep Learning: A Visual Approach. These share AI by Hand’s core philosophy: that true understanding emerges only when you implement, step-by-step, what textbooks describe abstractly. One contributor even traced their own GPT-2 124M implementation back through chronological pull requests—a deliberate, scaffolded path from scalar calculus to transformer inference.

Critically, the project doesn’t claim to solve AI alignment or replace regulatory tools. Instead, it fills a quiet but urgent gap: foundational literacy. When developers can reconstruct softmax from scratch—or derive backpropagation by hand—they’re better equipped to spot bugs, diagnose failures, and design safer abstractions. In an era where AI deployment outpaces comprehension, AI by Hand treats mathematical transparency not as optional rigor—but as prerequisite knowledge.
