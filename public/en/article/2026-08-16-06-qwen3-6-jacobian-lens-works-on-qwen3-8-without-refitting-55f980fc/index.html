Qwen3.6 Jacobian lens works on Qwen3.8 without refitting

News time: 2026-08-16T02:33:38.066818+00:00 · Source: reddit

A new empirical study demonstrates that an interpretability tool—specifically, the Jacobian lens trained on Qwen3.6-27B—retains strong performance when applied unchanged to the newer Qwen3.8-27B model. This finding challenges the common assumption that every model update requires rebuilding or retraining such tools from scratch. The researcher tested transfer across two closely related checkpoints released 113 days apart, sharing identical architecture (64 layers, same hidden dimension), tokenizer, and inference setup (bf16, greedy decoding, single seed). No fine-tuning or adaptation was performed—the exact same lens weights were loaded into the newer model.

The evaluation focused on latent entity retrieval using 40 carefully designed two-hop prompts, where the target entity (e.g., Italy in a prompt about the currency of a boot-shaped country) never appears explicitly. Results showed the transferred lens maintained high concept fidelity: at layer 48, median rank dropped only from 4 to 17; remarkably, at layer 24, it improved—from rank 121 on Qwen3.6 to rank 38 on Qwen3.8 (p < 1e−3). In contrast, the baseline logit lens performed poorly on both models, ranking targets in the thousands. Next-token prediction accuracy also degraded modestly under transfer—by ~1.2–1.3× in mid-layers and ~2× near the final layer—confirming that latent concept readout transfers more robustly than surface-level token prediction.

Steering experiments further validated functional continuity: direction vectors for concepts like 'paradox' and '悖论', extracted solely from Qwen3.6’s Jacobian lens and orthogonally projected into Qwen3.8’s residual stream during generation, successfully suppressed those terms in outputs—without disrupting coherence or factual content—when describing Escher’s staircase. This suggests the underlying conceptual geometry remains stable enough for cross-version intervention.

The work is deliberately narrow in scope—limited to one lens family, one model lineage, and one minor version increment—but its implications are practical and timely. As LLMs evolve rapidly in production, teams deploying interpretability tooling can now test whether existing lenses remain usable rather than assuming mandatory refits. The author openly shares evaluation code, prompt sets, per-layer rank tables, and ablation data on Hugging Face—inviting replication and scrutiny.
