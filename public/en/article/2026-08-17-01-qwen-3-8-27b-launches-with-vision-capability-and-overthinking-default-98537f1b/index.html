Qwen 3.8 27B launches with vision capability and overthinking default

News time: 2026-08-17T02:33:18.655147+00:00 · Source: rss

Alibaba’s Qwen team has released Qwen 3.8 27B — a new, Apache 2.0 licensed, vision-capable large language model with 27 billion parameters. Unlike many recent models that rely on multimodal adapters or separate vision encoders, Qwen 3.8-27B is described as a native vision-language model, meaning image and video understanding are built directly into its architecture. It’s designed for complex, multi-step tasks and ships with a novel ‘reasoning effort’ control — a configurable knob for balancing depth, speed, and cost. At launch, the model’s self-reported benchmarks claim clear gains over both its open-weight predecessor Qwen 3.6-27B and the closed-weight Qwen 3.7-Plus, a top-tier model released just months ago.

The release has drawn immediate attention from developers focused on local inference: at 17GB in Q4_K_M quantization, it fits comfortably on high-end laptops like the M5 Max MacBook Pro — a rare sweet spot between capability and accessibility. Simon Willison, a widely respected technical writer and tool builder, tested it across two systems: his laptop and an NVIDIA DGX Spark server. He used LM Studio’s pre-packaged GGUF build and also ran it via llama-server, confirming consistent behavior across environments.

But there’s a catch — and it’s dramatic. By default, Qwen 3.8-27B activates xhigh reasoning effort, a setting intended for highly complex problems. In practice, this causes the model to burn through enormous token budgets on even simple prompts. Willison observed it consuming all 8,192 tokens of LM Studio’s default context window on trivial queries — prompting him to expand to the full 262,144-token context. Even then, one SVG-generation prompt took 21 minutes and generated over 22,000 reasoning tokens to produce just 3,223 output tokens.

The result? A stunningly accurate pelican-on-a-bicycle SVG — complete with anatomically plausible wings gripping handlebars, correct bicycle geometry, motion lines behind the figure, and a tasteful pastoral background. It’s arguably the best locally runnable SVG generation yet seen. But the trade-off is extreme: that level of fidelity came at the cost of over 20 minutes of compute time on powerful hardware. The takeaway isn’t that the model is flawed — it’s that its default configuration assumes a different use case than most users expect. Switching to medium or low reasoning effort restores responsiveness without collapsing quality — a crucial tuning step every local user must learn.
