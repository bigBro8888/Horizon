SSOG-Attention replaces quadratic attention with separable Gaussians

News time: 2026-08-17T02:33:19.149402+00:00 · Source: reddit

A new attention mechanism called SSOG-Attention offers a compelling alternative to the standard scaled dot-product attention (SDPA) used in transformers. Instead of computing pairwise similarity scores between all query and key tokens — an operation that scales quadratically as O(N²·d) — SSOG models each attention head as a small set of learned Gaussian 'atoms' placed over relative positions. These atoms are separable, meaning their 2D spatial structure decomposes into two 1D filters — one for rows, one for columns — enabling efficient convolution-like computation. Crucially, the query token doesn’t compute dot products; it only applies tiny, bounded geometric nudges to steer the pre-learned Gaussian field. This eliminates the N×N attention matrix entirely.

The result is a theoretical complexity of O(N·√N·d), significantly better than SDPA’s O(N²·d) and competitive with other linear or sub-quadratic methods — but with a distinct geometric intuition. In practice, SSOG-Attention shows faster convergence and stronger generalization on small-data benchmarks like CIFAR-100, while matching SDPA’s final accuracy on large-scale ImageNet-1K. It also scales more gracefully in memory and speed as sequence length grows, making it especially promising for vision transformers (ViTs) processing high-resolution images.

What sets SSOG apart from sparse or low-rank approximations is its foundation in continuous geometry rather than discrete sparsity or matrix factorization. Each head learns a compact, interpretable spatial prior — a handful of smooth, localized Gaussian bumps — which content then subtly repositions. This design avoids the instability sometimes seen in random-feature or kernel-based linear attention, and sidesteps the need for careful masking or top-k selection. The authors open-sourced both the implementation and a detailed blog post with ablation studies, including comparisons to Performer, Linformer, and FlashAttention variants.

While not yet tested on language modeling tasks, the architecture’s strong ViT results suggest broader applicability. Its reliance on relative position and local steering — rather than global token interactions — raises interesting questions about long-range dependency capture. Still, for many real-world vision applications where data efficiency and inference speed matter, SSOG-Attention presents a principled, performant, and surprisingly simple upgrade path from standard attention.
