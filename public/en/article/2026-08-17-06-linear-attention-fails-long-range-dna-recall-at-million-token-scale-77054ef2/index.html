Linear attention fails long-range DNA recall at million-token scale

News time: 2026-08-17T02:33:19.149564+00:00 · Source: reddit

A researcher working on DNA sequence modeling has uncovered a critical limitation in linear attention models: their ability to recall information degrades sharply as context length increases—dropping to ~25% accuracy on Needle-in-a-Haystack-style tests for sequences up to 1 million tokens. Since DNA sequences routinely exceed hundreds of thousands of nucleotides (e.g., human chromosome 1 is ~249M bases), efficient attention mechanisms like linear attention are essential to avoid the O(n²) memory and compute cost of standard softmax attention. Yet despite their scalability, linear attention models—including the state-of-the-art HyenaDNA—struggle to reliably retrieve distant tokens, performing no better than random chance (25% for a 4-token vocabulary: A/C/G/T).

The issue isn’t isolated to one implementation. The researcher tested their own linear attention model and found recall plummeting from ~55% at 16K tokens to ~25% at million-token lengths. Crucially, they replicated the failure on HyenaDNA—the flagship genomic foundation model explicitly designed for million-token contexts—where performance also stagnated at 25–27%. This cross-model consistency suggests the problem lies deeper than engineering choices: it likely stems from how linear attention compresses sequence history into fixed-size state vectors, which may inherently discard or dilute long-range dependencies.

Existing mitigation strategies—external memory modules, sliding windows, hybrid softmax-linear layers—either reintroduce quadratic costs or fail to scale cleanly to genomic lengths. Even architectural tweaks to the linear attention mechanism yielded only marginal gains (up to ~27%), confirming the severity of the bottleneck. The researcher emphasizes that solutions must preserve linear scaling without resorting to expensive attention or large auxiliary memory—requirements that rule out many current workarounds.

This isn’t just a bioinformatics curiosity. As LLMs push toward million-token contexts for code, legal documents, and scientific literature, the DNA domain serves as a rigorous stress test: its uniform token distribution, lack of subword structure, and strict positional sensitivity expose weaknesses masked in natural language. Solving long-range recall in linear attention could unlock truly scalable sequence modeling across domains—provided the root cause isn’t fundamental to the linearization paradigm itself.
