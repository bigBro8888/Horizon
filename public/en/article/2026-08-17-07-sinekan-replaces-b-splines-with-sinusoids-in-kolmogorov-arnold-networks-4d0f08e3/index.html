SineKAN replaces B-splines with sinusoids in Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks

News time: 2026-08-17T02:33:19.149864+00:00 · Source: reddit

A new neural architecture called SineKAN reimagines Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) by swapping their standard B-spline activation functions for smooth, periodic sinusoidal ones. Introduced in a 2024 arXiv preprint and later published in the journal Mathematics (MDPI), SineKAN aims to preserve the theoretical grounding of KANs—rooted in the Kolmogorov–Arnold representation theorem—while improving practical trainability and expressivity. Unlike traditional multilayer perceptrons (MLPs), where weights are scalar parameters and activations are fixed node-level functions, KANs make the edges between neurons learnable via univariate functions. The original KAN design used piecewise polynomial B-splines for these edge functions because they’re flexible, local, and easy to regularize—but they’re only C² continuous and require careful knot placement and gradient clipping during training.

SineKAN proposes a fundamentally different parametrization: each edge function is modeled as a weighted sum of sinusoids—e.g., a₁·sin(ω₁x + φ₁) + a₂·sin(ω₂x + φ₂) + …—with learnable amplitudes, frequencies, and phases. This yields infinitely differentiable, globally smooth functions that avoid spline-specific artifacts like boundary discontinuities or vanishing gradients near knots. According to the authors, this change improves optimization stability, reduces sensitivity to initialization, and enables better approximation of oscillatory or high-frequency target functions—such as those arising in physics-informed learning or signal processing.

The paper reports empirical gains on benchmark tasks including symbolic regression, PDE solving, and classification, often matching or exceeding standard KANs with fewer parameters. Crucially, SineKAN retains full interpretability: since each edge corresponds to a single learned sinusoidal function, users can inspect frequency spectra or visualize phase shifts to understand what features the network has captured. The open-source implementation on GitHub includes training scripts, visualization tools, and notebooks reproducing key experiments—making it accessible for both theoretical exploration and applied use.

While still early-stage, SineKAN reflects a broader trend in neural architecture research: moving beyond hand-crafted, piecewise approximators toward mathematically principled, differentiable basis sets. Its reliance on sinusoids also echoes recent work in implicit neural representations (e.g., NeRF’s positional encoding) and Fourier feature networks—suggesting potential synergy with frequency-domain modeling techniques. For researchers interested in bridging approximation theory and deep learning, SineKAN offers a clean, testable hypothesis: that global smoothness and spectral expressivity may be more valuable than local adaptivity in many function-learning settings.
