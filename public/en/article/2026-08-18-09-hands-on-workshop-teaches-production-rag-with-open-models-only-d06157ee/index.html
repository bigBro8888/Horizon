Hands-on workshop teaches production RAG with open models only

News time: 2026-08-18T02:20:12.042555+00:00 · Source: reddit

On August 29, a hands-on workshop titled The GenAI Build Lab will guide participants through building and benchmarking a production-ready retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system — entirely using open-source models and tools, with zero reliance on proprietary APIs. Led by Ben Auffarth, AI consultant and founder of Chelsea AI Ventures, the session focuses squarely on real-world deployment challenges that often trip up teams moving from prototype to production.

Unlike many introductory RAG tutorials that rely on vector search alone, this workshop emphasizes hybrid retrieval — combining semantic vector search (e.g., FAISS or similar) with keyword-based methods like BM25. This dual approach helps surface relevant documents that pure embedding similarity might miss, especially for rare terms, acronyms, or structured data. Participants will also implement reranking — a second-stage model that reorders retrieved chunks based on contextual relevance — further improving answer fidelity.

Evaluation is treated as first-class infrastructure: the workshop uses RAGAS, an open, reference-free evaluation framework designed specifically for RAG pipelines. Rather than relying on subjective 'vibe checks', RAGAS computes metrics like answer faithfulness, context relevance, and answer correctness — all without requiring ground-truth labels. Guardrails are integrated early in the design phase, not bolted on later, covering input sanitization, output moderation, and hallucination detection. Finally, the lab includes actual cost and latency benchmarking across hardware configurations — helping teams make informed trade-offs between performance, budget, and scalability.

What makes this workshop stand out is its end-to-end, API-free constraint. Every component — from embedding models and rerankers to LLMs and evaluation metrics — runs locally or on self-hosted infrastructure. That discipline forces clarity on dependencies, reproducibility, and operational overhead — critical concerns for MLOps engineers and AI product teams building compliant, auditable, and cost-transparent systems. For practitioners tired of demos that vanish when they try to deploy, this is a rare opportunity to learn what actually works in production — and why.
