How to disable intrusive AI features in everyday software

News time: 2026-08-18T02:20:11.644527+00:00 · Source: hackernews

A growing wave of consumer software and devices now embed AI features not as optional enhancements—but as mandatory dependencies. A new guide at NoToAI.org compiles practical steps for disabling or avoiding these features across browsers, operating systems, and hardware—from turning off Siri to ditching AI-powered search bars and telemetry-laden apps. The site emerged from a Hacker News thread that quickly drew over 300 comments, revealing widespread frustration with design choices that conflate convenience with coercion.

The core tension lies in what developers call fallback functionality: the ability of a system to remain usable when AI components are disabled or unavailable. As one commenter noted, Apple CarPlay requires Siri to be enabled—even though basic functions like music playback and map navigation don’t rely on voice AI. When fallbacks are missing, users aren’t just opting out of AI—they’re locked out of core functionality. This isn’t theoretical: many modern apps now fail silently or degrade entirely without cloud-connected models, leaving no local, offline, or deterministic alternative.

The guide highlights privacy-preserving alternatives—not just philosophical objections. LibreWolf and Waterfox browsers strip AI integrations by default; LibreOffice avoids Microsoft’s cloud-based Copilot features; Linux distributions offer full control over telemetry and model loading. Some users report switching entirely to Linux after abandoning macOS, citing relentless AI integration in recent versions of Safari, Messages, and even system settings. Others point to self-hosted LLM runtimes like Ollama and LM Studio as viable local alternatives—where data never leaves the device.

What makes this moment distinct is scale, not novelty. Unlike early web-era privacy tools (e.g., ad blockers), today’s AI avoidance tactics must contend with deeply embedded OS-level services, opaque model calls hidden in firmware, and business models increasingly tied to AI-driven engagement metrics. Yet the community response shows demand for agency—not anti-technology sentiment, but pro-choice infrastructure. As the site’s creator writes: “This isn’t about rejecting AI. It’s about refusing to let it become the only door to the room.”
