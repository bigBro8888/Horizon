37% of US workers saw real wages fall from 2021 to 2024

News time: 2026-08-19T02:23:11.372642+00:00 · Source: hackernews

A newly released working paper from the University of Chicago’s Becker Friedman Institute reveals a sobering trend in the US labor market: nearly two in five workers—37%—experienced a decline in real wages between 2021 and 2024. Real wages adjust for inflation, meaning these workers’ paychecks bought less over time despite nominal raises or stable salaries. The finding challenges the narrative of broad-based wage recovery following the pandemic and highlights persistent pressures on household purchasing power.

The study distinguishes between workers who stayed in the same job and those who changed employers. Among ‘job stayers,’ only 57% managed to match or exceed inflation—meaning 43% saw their real earnings shrink even without switching roles. Crucially, many of those who did keep pace with inflation did so not through raises at their current jobs, but by moving to new positions offering higher pay. This suggests that internal promotion and merit-based increases remain sluggish, while external mobility serves as a de facto wage adjustment mechanism.

Geographic disparities also appear significant, though not quantified in the paper itself. Commenters noted that high-cost areas like New York and California likely experienced steeper real wage erosion—some estimating declines of up to 20% in effective purchasing power since 2020. That points to a critical nuance: national averages mask sharp regional differences in how inflation and wage growth interact with local housing, taxes, and services.

The paper’s title—‘Sticky Wage Norms and the Real Wage Cost of Unexpected Inflation’—underscores its theoretical anchor: the idea that wages adjust slowly to macroeconomic shocks, especially unanticipated inflation. When prices surge faster than employers can revise pay scales, workers bear the cost—not through layoffs, but through quietly eroding standards of living. For policymakers and employers alike, the findings signal that wage growth alone is insufficient; it must outpace inflation and be distributed equitably across tenure, sector, and location.
