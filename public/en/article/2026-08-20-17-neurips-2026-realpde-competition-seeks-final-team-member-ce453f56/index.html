NeurIPS 2026 RealPDE competition seeks final team member

News time: 2026-08-20T02:21:37.165190+00:00 · Source: reddit

A researcher has posted on Reddit seeking one additional teammate to complete a three-person team for the RealPDE Competition at NeurIPS 2026 — the first NeurIPS scientific machine learning contest centered entirely on real-world physical data. The competition focuses on fluid dynamics, using paired datasets from both real experimental measurements and high-fidelity simulations. Specifically, participants will work with particle image velocimetry (PIV) data — laser-based optical measurements capturing actual fluid flow — alongside computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulation outputs, such as those modeling airflow around a NACA4418 airfoil.

The competition features two official tracks: Simulation-to-Real (Sim2Real) transfer learning and Long-Term Test-Time Adaptation (LTTTA). In Track 1, models must generalize from synthetic CFD data to predict real PIV measurements — a core challenge in physics-informed ML where simulation fidelity rarely matches experimental noise, resolution, or boundary conditions. Track 2 emphasizes robustness over time, requiring models to adapt continuously as real-world sensor conditions drift — mimicking deployment in live industrial or lab environments.

Registration is open until August 20, 2026, and teams are capped at three members. The organizer — identified only as u/Alternative_Push9328 — emphasizes strong machine learning experience as the key qualification, with no explicit requirement for domain expertise in fluid mechanics, though familiarity with PIV or CFD would be advantageous. All competition resources — including data, baselines, and the Codabench evaluation platform — are publicly hosted and governed by research-use-only terms.

What makes RealPDE distinctive is its grounding in real physics data, not synthetic benchmarks. Unlike many prior ML-for-science challenges that rely on idealized or numerically generated ground truth, RealPDE bridges the persistent Sim2Real gap by providing synchronized, co-located real and simulated measurements. This reflects a broader shift in scientific ML toward validation against physical instrumentation — a step toward trustworthy, deployable models in engineering, climate science, and biomedical fluid applications.
