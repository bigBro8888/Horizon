Developer deploys 125M-parameter piano autocomplete model on iPhone 15

News time: 2026-08-21T02:36:17.710041+00:00 · Source: hackernews

A solo developer has built and shipped a real-time, on-device AI music assistant that autocompletes piano performances — not by streaming to the cloud, but entirely within an iOS app running on an iPhone 15. The system works like GitHub Copilot for music: you play a few notes on a MIDI keyboard, and the model instantly generates a musically coherent continuation, hitting ~108 notes per second. This isn’t background generation or offline rendering — it’s low-latency, interactive improvisation powered by a 125-million-parameter transformer, converted and optimized for Apple’s Core ML framework.

The technical ambition is striking. While many on-device AI demos use tiny models (<10M parameters) or rely on server-side inference, this project pushes the edge of what’s feasible on consumer mobile hardware. It leverages Apple’s Neural Engine when available, falling back to GPU or CPU as needed — all handled automatically by Core ML. The developer emphasizes that the model runs entirely on-device: no network calls, no data leaving the phone, and full privacy by design. That constraint shaped every decision — from tokenization (using MIDI events instead of audio waveforms) to quantization and layer pruning.

What makes it more than a tech demo is its grounding in musical practice. Users don’t just get random notes — the continuations reflect stylistic awareness, voice leading, and phrase-level logic. As one commenter noted, this mirrors historical training methods used by classical composers, who internalized reusable melodic-harmonic patterns called Gebrauchs-Formeln. The model doesn’t ‘compose’ in the Romantic sense; it extends idioms, much like a skilled accompanist responding to a soloist in real time.

The app is free and publicly available, and the developer openly shares lessons from what didn’t work — including failed architectures, overfitting on narrow datasets, and latency bottlenecks during early Core ML conversion. In a landscape where AI music tools often prioritize flashy output over usability, this project stands out for its tight coupling of creative intent, engineering rigor, and respect for human agency: the musician remains firmly in control, with the AI acting as a responsive, real-time collaborator.
