ChatGPT now uses site: operator at scale for web search

News time: 2026-08-21T02:36:16.449241+00:00 · Source: rss

ChatGPT’s web search functionality has quietly shifted toward domain-constrained retrieval—using the site: operator in a substantial share of its internal search queries. According to Promptwatch, a third-party observability platform focused on Generative Engine Optimization (GEO), the percentage of ChatGPT’s ‘fanout’ queries containing site: jumped from under 0.5% to 16–17% on August 8, 2026—coinciding with OpenAI’s GPT-5.6 Sol update for Plus and Pro users. Fanout queries are the hidden, rewritten search terms ChatGPT generates behind the scenes to gather information before composing an answer; they’re not visible to users but are critical to how AI-assisted search actually works.

This change suggests a deliberate architectural refinement—not just tweaking ranking or filtering, but restructuring how ChatGPT scopes its search intent. Rather than casting wide nets across the open web, it now frequently restricts searches to specific domains (e.g., site:gov.uk or site:arxiv.org) to improve factual reliability and answer focus. That aligns with OpenAI’s vague but telling August 6 announcement about making GPT-5.6 Sol “more reliable with facts and provide more focused answers.”

The shift also has real-world implications for publishers and SEO practitioners. Promptwatch’s follow-up report on August 18 noted a sharp drop in Reddit citations—likely because ChatGPT is now avoiding low-authority or discussion-heavy domains by design. While OpenAI hasn’t confirmed this, the correlation between the site: surge and Reddit’s decline is strong. It’s also worth noting that these metrics reflect only the subset of prompts Promptwatch actively monitors—not all ChatGPT usage—and that OpenAI continues to obscure its system prompts, making independent verification difficult.

For developers and content strategists, this signals a new layer of optimization: GEO. Unlike traditional SEO—which targets keyword rankings on Google—GEO focuses on how well your site appears inside AI-generated answers. If ChatGPT increasingly relies on site:-scoped searches, then domain authority, technical trust signals (like HTTPS), and consistent topical coverage become even more decisive. The era of ‘searching the web’ inside LLMs is giving way to ‘searching trusted domains’—and that changes who gets cited, and why.
