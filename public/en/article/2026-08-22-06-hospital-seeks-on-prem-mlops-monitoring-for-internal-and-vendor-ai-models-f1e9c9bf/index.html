Hospital seeks on-prem MLOps monitoring for internal and vendor AI models

News time: 2026-08-22T02:20:09.071898+00:00 · Source: reddit

A major hospital running a fully on-premises Red Hat OpenShift cluster is building a self-service MLOps platform to support clinical AI development across multiple teams — all while keeping sensitive patient data strictly inside its walls. With no cloud connectivity allowed, the hospital must implement production-grade model monitoring that satisfies stringent regulatory requirements: EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR 2017/745) and the upcoming EU AI Act. These mandate rigorous post-market surveillance — including real-time usage tracking, data and prediction drift detection, subgroup fairness analysis (e.g., sensitivity per age or gender group), immutable inference logging, and owner-specific alerting.

The team is evaluating two self-hosted platforms: Red Hat OpenShift AI (a natural fit given their existing OpenShift infrastructure) and ClearML (a mature open-core MLOps tool). Both handle development workflows well — notebooks, pipelines, model registry, and serving — but neither offers out-of-the-box, production-ready monitoring at the granularity required. For example, built-in drift detection in OpenShift AI covers data drift and basic bias metrics, but lacks clinical-grade fairness evaluation (e.g., calibration disparities across subpopulations). ClearML provides drift and latency monitoring and integrates with Grafana for dashboards, yet still requires custom engineering to support model-specific clinical metrics and automated inference log ingestion from external vendors.

The most complex challenge lies in monitoring third-party vendor models — which run entirely outside the hospital’s infrastructure. Since the hospital cannot deploy sidecars, inject instrumentation, or access vendor logs directly, it’s negotiating contractual obligations requiring vendors to stream anonymized input/output records for every inference. The hospital then ingests this feed into its own pipeline — using tools like Evidently AI — to compute drift, bias, and custom clinical performance metrics independently. This hybrid approach ensures auditability and regulatory defensibility without relying solely on vendor-reported metrics.

To avoid becoming a bottleneck, the platform must also enable self-service: each model owner needs a live, project-scoped dashboard they can access without central IT intervention. Alerting must route to named individuals, not generic channels. While Evidently + Grafana appears pragmatic and extensible, the team is seeking validation from peers who’ve implemented similar systems in highly regulated, on-prem healthcare environments — especially those balancing internal development velocity with vendor oversight and legal accountability.
