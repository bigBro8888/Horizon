Matt Webb used ChatGPT as a patient tutor to learn quaternions for AR

News time: 2026-08-22T02:20:08.251300+00:00 · Source: rss

When developer Matt Webb added augmented reality to his app Galactic Compass 2, he faced a familiar but formidable challenge: implementing smooth 3D rotations without gimbal lock. Rather than outsourcing the coding task to AI, he turned ChatGPT into an interactive learning partner—asking questions, requesting analogies, clarifying misconceptions, and iterating until he grasped just enough quaternion math to make his AR feature work.

This wasn’t about memorizing abstract algebra or deriving formulas from first principles. It was targeted, just-in-time learning: Webb needed to rotate virtual stars in 3D space relative to device orientation, and quaternions—though notoriously unintuitive—were the standard tool for that job in AR frameworks like ARKit and ARCore. Books and expert friends hadn’t unlocked the concept for him; but a responsive, low-stakes AI tutor did.

What makes Webb’s experience notable is how it reframes AI’s role in technical education. Instead of replacing deep understanding, ChatGPT acted as a scaffold—filling gaps in intuition, offering incremental explanations, and adapting to his pace. He didn’t stop learning because he used AI; he learned more, faster, and with clearer purpose. His goal wasn’t to become a quaternion theorist—it was to ship a working AR compass. And he did.

This reflects a broader shift in how developers engage with complex math and systems-level concepts. As generative AI becomes embedded in IDEs, documentation, and debugging tools, the barrier to applying specialized knowledge drops—not because the knowledge is obsolete, but because the path to functional fluency has shortened. For educators, tool builders, and learners alike, Webb’s story suggests that AI-augmented learning works best not as a shortcut, but as a personalized on-ramp.
