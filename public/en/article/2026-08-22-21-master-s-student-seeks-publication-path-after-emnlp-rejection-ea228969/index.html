Master's student seeks publication path after EMNLP rejection

News time: 2026-08-22T02:20:09.071656+00:00 · Source: reddit

A solo master’s student recently shared their experience of receiving a borderline rejection from EMNLP — one of the top-tier conferences in computational linguistics — despite earning moderately positive review scores: a meta-review of 3 (very positive), and reviewer scores averaging 2.83 out of 4 (with confidence scores averaging 3.67/4). The paper, submitted to the multimodality track, faced no rebuttal feedback, and many cited weaknesses were already addressed in the original manuscript. With internship applications looming and no prior publications, the student urgently needs a venue where their work can appear in time — ideally before application deadlines this fall.

Their immediate question centers on practical next steps: Can they resubmit to ACL Rolling Review (ARR) and use the existing reviews? Or must they start fresh? They’re also eyeing NAACL 2025 — but confusion remains about whether ARR reviews carry over to NAACL submission or if a new ARR submission is mandatory. Crucially, they worry about being stuck in another lengthy review cycle, especially as a time-constrained master’s student without advisor support or lab infrastructure.

According to official ACL ARR guidelines, papers are reviewed anonymously in standard short- or long-paper format, and reviewers assess submissions as if for ACL itself. ARR does not issue final accept/reject decisions — it delivers full reviews so authors can choose how to proceed: revise and resubmit to ARR, submit to a participating conference (like NAACL), or withdraw. Importantly, ARR submissions are not tied to specific conferences by default; authors must explicitly commit reviews to a target venue during Step 3 — and that commitment must happen before the conference’s deadline. For NAACL 2025, the submission deadline has not yet been announced, but NAACL 2024 operated under a model where ARR reviews submitted just before the deadline could be directly committed for consideration — cutting total turnaround to ~3 months.

The student’s best path likely involves revising the paper based on the EMNLP feedback (even without formal rebuttal responses), then submitting to ARR now, selecting NAACL 2025 as the target venue during commitment. This avoids duplicate submissions and leverages ARR’s faster, transparent process. While prior reviewers aren’t automatically reassigned, ARR’s rolling model means new reviewers may be drawn from the same pool — and the revision history itself strengthens credibility. For internship applications, even an ARR-reviewed (but not yet accepted) paper with strong reviews can be listed as "under review at NAACL 2025" — a common and acceptable practice in NLP hiring contexts.
