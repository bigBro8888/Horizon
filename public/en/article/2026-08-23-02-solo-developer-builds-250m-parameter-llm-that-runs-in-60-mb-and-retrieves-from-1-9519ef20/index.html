Solo developer builds 250M-parameter LLM that runs in 60 MB and retrieves from 100M-token disk cache

News time: 2026-08-23T02:36:20.195056+00:00 · Source: reddit

A solo developer has built and open-sourced SHADOW-250M, a fully custom 250-million-parameter large language model trained from scratch on 30 billion tokens of FineWeb data. Unlike typical quantized models that compress pretrained weights, this model was designed end-to-end for extreme efficiency: its weights are quantized to under 2 bits per parameter, resulting in a total deployment footprint of just 60 MB — small enough to run entirely in RAM on modest laptops. It executes at ~400 tokens per second on CPU alone, with no GPU required.

The model’s most striking innovation lies in how it handles long context. Instead of relying solely on memory-bound KV caches, it splits history into two tiers: the most recent 2,048 tokens remain in standard fp16 format in RAM, while all older tokens are compressed to just 1 bit and written to disk — about 320 bytes per token. This enables retrieval from up to 100 million tokens of archived context, stored on disk and accessed on-demand during inference. Crucially, the model was trained from the start to retrieve from this disk-backed store, not just reason over short contexts.

Even more unusually, SHADOW-250M abandons traditional learned token embeddings. Its vocabulary consists of 131,072 fixed 512-bit binary codes — like precomputed hash signatures — totaling only 8.4 MB and containing zero trainable parameters. These codes were validated against WordSim-353, achieving a 0.619 Spearman correlation with human similarity judgments — far above random (0.029). The base model scores 3.15 nats/token (perplexity 23.3) on held-out educational web text, indicating solid language modeling capability despite its size and constraints.

The developer openly acknowledges trade-offs: the model isn’t intended to rival frontier models on open-ended reasoning or factual recall, but rather to demonstrate what’s possible with tight resource budgets and novel architecture choices. It supports fine-tuning, includes reproducible demos, and ships with full training and inference tooling. The GitHub repo has already gained traction, and the Reddit post — initially posted with apprehension — drew overwhelmingly supportive, technically engaged feedback from the ML community.
