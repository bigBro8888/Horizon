DelveRL launches as open-source roguelike designed for RL agent training

News time: 2026-08-23T02:36:20.195198+00:00 · Source: reddit

A new open-source roguelike game called DelveRL has emerged—not for human players seeking thrills, but for AI researchers building game-playing agents. Created by developer /u/SnyderConsulting and shared on Reddit’s r/MachineLearning, DelveRL is explicitly engineered to solve persistent pain points in reinforcement learning (RL) benchmarking: non-deterministic physics, opaque game APIs, missing partial observability, and poor integration with training loops. Unlike commercial or hobbyist roguelikes—such as the unrelated first-person dungeon crawler Delver available on Steam—DelveRL runs entirely locally, offers a clean Python API, guarantees deterministic simulation across all runs, and generates procedurally unique floors with consistent difficulty scaling.

The game is turn-based and endless: agents explore dark, partially visible dungeons, manage limited resources like health and torches, fight monsters with varied behaviors, and attempt to escape each floor before succumbing to exhaustion or combat. Crucially, it ships with production-ready infrastructure—including batched, renderer-free environments that enable high-throughput parallel training—and a fully implemented recurrent Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) baseline. That baseline achieves a median floor completion of 18, with top-performing runs reaching floor 33, establishing an immediate, reproducible performance floor for future work.

What sets DelveRL apart is its dual focus: it’s both human-playable (with keyboard controls and visual feedback) and agent-native (with structured observation spaces, discrete action sets, and built-in reward shaping logic). This bridges a longstanding gap between game design and RL engineering—where most existing benchmarks either lack strategic depth (e.g., grid-worlds) or are too complex to instrument (e.g., NetHack or Minecraft). The project includes full documentation, pre-trained checkpoints, raw benchmark logs, and a lightweight bridge layer to integrate with common RL toolkits like Stable Baselines3 or TorchRL.

The release has already sparked technically grounded discussion on Reddit, with users probing design decisions around action space granularity, generalization across unseen floor layouts, and whether reward sparsity could be mitigated without compromising task fidelity. As RL research increasingly emphasizes generalization, memory, and long-horizon planning, DelveRL offers a compact, controllable, and extensible testbed—one that doesn’t require GPU clusters or cloud APIs to run, yet demands real reasoning about risk, resource trade-offs, and sequential decision-making.
