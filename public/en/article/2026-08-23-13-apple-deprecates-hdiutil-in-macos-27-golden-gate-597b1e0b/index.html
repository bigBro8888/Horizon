Apple deprecates hdiutil in macOS 27 Golden Gate

News time: 2026-08-23T02:36:19.705148+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Apple has officially deprecated the hdiutil command-line utility in macOS 27 'Golden Gate', signaling a shift in how macOS handles disk image operations. Long a staple for power users, administrators, and automation scripts, hdiutil has been used for decades to create, attach, verify, convert, and manage DMG and other disk image formats. Its deprecation does not mean immediate removal — Apple follows a long-standing pattern of marking tools as deprecated years before retiring them, if ever.

The announcement has sparked wide discussion among macOS developers and sysadmins, not so much over whether hdiutil will vanish soon, but over what its deprecation reveals about Apple’s broader tooling strategy. Many point to the xip archive format — deprecated since at least 2014 yet still used to distribute Xcode — as precedent. As one commenter noted, Apple continues shipping Xcode as .xip files despite publicly labeling the format obsolete, suggesting hdiutil may linger for years with only minimal maintenance.

Technically, Apple is steering users toward diskutil image, a newer subcommand introduced in macOS 13 Ventura that overlaps significantly with hdiutil’s core functionality: attaching, detaching, creating, and converting disk images. However, diskutil image lacks several advanced features — such as hdiutil’s support for RAM disks, custom encryption schemes, UDF flattening, or low-level checksum verification — meaning some workflows cannot yet be fully migrated. Documentation and real-world migration guides are still emerging, and third-party tools relying on hdiutil (e.g., CI pipelines, installer builders) now face uncertain maintenance paths.

Beyond technical concerns, the deprecation has become a lightning rod for deeper frustrations: slow bug resolution, opaque developer support channels, and perceived neglect of command-line tooling. One user recounted submitting detailed, reproducible bug reports — only to receive boilerplate replies asking for iOS diagnostics instead of investigating the issue. Others criticized Apple’s $4.5 trillion valuation alongside apparent underinvestment in foundational utilities that serve millions of developers daily. The debate reflects a growing tension between Apple’s consumer-first product rhythm and the needs of professional toolchains that depend on stability and transparency.
