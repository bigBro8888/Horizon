EACL 2027 Industry Track paper submissions due 11 September 2026

News time: 2026-08-24T02:36:12.545616+00:00 · Source: reddit

The European Chapter of the Association for Computational Linguistics (EACL) has opened its Industry Track call for papers for the 2027 conference — and the deadline is just three weeks away. Organizers are urging practitioners from industry, government, non-profits, and public-sector institutions to submit work grounded in real-world language technology deployments. Unlike traditional academic tracks, this venue explicitly centers on lessons learned after research leaves the lab: scalability bottlenecks, user feedback loops, integration with legacy systems, regulatory constraints, and unexpected edge cases that only emerge at scale.

Submissions must be no longer than six pages (excluding references, ethics statements, limitations, and appendices), and a dedicated ‘Limitations’ section is strictly required — papers omitting it will be desk-rejected without review. The track uses double-blind reviewing but allows concurrent arXiv preprints, removing a major barrier for practitioners who often publish early to signal capability or attract talent. Importantly, authors are not required to release proprietary data or models — a pragmatic concession to real-world IP and security concerns.

The conference itself will take place in Athens, Greece, from 9–14 March 2027. Authors will receive notification of acceptance or rejection by 18 December 2026. Submissions go through OpenReview at a dedicated venue page, and the organizing team is also actively recruiting reviewers with hands-on deployment experience — especially those who’ve shipped NLP systems into production environments, managed cross-functional teams, or navigated compliance requirements like GDPR or accessibility standards.

This track fills a persistent gap in computational linguistics venues: rigorous, peer-reviewed publication paths for applied work that doesn’t fit the ‘novel algorithm + benchmark gain’ mold. It signals growing recognition that robustness, maintainability, and societal impact are not footnotes — they’re core research outcomes. For engineers and product leads building chatbots, multilingual search, clinical documentation tools, or public-service chat interfaces, EACL’s Industry Track offers rare academic visibility without demanding unrealistic disclosure or theoretical framing.
