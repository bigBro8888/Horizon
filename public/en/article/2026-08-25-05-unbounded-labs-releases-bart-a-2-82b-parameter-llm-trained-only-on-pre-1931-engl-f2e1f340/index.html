Unbounded Labs releases Bart, a 2.82B-parameter LLM trained only on pre-1931 English text

News time: 2026-08-25T02:22:45.061339+00:00 · Source: reddit

Unbounded Labs has launched Bart — a purpose-built large language model designed not for utility, but for inquiry. With 2.82 billion parameters, Bart was trained from scratch on 20.1 billion tokens of English text published before 1931. Unlike standard LLMs trained on vast, modern web corpora, Bart’s training data excludes all knowledge, events, technologies, and cultural references that emerged after the early 20th century. The team built it to test a foundational question in AI: can statistical language models generate historically plausible original ideas — like those of Einstein or Curie — without exposure to modern science or concepts?

To make Bart possible, Unbounded Labs curated and cleaned one of the largest vintage text collections ever assembled: Harvard’s Institutional Books corpus, reducing it from 242 billion to 23 billion usable tokens. They also created Vintage CORE — the first benchmark suite tailored for vintage LLMs — comprising 20 distinct tasks spanning historical reasoning, counterfactual inference, and temporal grounding. Because no existing evaluation framework applied, they built everything from scratch: training code, SFT datasets (416,000 graded Q&A pairs grounded in pre-1930s sources), and even post-training pipelines using only period-appropriate materials like 19th-century encyclopedias and etiquette manuals.

The project was run entirely on a single H100 GPU over five days, achieving 60% model flops utilization — unusually high for such a constrained setup. The team openly documented every failure, ablation, and design choice, including how OCR noise degraded training efficiency and why certain pre-1931 texts had to be excluded due to temporal leakage. All weights, datasets, and code are open-sourced under permissive licenses. Crucially, Bart is not positioned as a production tool — it’s a research artifact meant to probe the epistemic boundaries of LLMs: what can emerge from pattern recognition alone, when stripped of modern context?

Beyond technical execution, Bart reflects a philosophical stance rooted in Richard Feynman’s dictum: “What I cannot create, I do not understand.” By building an LLM anchored in a fixed historical horizon, the team sought deeper insight into how language models internalize time, causality, and novelty. Their $807 self-funded effort — modest by AI standards — underscores how domain-specific curation and methodological rigor can yield outsized scientific value, even without massive compute. As they note, this is just the beginning: Bart is a stepping stone toward larger vintage models, including plans for GPT-3–scale capabilities frozen in 1930.
