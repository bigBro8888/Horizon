San Francisco recreated as a playable 3D browser game using Apple Maps data

News time: 2026-08-25T02:22:44.911629+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Step into San Francisco—not through a flight or a ferry, but directly in your browser. A new open project, accessible at sf.thijs.gg, renders the entire city in real-time 3D using reverse-engineered Apple Maps Flyover data. Users navigate with keyboard and mouse like in a classic video game: drive cars, walk streets, fly over hills, and even collect floating coins. The experience feels uncannily familiar—especially to longtime residents—thanks to accurate building footprints, terrain elevation, road networks, and textured surfaces pulled from Apple’s high-fidelity satellite and 3D map tiles.

This isn’t a static model or a pre-rendered animation. Built entirely with WebGL and vanilla JavaScript, it runs client-side without plugins or downloads. It leverages techniques pioneered by the retroplasma project, which first cracked Apple’s encrypted Flyover tile delivery system in 2019. Unlike earlier experiments that required local tooling or desktop apps, this version streams geometry and textures on-demand—loading only what’s visible—making it surprisingly responsive even on mid-tier laptops.

The technical ambition is matched by emotional resonance. One commenter who lived in SF for two decades described walking through their old backyard and tennis courts as ‘emotional’ and ‘weird as hell’—a testament to how precisely the simulation mirrors lived geography. Others imagine practical extensions: toggling street names or business labels, teleporting to addresses, or integrating Street View imagery for richer detail. Still more dream bigger—proposing live MMOs, RC car racing circuits, or even GTA-style modding pipelines that reconstruct fire hydrants, traffic lights, and trees from low-res source imagery.

What makes this especially notable is its accessibility. No app install, no VR headset, no sign-up—just a modern browser and curiosity. While it currently lacks semantic labels (e.g., ‘Golden Gate Bridge’) or dynamic elements (e.g., moving vehicles or weather), its foundation is robust and extensible. As one developer noted, it’s not just a demo—it’s a working prototype of what urban-scale, real-time 3D web mapping could become: open, interactive, and deeply personal.
