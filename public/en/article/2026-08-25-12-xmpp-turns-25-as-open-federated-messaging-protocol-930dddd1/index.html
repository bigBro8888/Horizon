XMPP turns 25 as open federated messaging protocol

News time: 2026-08-25T02:22:44.911698+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Twenty-five years after its inception, the Extensible Messaging and Presence Protocol (XMPP) — originally known as Jabber — stands not as a relic, but as a quietly resilient foundation for digital self-determination. A reflective essay by Daniel Gultsch marks this milestone by highlighting how XMPP’s core design principles — openness, federation, and decentralization — remain urgently relevant in an era dominated by walled-garden platforms and surveillance-driven business models.

Unlike centralized services like WhatsApp or iMessage, XMPP operates like email: anyone can run their own server, choose their client, and communicate across independent domains without relying on a single corporate entity. This architecture enables true user sovereignty — control over data, identity, and infrastructure. Though XMPP faded from mainstream consumer awareness after tech giants like Google Talk and Facebook Chat dropped support in the 2010s, it never disappeared. Instead, it evolved in niches where autonomy matters most: activist networks, privacy-conscious communities, self-hosted infrastructures, and even emerging AI agent systems.

Today, real-world deployments illustrate XMPP’s quiet vitality. jmp.chat bridges SMS and telephony into XMPP, letting users ditch carrier lock-in; Cheogram and Dino provide polished mobile and desktop clients; Movim and Fluux reimagine social interaction atop XMPP with modern web interfaces; and developers like pavo-etc use XMPP as a lightweight, standards-based layer for inter-agent communication on Raspberry Pi clusters. Meanwhile, mature, battle-tested servers like Prosody and ejabberd continue to power thousands of independent deployments worldwide.

The essay also acknowledges challenges — notably ecosystem fragmentation. As one commenter notes, Matrix’s emergence diverted significant funding and developer attention away from XMPP, despite overlapping goals. Yet rather than framing this as defeat, the reflection treats it as a reminder: open protocols thrive not through dominance, but through sustained stewardship, interoperability, and alignment with human values like privacy and choice. In celebrating 25 years, XMPP isn’t asking to be rediscovered — it’s inviting renewed participation in building infrastructure that serves people, not platforms.
