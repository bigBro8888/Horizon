BMVC 2026 authors seek clarity on IJCV special issue selection

News time: 2026-08-25T02:22:45.061673+00:00 · Source: reddit

A researcher posting on Reddit’s r/MachineLearning forum has raised a timely and practical question about the path from BMVC 2026 acceptance to inclusion in a forthcoming International Journal of Computer Vision (IJCV) special issue. The post asks how papers are selected for recommendation — whether it hinges primarily on numerical review scores, or whether Area Chairs and Program Chairs make a separate, qualitative judgment based on factors like oral presentation status, reviewer commentary, or perceived impact. With BMVC being a top-tier UK-based computer vision conference, and IJCV a premier archival journal with high selectivity and long-term visibility, this pipeline matters deeply to early-career researchers aiming for both conference exposure and journal credibility.

The query also touches on timing: do authors learn about potential IJCV recommendation at the time of BMVC notification, or only later — perhaps weeks or months after the conference? This uncertainty affects planning for revisions, resubmission deadlines, and even career milestones like tenure dossiers or grant applications. The poster explicitly invites input from those who have navigated this process before, underscoring that institutional knowledge is fragmented and not publicly documented in official BMVC or IJCV guidelines.

Official BMVC 2026 materials confirm that submissions are managed via OpenReview and that accepted papers must be “technically sound” and advance knowledge — with encouragement for novel, bold ideas, even if empirically limited. However, none of the published guidelines mention the IJCV special issue pathway, nor clarify whether recommendation is automatic for oral papers, discretionary, or tied to specific review criteria. Similarly, IJCV’s past special issues — such as those linked to GCPR or workshops co-located with ICCV — typically originate from curated selections by guest editors, not algorithmic score thresholds.

For authors, the stakes are real: IJCV publication carries greater weight in academic evaluation than conference proceedings alone, especially outside North America. Yet without transparency around selection logic or timelines, researchers face strategic ambiguity — potentially delaying journal-ready revisions or missing submission windows. As conferences increasingly serve as feeders to journals, clearer communication from organizing committees would strengthen trust and reduce procedural anxiety across the community.
