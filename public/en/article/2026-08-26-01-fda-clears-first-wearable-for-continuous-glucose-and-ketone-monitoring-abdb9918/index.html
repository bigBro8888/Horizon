FDA clears first wearable for continuous glucose and ketone monitoring

News time: 2026-08-26T02:38:28.018871+00:00 · Source: hackernews

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has authorized Abbott’s Libre Duo 10 Day system—the first wearable device capable of continuously and simultaneously tracking both interstitial glucose and ketone (beta-hydroxybutyrate, or BHB) levels. Unlike traditional fingerstick tests or standalone ketone meters, which offer only single-point snapshots, the Libre Duo provides real-time trends over 10 days using a single, minimally invasive sensor worn on the back of the upper arm. The device pairs with a smartphone app to display dynamic graphs, alerts for rising ketones, and customizable thresholds—giving users actionable insight into metabolic shifts before symptoms appear.

This authorization marks a clinical inflection point for people with diabetes, especially those at risk for diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), a life-threatening condition where high blood sugar and elevated ketones combine to acidify the blood. Until now, ketone monitoring required separate urine dipsticks or handheld blood meters—tools that couldn’t show directionality (e.g., whether ketones were climbing or plateauing) or integrate with glucose data. The Libre Duo closes that gap: by correlating glucose and ketone trajectories in one interface, it supports earlier intervention, better insulin dosing decisions, and improved safety during illness, fasting, or low-carb diets.

The sensor uses wired enzyme electrochemistry—similar to existing continuous glucose monitors—but adds BHB dehydrogenase chemistry to detect ketones in interstitial fluid. It is indicated for adults aged 18 and older and is not intended for diagnosis or disease management alone; rather, it serves as an adjunctive tool alongside standard care. Importantly, the device does not replace fingerstick calibration, nor does it claim noninvasive operation: like CGMs, it requires a tiny filament insertion under the skin. Users report minimal discomfort during application—describing it as a ‘snap’ rather than a needle prick.

While reimbursement pathways remain uncertain—and clinicians are still evaluating how best to interpret dual-parameter trends—the Libre Duo signals a broader shift toward multimodal metabolic sensing. As metabolic health gains attention beyond diabetes (e.g., in obesity, longevity, and athletic performance), integrated biosensors may become foundational infrastructure—not just for disease management, but for proactive physiological awareness.
