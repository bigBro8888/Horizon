Qwen3.8-Flash-Next previews Qwen4 with 125B-parameter multimodal MoE

News time: 2026-08-27T11:10:18.174224+00:00 · Source: rss

Qwen3.8-Flash-Next is a newly released open-weight large language model from Alibaba’s Tongyi Lab — and it’s not just another incremental update. Positioned explicitly as an architectural preview of the upcoming Qwen4, this model introduces a multimodal Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) design with a striking parameter profile: 125 billion total parameters, yet only 6 billion active per forward pass. That sparse activation enables high performance without the full computational cost of dense models at that scale.

The model is already being tested in real-world conditions. Developer Simon Willison ran it on an NVIDIA DGX Spark system using quantized GGUF versions provided by Unsloth — specifically the UD-IQ1_S (72.5 GB) and UD-Q2_K_XL (78.9 GB) variants. These aren’t just theoretical benchmarks; they’re practical deployments producing tangible outputs, including whimsical but technically impressive multimodal generations like a pelican riding a red bicycle through sunlit hills — rendered via text-to-image prompting within the model’s multimodal pipeline.

What makes this especially notable is its openness: unlike many frontier models kept behind closed APIs, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next ships with fully open weights and supports local inference via widely adopted tooling like llama.cpp and Unsloth’s optimized loaders. The quantization formats used — IQ1_S and Q2_K_XL — represent aggressive compression techniques that preserve usability on high-end consumer and enterprise hardware, trading minimal fidelity for dramatic memory and latency gains.

This release also signals a strategic shift in how Qwen is communicating its roadmap. Rather than waiting for Qwen4’s full launch, the team is sharing key architectural innovations early — inviting developers, researchers, and open-source contributors to explore, benchmark, and even co-shape the next generation. It’s a rare case of transparency meeting ambition: a preview that’s both technically substantive and practically deployable.
