Statistical ML researchers question where to publish amid LLM dominance at top conferences

News time: 2026-08-28T12:44:25.690324+00:00 · Source: reddit

A seasoned researcher in statistical and probabilistic machine learning has voiced growing unease about the shifting landscape of academic publishing in AI. In a candid Reddit post on r/MachineLearning, they describe how conferences once celebrated for methodological rigor—ICLR and NeurIPS—are now overwhelmingly populated by large language model (LLM) and agent-centric work. At this year’s ICLR, they observed that fewer than one in ten posters addressed classical statistical inference, Bayesian modeling, or probabilistic reasoning; instead, most showcased variations on prompting, fine-tuning, or evaluating LLMs on narrow benchmarks. NeurIPS workshops followed the same pattern, with nearly all focused on agentic behavior, planning, or tool use—leaving little room for foundational questions about uncertainty quantification, latent variable models, or asymptotic consistency.

This isn’t just about aesthetics or personal preference—it reflects a structural realignment in what counts as ‘high-impact’ ML research. The poster notes that respected figures like Arnaud Doucet, Aapo Hyvärinen, Christian Naesseth, and Stefano Ermon continue publishing in top-tier venues, but suggests their visibility may stem from historical reputation rather than current thematic fit. The author wonders aloud whether the so-called ‘top three’ conferences—NeurIPS, ICML, and ICLR—were ever truly designed for statistical ML, or whether their prestige simply attracted such work by default. As attention and funding pivot toward scalable, empirical, and application-driven paradigms, more theoretical or mathematically grounded subfields risk marginalization—not due to lack of quality, but due to misalignment with dominant narratives.

The researcher proposes a pragmatic recalibration: redirecting submissions to venues with deeper roots in statistics and uncertainty, specifically naming AISTATS and UAI. AISTATS explicitly bridges AI, statistics, and machine learning, emphasizing inference, modeling, and theoretical guarantees. UAI, short for ‘Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence,’ centers on probabilistic reasoning, causal modeling, and decision-making under uncertainty—core concerns for statistical ML practitioners. Both are peer-reviewed, highly selective, and long-standing, yet carry less mainstream visibility than NeurIPS or ICLR.

Ultimately, the post raises a quiet but urgent question: when prestige and relevance diverge, what does it mean for a subfield’s sustainability? It’s not a call to abandon top conferences—but a reminder that healthy scientific ecosystems need diverse venues, each serving distinct intellectual roles. For early-career researchers weighing where to invest time and effort, the message is clear: alignment with community values and methodological priorities matters as much as venue rank.
