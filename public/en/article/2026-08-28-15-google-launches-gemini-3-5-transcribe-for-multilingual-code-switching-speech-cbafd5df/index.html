Google launches Gemini 3.5-Transcribe for multilingual code-switching speech

News time: 2026-08-28T12:44:25.523640+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Google has introduced Gemini 3.5-Transcribe, its latest speech-to-text (STT) model designed specifically for real-world conversational complexity. Unlike generic ASR systems trained on clean, monolingual audio, this model targets scenarios where speakers fluidly mix languages—such as switching between German, Italian, and English mid-sentence—and embed domain-specific terminology, like technical jargon from enterprise meetings. It’s built on Gemini’s audio understanding architecture and offers features including utterance-level language detection, speaker diarization, word-level timestamps, and ‘Smart transcription’ that cleans up disfluencies like repetitions and false starts.

The model is already available via the Gemini API and integrated into the Gemini macOS app, with enterprise deployment support through the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform. Google emphasizes low-latency performance and multilingual robustness, positioning it as a step beyond traditional STT tools that struggle with code-switching—a known pain point in global teams, customer service, and multilingual education. Early adopters report practical utility in noisy, fast-paced settings, though not without caveats.

User feedback from Hacker News reveals nuanced, practitioner-led evaluation. One engineer tested over 20 STT models on real mixed-language meeting recordings and found only Voxtral Mini 3B (local) and ElevenLabs (paid API) comparable—praising their handling of rapid language shifts and industry terms. Another user noted that while Gemini 3.5-Transcribe excels at convenience for long-form dictation, it sometimes over-simplifies utterances, dropping semantically critical phrases like “I hesitated to check it” in favor of just “I should have verified”—a loss of speaker intent that matters in legal, medical, or accountability-sensitive contexts.

Technical clarifications also emerged: a commenter pointed out confusion around the phrase “function calling” in marketing materials, stressing that Gemini 3.5-Transcribe itself does not execute functions—it’s a pure STT model; any downstream task delegation happens in higher-level Gemini agents, not within transcription. Others benchmarked latency and noise resilience, citing Soniox STT v5 as currently superior in real-time translation applications. These discussions underscore that accuracy alone isn’t enough—latency, fidelity to speaker nuance, and integration clarity are equally vital for production use.
