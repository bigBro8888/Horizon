US designates Italian nonprofit Autistici/Inventati as global terrorist

News time: 2026-08-29T07:50:43.465635+00:00 · Source: hackernews

The U.S. Department of State has formally designated Autistici/Inventati — an Italian nonprofit internet hosting collective founded in 2003 — as a ‘Specially Designated Global Terrorist’ (SDGT). The move, announced in late August 2026, targets the group not for direct violence or operational command, but for allegedly providing infrastructure to entities the U.S. claims support terrorism — including the blogging platform noblogs.org, which hosts thousands of activist, feminist, anti-racist, and independent media sites across Europe and Latin America.

Autistici/Inventati has long operated as a privacy-first, volunteer-run infrastructure provider. It offers email, web hosting, XMPP chat, and Tor/I2P relay services — all built on free and open-source software, with no commercial revenue model. Its ethos centers on ‘socializing knowledge without founding power,’ and it explicitly refuses surveillance cooperation, data retention, or content takedowns beyond legally mandated court orders. Unlike corporate platforms, it does not monetize user data or enforce centralized moderation.

The designation stems from the U.S. government’s broader campaign against what it calls ‘far-left political terrorism,’ citing unspecified links between noblogs.org-hosted content and two newly sanctioned European groups advocating Palestinian rights. Notably, no evidence has been publicly released tying Autistici/Inventati to violent acts, planning, or financing. Instead, the Treasury Department’s Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) appears to treat technical hosting — even of lawful, nonviolent speech — as material support under an expansive interpretation of counterterrorism law.

This marks the first time a decentralized, noncommercial infrastructure provider has been labeled a terrorist entity solely for offering neutral technical services. Experts warn it sets a dangerous precedent: if running a mail server or Tor exit node can trigger SDGT status, then developers of Monero, I2P, Veilid, or Signal could face similar legal exposure. As one Hacker News commenter asked: ‘If a radical group sets up shop on I2P, are I2P users and devs now terrorists?’

Civil society groups, digital rights organizations, and cryptography researchers have condemned the move as a threat to the foundational architecture of internet freedom. Without legal clarity protecting infrastructure providers from guilt-by-association, open-source tools risk chilling development, funding withdrawal, and deplatforming by payment processors and cloud vendors — all before any judicial review. The case is now widely seen not just as an attack on one collective, but on the very idea that technical neutrality can coexist with national security policy.
