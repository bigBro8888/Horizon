A 100-year-old statistical method beats top time series anomaly detectors

News time: 2026-08-30T06:36:53.244198+00:00 · Source: reddit

In a striking critique posted to Reddit’s r/MachineLearning, renowned time series researcher Eamonn Keogh demonstrated that Statistical Process Control (SPC)—a foundational quality control technique developed in the early 20th century—achieves perfect or near-perfect performance on the widely adopted TSB-AD-M benchmark. This benchmark, curated by Paparrizos and colleagues and frequently used in top-tier venues like NeurIPS and SIGKDD, is intended to rigorously evaluate modern deep learning–based anomaly detection methods. Yet Keogh found that simple SPC rules—such as Shewhart charts monitoring deviations from mean and standard deviation—solve many benchmark instances trivially, especially in univariate datasets like ECG traces and those labeled 'TAO'.

The implication is not that SPC is universally superior, but that the benchmark itself lacks sufficient challenge. As Keogh puts it: if a century-old, parameter-light, interpretable method outperforms dozens of recent neural architectures—many requiring GPU training, hyperparameter tuning, and architectural innovation—then the evaluation protocol may be measuring overfitting to artificial simplicity rather than real-world robustness.

Keogh doesn’t dismiss all recent work outright. He explicitly states he makes no claims about the intrinsic value of individual algorithms proposed in these papers. Instead, his concern targets the evaluation ecosystem: the TSB-AD-M benchmark, while large and diverse in nominal scope, appears to contain many low-difficulty cases where anomalies are merely large, isolated outliers—not subtle regime shifts, concept drifts, or multivariate interdependencies. His slides ([b], [c]) show side-by-side comparisons where SPC achieves 100% F1 while leading models score below 80%—not due to algorithmic weakness, but because the task is too shallow.

To move forward, Keogh points to more realistic domains he’s begun curating: industrial fuel cell sensor streams, tuna migration telemetry, sled dog biometrics, and smart manufacturing process data. These reflect true operational challenges—nonstationarity, missing data, label scarcity, and causal confounding—that current benchmarks ignore. His broader call is for the TSAD community to treat benchmark design with the same scientific rigor as model development—prioritizing ecological validity over convenience and scale.
