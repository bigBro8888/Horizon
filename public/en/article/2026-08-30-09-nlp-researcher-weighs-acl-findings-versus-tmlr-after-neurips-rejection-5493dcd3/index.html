NLP researcher weighs ACL Findings versus TMLR after NeurIPS rejection

News time: 2026-08-30T06:36:53.244246+00:00 · Source: reddit

A machine learning researcher recently posted on Reddit’s r/MachineLearning asking for community advice: having received low scores (5/2/2) at NeurIPS—a strong signal of likely rejection—they’re now deciding between submitting to ACL Findings or Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR). The post reflects a growing dilemma among NLP and ML researchers navigating the increasingly crowded academic publishing landscape, where venue choice carries real weight for visibility, perceived rigor, and career advancement.

ACL Findings is the official companion journal of the Association for Computational Linguistics, launched in 2021 to publish solid, peer-reviewed work that doesn’t meet the bar for the main ACL conference but still contributes meaningfully to the field. It operates via the ACL Rolling Review (ARR) system, which standardizes submission, review, and decision timelines across ACL venues. In contrast, TMLR is an open-access, double-blind, editor-invited journal founded in 2022 by prominent ML researchers—including Yann LeCun and Bernhard Schölkopf—with a mission to prioritize scientific soundness over novelty or trendiness. Papers undergo multiple rounds of revision and are publicly reviewed, with decisions made by editors rather than program committees.

The researcher notes they’re leaning toward ACL Findings because their paper is more aligned with NLP than general ML—and because NAACL Findings (a sibling venue) feels like a more natural fit than TMLR. But they’re genuinely uncertain: Does TMLR’s rigorous, transparent process outweigh ACL Findings’ stronger disciplinary recognition in NLP hiring and promotion? Are citations, tenure committee perceptions, or reviewer feedback quality more decisive?

This question taps into deeper tensions in modern AI research: the trade-off between speed and scrutiny, between field-specific prestige and cross-disciplinary credibility, and between traditional conference culture and newer journal-based models. While neither venue is considered ‘less serious’ than top conferences, their roles differ—ACL Findings serves as a high-quality outlet within the NLP ecosystem, whereas TMLR positions itself as a foundational alternative to conference-centric publishing across all of ML. For early-career researchers especially, such decisions can shape CVs, grant applications, and even job interviews.
