GitHub repo claims to list 7,000 NeurIPS 2026 accepted papers

News time: 2026-08-31T07:01:00.272309+00:00 · Source: reddit

A GitHub repository titled 'NIPS26-' surfaced on Reddit this week, allegedly containing around 7,000 anonymized papers accepted to NeurIPS 2026 — one of the most prestigious machine learning conferences worldwide. The post, shared by a user identifying as /u/Feuilius, links to an HTML file hosted on GitHub and urges the community to verify its authenticity. Given that NeurIPS 2026’s official acceptance notifications are not scheduled until late October 2025 (per standard timelines), the appearance of such a large-scale list so early has raised immediate red flags.

The NeurIPS peer-review process is double-blind and highly structured: submissions close in May, reviews occur through summer, area chairs deliberate in September, and final decisions are communicated to authors in late October. Any leak before that date would violate core principles of fairness, confidentiality, and anonymity — all central to maintaining trust in academic evaluation. Moreover, the repository’s naming convention ('NIPS26-') uses outdated branding (NeurIPS officially dropped 'NIPS' in 2018), which several commenters flagged as suspicious.

Initial community inspection revealed inconsistencies beyond the naming: some paper IDs mismatch official NeurIPS OpenReview ID formats; anonymization appears uneven — with certain sections retaining author-identifying metadata or institutional clues; and no accompanying metadata (e.g., reviewer scores, AC comments, or decision rationale) is present, unlike real acceptance emails or official dashboards. Crucially, neither the NeurIPS 2026 Program Committee nor the conference’s official channels have acknowledged, confirmed, or commented on the repository.

While the list includes plausible paper titles and technical phrasing consistent with ML research trends, its lack of verifiable provenance makes it impossible to treat as authoritative. For authors, premature exposure could risk scooping, citation bias, or unintended disclosure of unpublished work. For reviewers and area chairs, it threatens the integrity of their confidential deliberations. The incident underscores how fragile academic trust can be — especially when infrastructure like GitHub is used outside official review platforms. Until verified, the safest stance remains cautious skepticism.
