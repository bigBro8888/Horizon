Sliding-window attention with sinks outperforms linear attention on long-context tasks

News time: 2026-09-01T06:13:46.031244+00:00 · Source: reddit

A new arXiv preprint challenges a major trend in efficient LLM research: the widespread pursuit of linear attention methods through costly post-training. The paper, titled Sliding-Window Attention with Sinks Beats Linear Attention, shows that a remarkably simple alternative—sliding-window attention (SWA) augmented with sink tokens—consistently matches or exceeds the performance of state-of-the-art linear attention models on demanding long-context reasoning benchmarks. Crucially, it does so without any post-training, while also running faster and using significantly less memory.

The authors—Alexia Jolicoeur-Martineau, Rhea Sanjay Sukthanker, Pashmina Cameron, and Emy Gervais—benchmark across multiple open LLMs and focus on two rigorous evaluation suites: Needle-in-a-Haystack and BABILong. Both are designed to test whether models can reliably retrieve and reason over sparse, critical facts buried in thousands of tokens of irrelevant context. On these tasks, SWA with sinks achieves 2 to 10 times higher accuracy than leading linear attention variants—including those fine-tuned with substantial compute budgets.

The paper’s core critique is methodological: much of the recent linear attention literature has compared new methods against weak or outdated baselines—not against well-tuned, practical alternatives like SWA. As the authors put it, "This line of research has not been properly compared to simpler baselines." Their results suggest that the engineering effort poured into post-training linear attention may have been misdirected. SWA with sinks requires no architectural overhaul, no specialized training pipelines, and integrates seamlessly into existing inference frameworks.

Beyond raw accuracy, the practical advantages are compelling. Linear attention models often demand either full retraining from scratch or expensive post-training steps like distillation or adapter tuning—costly in both time and GPU hours. In contrast, SWA with sinks works out of the box with standard pretrained weights. It reduces attention computation from quadratic O(n²) to near-linear O(n × w), where w is the fixed window size—making it highly scalable for contexts up to hundreds of thousands of tokens. The authors conclude with a direct recommendation: "we strongly recommend switching to SWA instead of post-training linear models."

This work doesn’t dismiss linear attention entirely—it acknowledges its theoretical appeal—but reframes the priority: simplicity, speed, and empirical robustness should guide efficiency choices, not just asymptotic complexity. For developers building long-context applications—from legal document analysis to scientific literature review—SWA with sinks offers a ready-to-deploy, high-performing, low-overhead alternative that resets the benchmark for what ‘efficient’ really means.
