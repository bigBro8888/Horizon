Anthropic releases Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 with better writing and science reasoning

News time: 2026-09-02T05:47:15.247985+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Anthropic has launched two major new model versions: Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1. Both are built on the same underlying architecture but differ in access and safety controls — Fable 5.1 is publicly available for general use, while Mythos 5.1 remains invitation-only through trusted programs like Project Glasswing, where it supports high-stakes work in cybersecurity and life sciences. According to Anthropic, Fable 5.1 sets a new bar for long-horizon tasks — from managing Slack requests via Claude Tag (beta) to operating browsers autonomously or running unattended as a managed agent.

The upgrade brings tangible improvements across several dimensions. Users report notably more natural, less formulaic writing — a shift that insiders describe as moving away from ‘stereotypical Claude’ phrasing toward responsive, stylistically flexible prose. Scientific reasoning capabilities have also strengthened significantly, especially on complex, multi-step problems. Benchmark data shows measurable gains over Fable 5, particularly in knowledge-intensive and coding-heavy workflows. Meanwhile, instruction fidelity — how reliably the model follows nuanced, layered user directions — has improved, making it more dependable for real-world automation.

A key practical win is cost efficiency: cache-read pricing dropped from $1 per million tokens to $0.25 per million, cutting Fable 5.1’s cache costs to half those of Claude Opus. This change lowers operational overhead for developers building stateful, memory-augmented agents. Notably, Simon Willison’s ‘pelican’ visualizations — which render internal reasoning traces as SVG diagrams — now capture richer, summarized thought processes in Fable 5.1, including a newly stable ‘xhigh’ effort mode and a fully functional ‘max’ mode after tooling fixes.

Despite enthusiasm, community discussion reveals healthy skepticism. Some users question whether benchmark gains hold up outside narrowly tuned tests — one commenter notes that removing Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 results makes improvements hard to detect. Others critique the removal of raw thought traces in default outputs, calling it a step back for prompt debugging. Still, the consensus leans positive: Fable 5.1 feels more capable and more pleasant to use, while Mythos 5.1 quietly expands what’s possible in high-trust domains — without changing the fundamental model equation.
