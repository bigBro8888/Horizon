Latent reasoning architectures challenge chain-of-thought dominance

News time: 2026-09-02T05:47:15.455873+00:00 · Source: reddit

A growing body of research is shifting focus from verbalized chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning to latent reasoning—where models manipulate continuous hidden states instead of generating intermediate tokens. This conceptual pivot, outlined in a high-scoring r/MachineLearning post titled 'Latent Reasoning Landscape in 2026', argues that CoT is not reasoning itself but a flawed proxy: LLMs often produce logically coherent yet factually wrong chains—or correct answers via internally inconsistent steps (Kambhampati, 2025). The post synthesizes five emerging families of latent reasoning systems, each rethinking where and how computation unfolds during problem solving.

One prominent approach is Coconut (Hao et al., 2024), which feeds a model’s final hidden state back as the next input embedding—enabling autoregressive reasoning entirely within the continuous latent space. Unlike token-level CoT, Coconut avoids linguistic constraints and supports parallel expansion of multiple search frontiers in a single state vector. Complementary work like Soft Thinking (Zhang et al., 2025) operates in learned concept spaces, while Abstract-CoT (Ramji et al., 2026) uses compact, non-linguistic discrete tokens—occupying a middle ground between language and continuity.

Recurrent architectures take a different tack: recurrent-depth LMs (Geiping et al., 2025) and looped Transformers (Saunshi et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2026) reuse shared blocks over latent states, primarily optimizing for parameter efficiency—not necessarily interpretability or AGI alignment. In contrast, HRM (Wang et al., 2025) and TRM (Jolicoeur-Martineau, 2025) are task-trained recursive solvers that refine both latent representations and candidate answers across iterations. Their evaluation relies on transductive pipelines: unseen puzzles trigger an internal backward pass before inference begins.

BDH-CQ (Engdahl et al., 2026), built atop the Dragon hatchling architecture (Kosowski et al., 2025), stands out as an in-context recurrent latent solver. It writes demonstration examples directly into a recurrent memory at inference time, then solves new inputs through iterative computation in a dedicated continuous latent space—not the token stream. Early results show it surpasses prior cost–accuracy trade-offs on ARC-AGI-1 and maintains transformer-like scaling up to 600B parameters without losing latent reasoning behavior. The post closes with a pressing question: if latent reasoning proves more efficient and robust, what becomes of human-readable CoT traces—the foundation of today’s safety audits, debugging tools, and benchmark evaluations?
