TontaubeV1 launches as open-weight character-level TTS for expressive long-form speech

News time: 2026-09-02T05:47:15.456185+00:00 · Source: reddit

TontaubeV1, a newly released open-weight text-to-speech model, marks a deliberate departure from mainstream TTS design by embracing character-level tokenization and a novel chunked position scheme—both aimed at improving expressiveness, stability, and efficiency in long-form narration. Developed by two brothers and publicly shared on Reddit, the 2.9-billion-parameter model targets English and German speakers and supports zero-shot voice cloning from as little as one minute of reference audio. Unlike most LLM-based TTS systems that inherit subword tokenizers (e.g., BPE) from their language model backbones, TontaubeV1 forces its Qwen3-1.7B-derived semantic model to treat input text as sequences of individual characters—even though it retains the original tokenizer’s vocabulary and weights.

This choice wasn’t arbitrary: the team found that character-level representation reduced out-of-distribution errors during inference, especially with punctuation, code-switching, or rare word forms. Since TTS doesn’t require deep reasoning over long contexts—and since they use chunking—the added sequence length isn’t prohibitive. More importantly, speech phonetics align more naturally with character and syllable units than with variable-length BPE tokens, simplifying the internal mapping from text to acoustic features.

The second innovation lies in how TontaubeV1 handles long inputs. Instead of feeding text and audio tokens into a flat transformer sequence with standard positional IDs, the model uses a dual-position scheme: physical positions govern attention visibility, while logical positions encode temporal alignment across modalities. Text advances one position per character; audio advances at 12.5 frames per second—close enough to speech rhythm that minor realignments suffice at chunk boundaries. Each chunk includes overlapping context (previous text/audio, current text, and a short lookahead), and higher acoustic codebook models process chunks independently—enabling bounded memory usage without sacrificing local coherence.

Underpinning all this is DualCodec, a recently introduced multi-codebook discrete audio codec trained on 100K hours of multilingual speech. With eight codebook layers and low frame rates (12.5 Hz for semantic and 25 Hz for acoustic modeling), DualCodec delivers high-fidelity reconstruction at low bitrates—outperforming SpeechTokenizer and Mimi in both audio quality and TTS integration. TontaubeV1 was trained on ~200K hours of audio across seven languages, though evaluation focused on English and German. Its open weights, local inference capability, and support for expressive, zero-shot cloning make it especially appealing for developers prioritizing privacy, customization, and real-time responsiveness.
