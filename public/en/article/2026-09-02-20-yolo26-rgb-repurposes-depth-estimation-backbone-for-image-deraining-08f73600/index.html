YOLO26-RGB repurposes depth-estimation backbone for image deraining

News time: 2026-09-02T05:47:15.455555+00:00 · Source: reddit

A new model called YOLO26-RGB demonstrates that a backbone originally trained for monocular depth estimation can be effectively reused for image deraining — a low-level vision task requiring pixel-accurate restoration. Rather than designing a new architecture from scratch, the author retained the full CSPDarknet backbone and PAN-FPN neck from YOLO26’s depth-estimation variant, keeping all weights intact. What changed was the head: the original 1-channel depth predictor was replaced with a custom RGBHead — a new decoder designed specifically for rain removal, featuring full-resolution reconstruction, residual output (predicting rain-correction residuals), skip connections from early backbone layers, and LayerNorm in its conv blocks.

The experiment was carefully controlled: two versions of the same nano-scale architecture (5.25M parameters) were trained identically for 100 epochs — one initialized with YOLO26-depth weights, the other with random initialization. Results showed consistent gains: the depth-initialized model achieved +0.48 dB higher average PSNR and +0.006 higher SSIM across all 10 ClearView test sets — not just on average, but on every single set. The gap emerged early (by epoch 20) and persisted, ruling out simple convergence-speed differences. This suggests the depth-trained representation encodes spatial or structural priors beneficial for restoration — even though depth and deraining tasks differ in output modality and supervision signal.

YOLO26-RGB comes in two sizes — nano (5.25M) and small (12.13M) — and is benchmarked against established models like Restormer, NAFNet, and UNet variants using ClearView’s standardized protocol (mixed synthetic/real rain, Charbonnier loss, 10-test-set evaluation). While its absolute PSNR (30.83–30.95) lags behind top-performing large models (e.g., Restormer at 35.10), it achieves competitive accuracy for its parameter count — notably outperforming ResNet18-UNet (14.4M) and matching some lightweight baselines. Crucially, the work isn’t primarily about deraining performance; it’s a targeted probe into cross-task transferability among dense regression problems.

The choice of architecture components reflects deliberate design trade-offs: CSPDarknet provides strong hierarchical feature extraction, while PAN-FPN enables robust multi-scale fusion — both widely used in detection and now shown to generalize beyond it. By preserving BatchNorm in the backbone (for compatibility with YOLO26’s pretrained checkpoints) and introducing LayerNorm only in the new head, the model remains deployable via TensorRT without retraining the entire stack. This pragmatic reuse — swapping heads while freezing backbones and necks — offers a template for rapid adaptation of large vision models to new low-level tasks without full retraining.
