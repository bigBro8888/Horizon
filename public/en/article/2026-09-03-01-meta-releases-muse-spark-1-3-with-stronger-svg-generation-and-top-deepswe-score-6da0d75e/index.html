Meta releases Muse Spark 1.3 with stronger SVG generation and top DeepSWE score

News time: 2026-09-03T05:45:02.384970+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Meta has launched Muse Spark 1.3, the latest iteration of its open, lightweight multimodal large language model — designed specifically for developers who need capable yet cost-efficient AI for structured output tasks. Unlike frontier models optimized for raw reasoning power, Muse Spark focuses on practical multimodal reasoning: it natively handles text, images, and tool calls, supports visual chain-of-thought, and excels at generating editable, precise outputs like Scalable Vector Graphics (SVG). The update isn’t just incremental: benchmark results show Muse Spark 1.3 achieves a DeepSWE score of 75.4 — the highest recorded to date, surpassing Google’s Gemini 3.8 Flash, which briefly held the top spot.

What makes this release especially notable is how tightly performance gains align with real-world developer needs. In Hacker News discussions, users ran side-by-side comparisons — prompting both v1.2 and v1.3 to generate an SVG of ‘a pelican riding a bicycle’. The v1.3 output featured cleaner geometry, more accurate proportions (e.g., bicycle frame and wing articulation), and even subtle stylistic improvements like a properly rendered pelican hat — all without animation unless explicitly requested. That fidelity matters: SVGs generated by Muse Spark are not just images but code — fully editable, scalable, and embeddable in web interfaces or design tools.

Pricing reinforces its developer-first positioning: $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.20 per million output tokens, with zero cost for cached inputs. Crucially, Meta now transparently links pricing to data usage — stating outright that inputs may be used for training, and assigning explicit monetary value to that contribution. This contrasts sharply with opaque opt-out policies common elsewhere. Early adopters report using Spark 1.2 for internal tooling and prototyping, praising its predictability and lack of overreach — ‘it knew its weaknesses and didn’t impose opinions,’ one developer noted.

The broader significance lies in Meta’s dual-track strategy: while Llama remains its flagship open foundation model, Muse Spark carves out a distinct niche — small, fast, multimodal, and purpose-built for structured generation. As benchmarks like DeepSWE gain traction as proxies for real-world multimodal reliability, Muse Spark 1.3 signals a shift toward models evaluated not just on fluency or logic puzzles, but on their ability to produce production-ready artifacts. For teams building UI generators, documentation tools, or low-latency multimodal agents, this isn’t just an upgrade — it’s a new viable default.
