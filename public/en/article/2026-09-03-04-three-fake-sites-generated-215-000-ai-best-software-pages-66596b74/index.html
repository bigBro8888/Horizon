Three fake sites generated 215,000 AI 'best software' pages

News time: 2026-09-03T05:45:02.385057+00:00 · Source: hackernews

An investigative report by Trellner has uncovered a large-scale web spam operation that created over 215,000 nearly identical 'best software' pages across three fabricated websites — all designed to look like legitimate tech review hubs. These pages, filled with generic rankings and AI-generated prose, were not written by humans but systematically produced using automated tools. Crucially, Perplexity — an AI-powered search engine known for citing sources — repeatedly surfaced and cited content from these domains in its answers, treating them as authoritative references.

The investigation reveals how easily LLM-based systems can be misled by volume and surface-level credibility. The three sites shared identical templates, reused stock images, hosted no real editorial teams, and lacked contact information or About pages — yet their content ranked highly in Perplexity’s retrieval pipeline. This isn’t just about low-quality results: it demonstrates how AI tools trained or fine-tuned on web-scraped data may absorb and amplify synthetic, self-reinforcing narratives — especially when those narratives are optimized for search visibility and LLM ingestion.

Beyond Perplexity, the report connects this case to broader systemic risks: training data contamination, where AI models learn from other AI outputs; source blindness, where models fail to assess authorship, motive, or verifiability; and hallucination amplification, where false claims gain legitimacy through repetition across synthetic sources. Researchers observed geographic hallucinations (e.g., inventing non-existent town squares), preference for AI-written text over human-refactored code, and rapid degradation in answer quality as speed optimizations overrode source vetting.

This isn’t isolated spam — it’s infrastructure-level manipulation. The sites didn’t target users directly; they targeted the AI supply chain. By flooding the web with high-volume, SEO-optimized, LLM-friendly content, they gamed the very mechanisms that power modern AI search and recommendation systems. As one commenter noted, the problem isn’t just LLMs training on AI output — it’s that they’re increasingly trained on AI output that was itself trained on earlier AI output, forming recursive loops of diminishing fidelity.
