Deepity C++ library matches backprop speed and accuracy on MNIST with predictive coding

News time: 2026-09-03T05:45:03.041588+00:00 · Source: reddit

A new open-source C++ library called Deepity demonstrates that biologically inspired Predictive Coding Networks (PCNs) can match—and even slightly outperform—standard backpropagation in both speed and accuracy on the MNIST digit classification task. Developed over one month by an independent researcher, Deepity implements an accelerated variant of PCNs known as Direct Kolen–Pollack Predictive Coding Networks (DKP-PCNs). By combining learnable feedback connections from output to hidden layers with algorithmic caching during the inference settling phase, the library avoids redundant forward projections—dramatically cutting runtime without sacrificing performance.

On a standard CPU, Deepity trained a DKP-PCN for 50 epochs on MNIST in just 59.5 seconds and achieved 97.73% test accuracy. For comparison, a PyTorch baseline using conventional feedforward backpropagation reached 98.27% accuracy—but took approximately 70 seconds. This narrow gap in accuracy, coupled with a clear win in training time, challenges the long-held assumption that local, biologically plausible learning rules must be inherently slower or less effective than global error backpropagation.

The significance goes beyond benchmark numbers. Predictive coding is grounded in neuroscience: it models how cortical areas continuously minimize prediction errors via top-down and bottom-up signals—a mechanism thought to underlie perception, learning, and attention in the brain. Unlike backprop, which requires non-local weight updates and precise symmetric connectivity, PCNs rely only on local synaptic plasticity and bidirectional communication—making them promising candidates for neuromorphic hardware and continual learning, where catastrophic forgetting plagues backprop-based systems.

The developer plans next to port Deepity’s kernels to CUDA, enabling larger-scale experiments and testing in continual learning settings—scenarios where backprop consistently struggles due to its reliance on full-dataset retraining. The project is already drawing technical interest: early discussion on Reddit includes nuanced questions about settling dynamics, credit assignment locality, and scalability—signs that the ML community recognizes Deepity not as a curiosity, but as a credible step toward more brain-like, efficient, and adaptive machine learning.
