CABiNet outperforms YOLO26-sem on UAVid with better accuracy and lower latency

News time: 2026-09-03T05:45:03.042078+00:00 · Source: reddit

A direct, reproducible benchmark compares CABiNet — a 2021 real-time semantic segmentation model designed specifically for aerial imagery — against YOLO26-sem, a 2026 multi-task vision model adapted for semantic segmentation on the UAVid dataset. The comparison, conducted by CABiNet’s original first author, focuses on three practical axes: accuracy (mIoU), computational cost (FLOPs and parameters), and real-world GPU latency on an RTX 4070 SUPER. Though the author discloses their affiliation with CABiNet, the experimental setup is rigorously controlled: both models use identical UAVid preprocessing, class weighting (ENet inverse-log, cls_pw=0.5), EMA-weighted evaluation, and single-scale inference without test-time augmentation.

Key results show CABiNet-L achieves 67.14% mIoU at just 4.44 ms latency and 54.8 GFLOPs — outperforming even the largest YOLO26 variant (YOLO26x-sem, 64.41% mIoU, 13.09 ms, 430.9 GFLOPs). At near-identical compute levels (~44 GFLOPs), CABiNet-S (65.25% mIoU) beats YOLO26s (61.69% mIoU) by +3.6 mIoU while maintaining comparable speed (3.09 ms vs. 2.52 ms). This suggests CABiNet’s dual-branch design — combining a high-res spatial branch with a lightweight context branch over MobileNetV3 — remains highly effective for aerial scenes, despite being five years older.

However, the advantage is not universal. On other aerial benchmarks like VDD and AeroScapes, YOLO26 variants surpass CABiNet-Large, indicating dataset-specific strengths. The analysis also highlights an important hardware nuance: MobileNetV3’s depthwise convolutions are FLOP-efficient but less latency-efficient on modern GPUs — explaining why CABiNet-S and YOLO26s have nearly identical FLOPs yet differ slightly in measured latency. The full code, configs, and numbers are publicly available in updated repositories using PyTorch 2.x, Hydra, AMP, and EMA.

This work doesn’t claim one model is ‘better’ overall. Instead, it offers a grounded, apples-to-oranges-but-still-meaningful comparison that reflects real engineering trade-offs: purpose-built architectures can still dominate in their niche, even against newer general-purpose models — especially when latency, accuracy, and deployment constraints align tightly.
