Proposing JEPA world models in physics sims to ground LLMs

News time: 2026-09-04T05:50:52.309825+00:00 · Source: reddit

Large language models excel at describing physical phenomena — they can recite Newton’s laws, explain why apples fall, or simulate dialogue about orbital mechanics. But they don’t understand physics the way humans or embodied agents do. They lack grounded intuition: no internal model of object permanence, momentum, or causal force propagation. This gap is more than academic — it limits reasoning, planning, and safe interaction with the real world. The Reddit post frames this as a modern version of the ‘Mary’s Room’ thought experiment: an agent who knows all propositional facts about color but has never seen red. LLMs are Mary — fluent in physics vocabulary, yet blind to its embodied reality.

The proposal offers a concrete path forward: train a Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) inside a controlled physics simulation — such as MuJoCo or a minimal 2D rigid-body environment. Unlike video-prediction models that forecast pixels or tokens, this JEPA predicts abstract future state embeddings, not raw observations. Because prediction error directly reflects physical inconsistency — e.g., violating conservation of energy — the model is forced to discover invariant structure: trajectories, collisions, inertia. The resulting latent space isn’t about textures or appearances; it encodes principles.

Once trained, those representations are frozen and integrated into an LLM’s reasoning pipeline — not as extra tokens, but as a conditioning signal, possibly via cross-attention or embedding-space fusion. The LLM retains its linguistic fluency while gaining access to a fast, differentiable ‘physics engine’ — one it didn’t learn from text, but from simulated causality. This could dramatically accelerate downstream learning: instead of rederiving gravity from statistical co-occurrence, the model starts with representations where ‘falling’ is already embedded as a dynamical trajectory.

While related ideas exist — V-JEPA for video, DreamerV3 for RL, or recent work giving LLMs runtime access to simulators — none cleanly combine JEPA’s self-supervised, representation-level prediction with explicit grounding in physics priors before language modeling. The post ends with three sharp engineering questions: Is this already done? What’s the best interface between JEPA embeddings and LLMs? And crucially — will abstractions learned in simulation survive the jump to real-world complexity? These aren’t just implementation details; they cut to the heart of whether grounded world models can scale.
