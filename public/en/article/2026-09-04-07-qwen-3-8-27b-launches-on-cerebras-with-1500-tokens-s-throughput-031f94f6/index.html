Qwen 3.8 27B launches on Cerebras with 1500 tokens/s throughput

News time: 2026-09-04T05:50:51.986038+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Cerebras has officially added Qwen 3.8 27B — the dense 27-billion-parameter variant of Alibaba’s latest open-weight LLM — to its managed inference platform. According to Cerebras’ documentation, the model delivers a peak output speed of 1500 tokens per second, powered by the company’s wafer-scale WSE-3 chip. This places it among the fastest publicly available inference options for dense models of its class, outpacing many GPU-based providers that typically deliver under 200 tokens/s for comparable models.

However, real-world usage reveals sharp tradeoffs. Users report hitting strict token-per-minute (TPM) caps almost immediately: one developer burned $1.10 in just 90 seconds while hitting a 450,000 TPM limit — and cached tokens count toward that cap. Another noted a hard 150,000 TPM ceiling on the public endpoint, rendering it impractical for sustained coding or debugging tasks. Worse, billing access appears inconsistently available: some accounts land in a ‘limbo’ state where self-serve payment setup is disabled without clear escalation paths.

Cost-performance comparisons further undermine the headline speed. In identical coding benchmarks, Qwen 3.8 27B on Cerebras stalled mid-task while DeepSeek-V4-Flash completed the same workload in 172 seconds for just $0.024 — over 45× cheaper. Meanwhile, local alternatives like ninfer on an RTX 5090 achieve ~200 tokens/s sequentially and over 400 tokens/s concurrently — sufficient for most development use cases — with full control and zero rate limits.

The community response reflects deep technical engagement: a 155-comment thread includes detailed benchmark logs, side-by-side pricing tables, infrastructure pain points, and even suggestions like listing the model on OpenRouter to improve accessibility. While Cerebras’ hardware promise remains compelling, the current implementation highlights how raw throughput alone doesn’t guarantee usability — especially when rate policies, billing opacity, and cost efficiency are misaligned with developer workflows.
