AI tools now generate functional PCB prototypes with minor errors

News time: 2026-09-05T05:42:58.807979+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Can AI truly design printed circuit boards yet? A growing number of hardware engineers and hobbyists say yes — but with important caveats. Recent hands-on experiments using tools like Fable, Claude Opus (via KiCAD MCP Server), and open-source automation frameworks show that large language models can now produce functional PCB layouts: boards that pass DRC checks, get manufactured without rejection, and — critically — power up and operate as intended after assembly.

One practitioner with 15 years of experience designed an LED earring using Fable — a compact wearable with an RP2350 microcontroller, IMU, and 45 addressable LEDs. The AI-generated layout contained two small but fixable issues: missing through-hole pads on the coin-cell battery holder and an undersized thermal pad. These were resolved by swapping to a surface-mount battery holder and minor manual edits — no redesign needed. Another user tasked Claude Opus 4.8 with designing a VGA monochrome video generator using only 74-series logic chips and GAL programmable logic devices. Though routing was done manually, the AI produced correct schematics and GAL code; the final board worked after one blue-wire fix.

The KiCAD MCP Server — an open-source Model Context Protocol bridge — is emerging as a key enabler. It lets LLMs like Claude interact directly with KiCAD’s internal project state, enabling iterative, context-aware design steps: generating footprints, placing components, checking netlists, and even suggesting fixes. Users report generating DRC-clean flex PCBs for guitar pickguard art projects and experimental NeXTBus development boards — all without traditional netlist inputs.

Still, experts caution against over-optimism. As one commenter noted, electronics design remains deeply empirical: even top-tier SPICE simulations and datasheets often omit real-world parasitics or errata. An AI may accelerate time-to-first-prototype, but it cannot eliminate physical validation. The consensus isn’t that AI replaces PCB designers — rather, it shifts their role toward higher-level architecture, verification, and rapid iteration. For now, AI is best seen as a collaborative partner: fast at drafting, precise at rule-checking, but still reliant on human judgment for reliability-critical decisions.
