Mullvad shuts down its public encrypted DNS to fund Quad9

News time: 2026-09-05T05:42:58.807995+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Mullvad, the privacy-first VPN provider known for its strict no-logs policy and transparent operations, has announced it will discontinue its public encrypted DNS service — including its DoH (DNS over HTTPS) and DoT (DNS over TLS) endpoints — effective immediately. In a blog post published on May 2024, Mullvad explained that rather than maintaining its own public DNS infrastructure, it will now financially sponsor Quad9, a nonprofit DNS provider focused on security and privacy.

This decision reflects a strategic pivot toward specialization and resource efficiency. Mullvad emphasized that running a high-reliability, globally distributed, privacy-respecting public DNS resolver requires deep expertise in threat intelligence, real-time malware domain blocking, operational security, and legal jurisdiction management — areas where Quad9 has invested over a decade. By redirecting engineering and financial resources away from duplicating that work, Mullvad says it can strengthen its core VPN offering while still advancing shared privacy goals.

Quad9’s service blocks malicious domains using continuously updated threat feeds — independently verified to stop over 97% of tested phishing and malware domains — and offers encrypted DNS resolution with no logging of user queries. It operates under Swiss and U.S. nonprofit governance, with transparency reports and open collaboration with cybersecurity researchers. Mullvad’s sponsorship does not grant it operational control; instead, it supports Quad9’s infrastructure and mission as a donor among other institutional backers.

The move has sparked thoughtful discussion in privacy communities. Some users applaud the pragmatic consolidation of effort, calling it a sign of ecosystem maturity. Others raise valid concerns about centralization: if many privacy tools converge on a single DNS provider, does that make it a more attractive target for surveillance or coercion? A few commenters recommend self-hosted alternatives like Unbound — a lightweight, open-source recursive resolver that can be configured with ad/malware blocklists and routed through Mullvad VPN or Tor for added anonymity. Still, most agree that Mullvad’s choice of Quad9 — given its nonprofit status, jurisdictional resilience, and technical rigor — is among the most defensible options available today.
