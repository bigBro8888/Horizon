Statichost.eu launches GDPR-compliant static hosting from EU infrastructure

News time: 2026-09-05T05:42:58.807936+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Statichost.eu has emerged as a new European alternative for developers seeking privacy-first, GDPR-compliant static site hosting. Unlike global platforms such as Netlify or Vercel, Statichost emphasizes full European ownership — not just server locations — with infrastructure, operations, and data processing all based within the EU. The service is designed for simplicity: it supports Git-based deployments, custom domains with free SSL, instant rollbacks, and upcoming features like branch previews and a GDPR-aligned CDN. Its core promise is zero collection or processing of visitor personal data — a direct response to growing concerns about third-party tracking, bot traffic abuse, and jurisdictional risks tied to US-based cloud providers.

The platform’s timing aligns with real-world friction among small-scale developers. One user switched after Netlify raised prices and struggled with unfiltered bot traffic — especially problematic for low-traffic sites where build minutes and bandwidth are wasted on crawlers. Another adopted Statichost for a family website (touchofglass.eu), praising its generous free tier (10 GB/month) and reliability, though noting the Git-first workflow isn’t ideal for non-developers. That limitation highlights a trade-off: while Git integration enables automation and version control, it adds friction for users who prefer traditional tools like rsync or SFTP.

Design and UX feedback from early adopters is mixed. A developer criticized the website’s mobile menu and inconsistent spacing, calling it “cheap and unconsidered” — a reminder that trust in infrastructure services extends beyond compliance to perceived professionalism. Others pointed to complementary tools: Codefloe, an EU-hosted Git forge, offers seamless integration with Statichost and supports private repositories at no cost — filling a gap for teams wanting fully EU-resident toolchains. Meanwhile, long-time users of OVH’s free static hosting noted they’re happy but welcome more competition, underscoring demand for choice in ethical web infrastructure.

What sets Statichost apart isn’t raw performance or feature depth, but intentionality: every design decision appears rooted in regulatory accountability and data sovereignty. It avoids global CDNs that route traffic through non-EU jurisdictions, discourages external font loading from US servers, and makes no assumptions about analytics or tracking. For EU-based creators, small businesses, or public institutions bound by strict privacy rules, this isn’t just convenience — it’s operational necessity.
