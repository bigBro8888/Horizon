New guide visualizes how Rust's dyn Trait uses vtables and fat pointers

News time: 2026-09-06T05:53:56.050480+00:00 · Source: hackernews

A newly published, highly illustrated technical guide demystifies how Rust implements dynamic dispatch through dyn Trait. Written by Sofía Belén and hosted on her personal site, the article walks readers step-by-step through the memory layout of trait objects — explaining why a &dyn Display isn’t just a regular pointer, but a fat pointer: two machine words wide, pairing a data address with a pointer to a vtable (virtual function table). Clear diagrams show how each vtable entry corresponds to a method in the trait, and how calling obj.method() under dyn Trait resolves at runtime via that table.

The guide goes beyond basics to tackle nuanced topics like dyn compatibility — the modern term for what used to be called object safety. It clarifies why traits like FnOnce or those with generic methods can’t be made into dyn Trait, not due to arbitrary restrictions, but because the vtable would lack fixed, known offsets for such methods. Real-world implications are highlighted: for example, how violating dyn compatibility leads to compile-time errors, not silent misbehavior — a deliberate Rust design choice favoring correctness over convenience.

The article also addresses subtle pitfalls developers encounter in practice. One section warns that multiple vtable copies may exist across compilation units — meaning two &dyn Trait pointers to the same underlying object might compare unequal, even though they point to identical data. This isn’t a bug, but a consequence of Rust’s current vtable ABI, where vtables aren’t guaranteed unique across crates or translation units. The author links this directly to debugging experiences, reinforcing why understanding memory layout matters when reasoning about pointer equality or FFI interop.

Complementing the deep dive, the guide recommends practical tools like cheats.rs, whose memory layout visualizations serve as excellent companion material. Readers praised its clarity and joyful writing style — one commenter noted re-reading the introduction just to savor its structure. While aimed at intermediate Rustaceans, the piece assumes no prior knowledge of vtables or ABI details, scaffolding concepts with concrete examples and consistent visuals. It stands out not just for accuracy, but for making low-level mechanics feel approachable and deeply relevant to everyday Rust development.
