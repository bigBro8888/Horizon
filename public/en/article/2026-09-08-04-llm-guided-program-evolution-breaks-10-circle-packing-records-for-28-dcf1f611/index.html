LLM-guided program evolution breaks 10 circle-packing records for $28

News time: 2026-09-08T05:55:49.224443+00:00 · Source: reddit

A researcher has demonstrated a novel use of large language models—not to generate answers directly, but to iteratively evolve optimization algorithms themselves. Starting from a simple baseline solver for the circle-packing problem, the system used an LLM to propose targeted code modifications—such as new neighborhood search strategies or perturbation rules—based on a scoreboard of prior results and a log of failed attempts. Each candidate algorithm was then independently compiled, run, and scored by a deterministic verifier; only improvements were retained. This human-in-the-loop, program-level evolutionary loop ran for just 15 iterations and improved the best-known sum-of-radii values for 10 consecutive problem sizes (N = 101 to 114) on the authoritative Packomania csqv benchmark.

The gains were modest in absolute terms but highly significant in context: improvements ranged from 2.4% to 5.4%, surpassing decades-old human-designed heuristics that had stood unchallenged. Crucially, all results were submitted to and independently validated by Packomania—the long-standing, community-maintained repository for optimal and best-known packings—whose maintainers accepted and published the new records. The total cost for LLM API calls was $27.72, underscoring the approach’s accessibility compared to traditional high-performance computing or expert labor.

Unlike standard LLM prompting or fine-tuning, this method treats the LLM as a meta-optimizer: it reasons about algorithmic structure, interprets empirical performance feedback, and proposes semantically meaningful code changes—not just syntax corrections. The paper explicitly documents its plateau-detection stopping rule, inviting scrutiny on how the loop decides when further iteration is unlikely to yield gains. That transparency, combined with open code, reproducible benchmarks, and third-party validation, sets a new bar for rigor in LLM-augmented scientific discovery.

This work sits at the intersection of combinatorial optimization, automated algorithm design, and responsible AI augmentation. It does not replace domain expertise—but rather extends it, turning years of incremental human progress into a testable, evolvable codebase. For researchers tackling other stubborn discrete optimization problems—from bin packing to graph layout—the framework offers a low-cost, high-signal template: define a verifier, seed a solver, and let the LLM suggest intelligent mutations.
