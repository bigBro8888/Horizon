Researchers track LLM performance drift with 31,352 repeated benchmark measurements

News time: 2026-09-08T05:55:49.224956+00:00 · Source: reddit

Most LLM benchmarks today act like one-time snapshots: a model is tested once, scored, and that number becomes its ‘official’ performance—despite the reality that API-hosted models often change silently behind the same name. A new longitudinal study challenges this static view by treating benchmarking as an ongoing measurement process. The team—led by Ionut Vi, founder of AI Stupid Level—ran over 31,000 repeated evaluations across 49 models, focusing on coding, multi-turn reasoning, and tool use. Rather than chasing leaderboard rankings, they asked sharper questions: Is a model behaving differently from its own past baseline? Is the observed shift larger than normal day-to-day variability? Could it be infrastructure failure—not capability loss?

Their data revealed striking temporal variation: within-day score standard deviation was just 2.80 points, while between-day median standard deviation jumped to 8.43 points—a roughly 3:1 ratio. This doesn’t prove daily model updates, but it does show that treating scores as stable constants is statistically unjustified. To avoid confounding factors, the methodology strictly versioned benchmark configurations, separated availability failures from task outcomes, logged serving metadata when available, and applied statistical change-point detection to time-series results.

The team also confronts benchmark contamination—a growing concern as popular evaluation sets leak into training data. Instead of publishing live prompts and test cases, their public methodology document (released as a PDF) details design choices, assumptions, limitations, and statistical interpretation—while withholding the exact task bank and operational parameters. This balances scientific transparency with measurement integrity.

Crucially, the work isn’t about selling a product: Vi explicitly states the goal is technical critique—not promotion. He invites feedback from experts in evaluation, change-point detection, and production ML on open questions: Should daily medians or raw repeated observations anchor the time series? How do we disentangle true model drift from infrastructure quirks when version metadata is missing? And how much of a live benchmark should stay hidden to prevent contamination—without sacrificing inspectability? These aren’t theoretical puzzles; they’re urgent operational questions for anyone deploying LLMs at scale.
