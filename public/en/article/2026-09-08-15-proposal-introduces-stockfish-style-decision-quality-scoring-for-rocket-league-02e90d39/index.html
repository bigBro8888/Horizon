Proposal introduces Stockfish-style decision quality scoring for Rocket League

News time: 2026-09-08T05:55:49.224799+00:00 · Source: reddit

What if competitive video games had a built-in 'truth engine'—a system that doesn’t just track stats like boost usage or top speed, but judges whether each in-game decision was mathematically optimal? That’s the core idea behind a recent proposal on Reddit’s r/MachineLearning, where a first-semester student—writing under the username /u/Ligras—asked why real-time esports like Rocket League lack an equivalent to chess engines such as Stockfish. Unlike chess, where move quality can be precisely evaluated against known optimal play, fast-paced physics-driven games have no widely adopted standard for measuring decision fidelity.

The proposal moves beyond naive physics simulation (e.g., cloud-based 5-second rollouts) and instead leans into modern offline reinforcement learning. At its heart is a Sequential Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) framework, where player actions are assessed not in isolation, but as part of temporally extended trajectories—states, actions, and rewards treated as a unified sequence. The model leverages Trajectory Transformers (trained on professional replay datasets) and Implicit Q-Learning to estimate how closely a given action aligns with high-level expert behavior distributions—not perfect simulation, but statistically grounded plausibility.

Beyond performance evaluation, the system doubles as an anti-cheat tool. It applies Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) analysis to raw controller input signals to detect unnatural frequency patterns typical of bot scripts. It also monitors kinematic limits—specifically the fourth derivative of position, known as snap—to flag movements exceeding human biomechanical feasibility. Smurf detection uses negative log-likelihood (NLL) divergence: when a player’s observed behavior deviates significantly from expected skill-level distributions, the system raises a statistical red flag.

Crucially, the author emphasizes this is a conceptual sketch—not a working implementation—and explicitly disclaims any intent to claim credit. They acknowledge lacking the technical background to build it themselves, framing the post as an invitation for researchers and engineers to explore the intersection of offline RL, signal processing, and competitive integrity. The full technical outline is hosted publicly on GitHub, inviting scrutiny, refinement, and potential collaboration.
