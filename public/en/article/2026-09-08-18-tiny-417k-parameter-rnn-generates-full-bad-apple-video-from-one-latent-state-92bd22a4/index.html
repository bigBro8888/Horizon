Tiny 417k-parameter RNN generates full Bad Apple video from one latent state

News time: 2026-09-08T05:55:49.224142+00:00 · Source: reddit

A researcher has built a remarkably compact recurrent dynamical system — just 417,129 parameters — that autonomously generates the entire 6,500-frame, full-resolution Bad Apple!! anime music video from a single 128-dimensional initial latent state. Unlike standard video generation models that rely on explicit timestamps or frame-by-frame conditioning, this system runs entirely in closed-loop mode: it iteratively updates its internal hidden and cell states using a custom 4-gate LSTM-style transition function, then decodes each state into a 384×512 grayscale frame — all without any time input.

The architecture splits cleanly into two parts: a lightweight recurrent transition function (CTF) with only 16,640 parameters, and a frame decoder (FD) using depthwise-separable convolutions and bilinear upsampling (400,361 parameters). The model’s total inference footprint is just ~1.6 MB in FP32, and it runs at over 200 FPS on an RTX 4080 while using only ~17.2 MB of VRAM — orders of magnitude leaner than diffusion- or transformer-based video models.

Training such a long autonomous sequence was far from trivial. To avoid gradient instability and error accumulation over thousands of steps, the author used a multi-stage curriculum: starting with 2-frame rollouts and progressively doubling the horizon up to 512 frames. Crucially, they introduced learned latent teacher tables — temporary h_table[t] and c_table[t] vectors optimized during training but discarded at inference — enabling parallel segment training. Additional techniques included state perturbation noise (σ = 0.005) to encourage robustness to numerical drift, second-difference regularization to enforce smooth motion without suppressing velocity, and careful momentum scaling in Muon optimizer when increasing rollout length.

What makes this work especially compelling is its conceptual clarity: it treats video not as a sequence of discrete frames, but as a continuous trajectory in a low-dimensional dynamical system — echoing ideas from physics-informed modeling and implicit neural representations (INRs). While the SIREN MLP approach memorizes video as a static coordinate-to-pixel mapping (t, x, y) → pixel, this RNN learns how the latent state evolves over time — a fundamentally generative, autonomous perspective. The fact that the best-performing checkpoint wasn’t the one with lowest training loss underscores a key insight: dynamical stability matters more than short-horizon fidelity. This isn’t about scaling up — it’s about designing for emergence, resilience, and minimalism in generative dynamics.
