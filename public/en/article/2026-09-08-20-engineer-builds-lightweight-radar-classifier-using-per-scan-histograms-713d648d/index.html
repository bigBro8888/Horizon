Engineer builds lightweight radar classifier using per-scan histograms

News time: 2026-09-08T05:55:49.224724+00:00 · Source: reddit

A radar signal processing engineer has developed a practical, five-class object classifier for automotive radar perception using the publicly available RadarScenes dataset. Instead of relying on complex point cloud architectures like PointPillars or VoxelNet, the approach uses a simple yet effective feature: a 16-bin histogram computed per radar scan — capturing distributions of velocity-compensated range, Doppler, and radar cross-section (RCS) across all detected points. This histogram is fed into a compact three-layer multilayer perceptron (MLP), trained with class-weighted cross-entropy loss to counteract severe data imbalance — especially for rare classes like two-wheelers and large vehicles.

The model achieves a macro F1 score of 0.764 when objects have at least five radar detections — but drops sharply to 0.381 for single-point instances. This reveals a core challenge in radar perception: sparsity. Ablation studies confirmed that architectural changes — deeper MLPs, alternative encodings, or different binning strategies — yielded smaller improvements than the variance introduced by train/validation/test splits, underscoring how sensitive radar classification is to data partitioning. Notably, the author found that replacing histograms with per-instance statistics (e.g., mean/median/std) slightly degraded performance, suggesting distributional shape matters more than summary aggregates.

Confusion patterns offer valuable diagnostic insight. Cars are often misclassified as large vehicles when exhibiting unusually high RCS — possibly due to multipath reflections — while two-wheelers are frequently confused with pedestrians. This stems from overlapping velocity-compensated signatures: stationary or idling scooters and bicycles produce near-zero Doppler shifts indistinguishable from standing people. In one illustrative case, a single-point two-wheeler was labeled as pedestrian, whereas a single-point car in the same scene was classified correctly — highlighting how RCS and Doppler jointly support robust car identification, but not for two-wheelers.

The work intentionally focuses on single-scan inference — a pragmatic starting point — with multi-scan accumulation flagged as critical future work. The author also plans to explore richer spatial encodings like PointNet and investigate micro-Doppler features to better separate subtle motion patterns. All code, experiments, and visualizations are openly shared in a detailed technical report, making this a highly accessible reference for engineers tackling real-world radar perception challenges.
