DaVinci Resolve 21.1 launches with free Pro upgrades and Linux audio codec gaps

News time: 2026-09-09T05:57:31.956148+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Blackmagic Design has released DaVinci Resolve 21.1 — a refined, stability-focused update that continues the company’s rare commitment to free major upgrades for existing Pro users. Unlike most professional creative software vendors shifting toward subscription-only models, BMD maintains a perpetual license path: once you own DaVinci Resolve Studio, all future versions — including AI-powered features in 21.1 — arrive at no extra cost. This policy has drawn consistent praise from long-time users, who highlight its contrast with industry trends and emphasize how it lowers long-term cost of ownership for editors, colorists, and sound designers.

Yet the release also spotlights persistent friction points, especially for Linux users. While Resolve officially supports Linux on select distributions like Rocky Linux 8.6+ and CentOS 7.3, critical limitations remain unaddressed in 21.1: native H.264 video and AAC audio playback and export are still unsupported. That forces Linux-based editors to transcode source files — a time- and storage-intensive workaround — before editing or exporting, undermining real-world workflow efficiency. One user noted they’d edited over 100 podcasts on Windows Resolve but found Linux alternatives like Kdenlive frustratingly slow by comparison.

Audio production on Linux faces deeper integration gaps. Although Fairlight — Resolve’s built-in audio suite — runs on Linux, it lacks VST3 plugin support, JACK audio server compatibility, and MIDI control surface functionality. As a result, professionals must shuttle audio work to external DAWs like Reaper, breaking continuity and adding complexity. These omissions aren’t oversights in isolation; they reflect broader ecosystem challenges around open-source audio infrastructure and hardware abstraction layers on Linux.

The new AI assistant integration — supporting Claude, Claude Code, and ChatGPT Codex — adds conversational project analysis, media organization, batch rendering, and even automated highlight reel generation. While promising for power users, some community members expressed concern about privacy, reliability, and whether such features distract from core editing stability. Still, overall sentiment remains strongly positive: Resolve 21.1 is widely seen not as a flashy overhaul, but as a dependable, incremental step forward — one that reaffirms BMD’s engineering discipline and respect for professional workflows across platforms.
