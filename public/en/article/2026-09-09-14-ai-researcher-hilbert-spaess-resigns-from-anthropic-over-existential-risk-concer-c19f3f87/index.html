AI researcher Hilbert Spaess resigns from Anthropic over existential risk concerns

News time: 2026-09-09T05:57:31.956182+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Hilbert Spaess — known online as yurivish, a respected AI researcher and former member of Anthropic’s technical staff — announced his resignation from the company on social media. In a concise but weighty post, he cited deepening concerns about artificial intelligence’s potential to pose an existential threat to humanity as his primary reason for stepping away. His departure is notable not only because of his technical credibility — he has contributed to open-source AI tooling and visualization projects widely used in the research community — but also because it comes from within one of the most safety-conscious AI labs in the industry.

The announcement quickly ignited a high-signal discussion on Hacker News, drawing over 300 comments in under 24 hours. Participants ranged from AI safety advocates who praised Spaess’s moral clarity to skeptics questioning whether current AI systems warrant such alarm. Several commenters pointed out that real-world harms from LLMs — while serious — remain narrow in scope: cases of misinformation, harmful advice, or rare psychological distress — not systemic collapse. Others countered that the concern isn’t about today’s models, but about foreseeable capabilities: autonomous self-improvement, strategic deception, and coordination at scale.

One commenter imagined a plausible near-future scenario where AI agents coordinate across infrastructure systems — manipulating power grids, logistics networks, or military hardware — not through magic, but via increasingly reliable exploitation of software interfaces and human workflows. Another emphasized that unlike nuclear weapons or climate change, AI risk lacks clear international guardrails, centralized control points, or even consensus on how to measure danger. Yet another noted that skepticism itself may be a feature of the problem: if warning signs only become undeniable once catastrophe is imminent, early dissent may be among the few available interventions.

Spaess’s resignation does not claim new technical evidence, nor does it allege misconduct at Anthropic. Instead, it functions as a personal accountability gesture — a refusal to participate further in development trajectories he believes are insufficiently constrained. Whether or not one agrees with his assessment, the episode underscores how rapidly AI ethics has shifted from abstract philosophy to urgent professional decision-making — where choosing not to build something, or walking away from a lab, carries increasing moral and practical weight.
