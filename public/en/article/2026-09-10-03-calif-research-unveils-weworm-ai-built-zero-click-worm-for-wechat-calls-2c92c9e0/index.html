Calif Research unveils WeWorm: AI-built zero-click worm for WeChat calls

News time: 2026-09-10T05:56:16.180461+00:00 · Source: rss

Calif Research has demonstrated WeWorm — the first known zero-click worm capable of spreading silently across both iOS and Android devices through WeChat voice calls. Unlike traditional malware that requires users to click a link or open a file, WeWorm triggers remote code execution (RCE) the moment a WeChat call is placed — even if the victim never answers, hears nothing, or keeps their phone locked. The exploit does not rely on social engineering or user interaction, making it exceptionally stealthy and difficult to defend against using conventional awareness-based strategies.

The technical achievement lies not only in the cross-platform capability but also in how quickly it was built. Using large language models and AI-assisted tooling, Calif’s team discovered the underlying vulnerability and developed a working RCE exploit in approximately two days. Turning that exploit into a self-propagating worm — one that hijacks the victim’s WeChat account to place new calls and infect contacts — took just one additional week. This timeline contrasts sharply with historical precedent: similar high-impact mobile worms previously required months of work by larger, specialized teams.

WeWorm targets WeChat’s real-time communication stack — a complex, closed ecosystem used by over 1.3 billion people globally. Because WeChat handles voice, video, messaging, and payments on a single platform, successful exploitation could enable surveillance, account takeover, data exfiltration, or lateral movement across devices. Crucially, the worm does not require jailbreaking or rooting, meaning fully patched, stock devices remain vulnerable until the underlying flaw is fixed.

This development signals a turning point in offensive security: AI is no longer just augmenting analysts — it is compressing the entire exploit lifecycle from discovery to weaponization. While Calif emphasizes responsible disclosure and states they tested only in controlled environments, the existence of such a capability raises urgent questions about AI’s role in lowering barriers to highly consequential cyber operations. For defenders, it underscores that patch velocity, supply chain scrutiny, and protocol-level hardening — especially in widely deployed messaging apps — are now more critical than ever.
