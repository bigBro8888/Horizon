No Man's Sky launches Cosmos update with space stations and terrain overhaul

News time: 2026-09-10T05:56:16.628102+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Hello Games has released the 'Cosmos' update — version 7.0 — for No Man's Sky, its procedurally generated space exploration game now in its tenth year of active development. The update introduces player-owned space stations, alliance systems, deep-space marvels, and a major visual overhaul to planetary terrain rendering. It arrives not as a paid expansion but as the 40th free major update since launch, continuing a sustained post-launch evolution that has transformed the game from a controversial 2016 release into a critically and commercially successful title.

The Cosmos update significantly refines how players interact with the cosmos itself. For the first time, players can claim and fully customize their own orbital space station — complete with interior layouts, resource processing, and defense systems. Alliances let groups of players coordinate large-scale projects like fleet construction or galactic cartography. Meanwhile, behind the scenes, the terrain engine has been rebuilt using improved tessellation techniques, delivering sharper ground textures, smoother elevation transitions, and richer surface detail on all 18 quintillion procedurally generated planets.

This expansion lands amid a broader cultural reassessment of No Man’s Sky’s identity. Some longtime players still describe it as "a stunning tech demo" — praising its scale and ambition while questioning whether its systems cohere into meaningful gameplay loops. Others point to concrete metrics: over $500 million in revenue, 15–20 million copies sold, and an 84% positive Steam review rating — all achieved without monetizing core updates. The debate reflects deeper tensions in game design: Can procedural scale substitute for authored depth? Does persistent iteration redeem early missteps?

What makes Cosmos notable isn’t just its features, but its place in a decade-long arc of accountability and adaptation. After the backlash to its 2016 launch — widely criticized for missing promised features — Hello Games committed to transparent, frequent, and free updates. Cosmos doesn’t attempt to reinvent the game; instead, it extends existing pillars — exploration, base-building, economy, and community — with greater fidelity and interconnectivity. For players returning after years away, it feels less like a new game and more like the universe finally catching up to its original vision.
