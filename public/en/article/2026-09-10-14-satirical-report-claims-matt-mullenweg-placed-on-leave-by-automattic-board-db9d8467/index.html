Satirical report claims Matt Mullenweg placed on leave by Automattic board

News time: 2026-09-10T05:56:16.628182+00:00 · Source: hackernews

A satirical article circulating online claims that Automattic’s board has placed co-founder and CEO Matt Mullenweg on a paid leave of absence — a story that quickly gained traction on tech forums despite containing clear fictional markers. The piece, dated September 9, 2026 (a date two years in the future), cites an internal Slack announcement in which Mullenweg allegedly accuses CFO Mark Davies and board members Ann Dunwoody, Toni Schneider, and Sue Decker of conspiring behind his back. It further describes a board vote he opposed — all presented with deadpan seriousness, as if reporting breaking corporate news.

In reality, no credible outlet or official channel has confirmed this event. The URL domain (techcrunch.com) hosts real journalism, but the publication date is impossible, and the article does not appear on TechCrunch’s actual site. There are no corroborating reports from Reuters, Bloomberg, or Automattic’s own blog or press releases. The story bears hallmarks of internet satire: exaggerated timing (coinciding with Burning Man), absurd board composition (Dunwoody is a retired U.S. Army general with no known ties to Automattic), and tone-deaf corporate drama tropes.

Still, the hoax resonated because Mullenweg is a singular figure in open-source infrastructure. As the original creator of WordPress and long-time steward of Automattic, he embodies both the idealism and growing pains of decentralized web governance. His leadership style — highly centralized, opinionated, and sometimes polarizing — has drawn scrutiny in recent years, especially as WordPress faces competition from modern headless CMSs and AI-native platforms. That context made the satire feel plausible enough to spark discussion, even among seasoned observers.

The Hacker News thread reveals how easily irony blurs with concern in tech discourse. Some commenters feigned alarm, others mocked the premise outright, while a few voiced genuine unease about leadership continuity in foundational open-source projects. One user noted that while the story is fake, the underlying questions — about accountability, succession planning, and founder control in mission-driven companies — are very real. In that sense, the satire succeeded not as news, but as a mirror.
