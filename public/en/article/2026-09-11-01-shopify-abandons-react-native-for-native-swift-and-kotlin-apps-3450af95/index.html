Shopify abandons React Native for native Swift and Kotlin apps

News time: 2026-09-11T05:55:51.347851+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Shopify has officially reversed its 2020 decision to adopt React Native for its mobile apps, announcing a full migration back to separate native codebases: Swift for iOS and Kotlin for Android. In a candid engineering blog post titled Native is now the future of mobile at Shopify, the company cites sustained performance bottlenecks, growing maintenance complexity, and mounting security concerns—particularly around npm dependency supply chain risks—as decisive factors. Unlike startups or mid-sized teams that benefit from cross-platform speed, Shopify’s scale magnified React Native’s trade-offs: inconsistent UI rendering across platforms, unpredictable memory behavior under heavy merchant workflows, and increasing difficulty debugging deep platform-specific issues.

The shift wasn’t impulsive. Shopify spent over two years evaluating alternatives—including Flutter—and ran extensive benchmarks comparing startup time, frame consistency, memory pressure, and crash rates across real-world merchant use cases (e.g., inventory scanning, order fulfillment, live dashboard updates). Native implementations consistently outperformed React Native by 30–50% in cold-start latency and reduced jank during complex animations. Crucially, the team also observed that over 40% of critical production crashes originated in third-party React Native modules or bridging layers—not their own business logic.

Security played an equally pivotal role. As highlighted by community commenters and corroborated by recent industry reports, npm-based ecosystems have faced dozens of high-impact supply chain compromises since 2023—from malicious typosquatting packages to hijacked maintainer accounts. For a platform handling billions in e-commerce transactions, every unvetted JavaScript dependency introduced unacceptable risk. Moving to Swift and Kotlin eliminates reliance on npm entirely, shifting trust boundaries to Apple’s and Google’s curated SDKs and toolchains.

This isn’t just a technical course correction—it’s a strategic signal. Shopify joins a quiet but growing cohort of large-scale adopters (including Microsoft, Discord, and Bloomberg) stepping away from hybrid frameworks as their apps mature. Their public rationale underscores a broader truth: cross-platform tools excel at velocity for MVPs and simpler apps, but native development remains the gold standard for performance-critical, security-sensitive, and long-lived applications. For engineering leaders weighing architecture decisions, Shopify’s case offers hard-won, empirically grounded lessons—not theory, but lived experience at planetary scale.
