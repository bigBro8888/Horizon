A 210M DiT trained on one GPU reveals null attention sinks and loss-quality decoupling

News time: 2026-09-12T05:44:44.875676+00:00 · Source: reddit

A practitioner named Ivan Mikhnenkov successfully trained a 210-million-parameter text-to-image diffusion transformer (DiT) from scratch on a single RTX PRO 6000 GPU — completing the full training run in just 3.5 days. Using 4.2 million images at 256×256 resolution, the experiment was not aimed at setting new generation benchmarks, but rather at dissecting the training dynamics of modern DiTs end to end. The result is a rare, hands-on empirical study that surfaces three previously undocumented behaviors — two of which challenge widely held assumptions in diffusion model development.

The first finding centers on learned null attention slots. The model incorporates two extra learnable key/value slots in every cross-attention layer — inspired by register tokens used in vision transformers. Mid-training, these slots absorb roughly 90% of the cross-attention mass at mid-noise levels in middle transformer blocks. In contrast, the EOS token — traditionally treated as the primary attention sink — drops to just ~4%, while content words retain only a few percent each, tightly aligned with their visual referents. Crucially, the register vectors grow to 4–13× the norm of regular image tokens, suggesting they evolve into dedicated global summarizers rather than passive placeholders.

Second, the flow-matching loss — often treated as a proxy for sample quality — proves misleading. Over training, it improved only slightly (0.805 → 0.754), while perceptual metrics surged: FID dropped from 33.7 to 27.0, FD-DINOv2 from 570 to 218, and object detection accuracy rose from 65% to 90%. The loss plateaued early and stayed nearly identical between train and validation sets for 24 epochs — indicating it tracks optimization stability, not fidelity. Most of the residual loss stems from irreducible noise in the velocity target itself.

Third, a simple timestep shift — derived from FLUX.2’s latent geometry — delivered more gains than doubling sampling steps. With shift 2.8, 20-step sampling achieved FID 27.0; without shift, the same steps yielded FID 27.3 and worse FD-DINOv2. This underscores how architectural choices in scheduling can outweigh brute-force step inflation. The full setup — including RoPE, QK-norm, SwiGLU, and frozen FLAN-T5 text encoders — is fully open-sourced, with weights, demo, and blog post publicly available.
