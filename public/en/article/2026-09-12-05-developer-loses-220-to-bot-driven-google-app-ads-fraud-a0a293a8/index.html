Developer loses $220 to bot-driven Google App Ads fraud

News time: 2026-09-12T05:44:44.377375+00:00 · Source: hackernews

A mobile app developer recently shared a sobering experience: after spending $220 on Google App Ads to promote their Android app, they discovered that 60% of the resulting installs were fraudulent — generated not by real users, but by coordinated bot farms. Using basic analytics and manual IP inspection, the developer traced suspicious traffic to data center IP ranges, confirmed via geolocation tools like ipgeolocation.io. The post, hosted on dayzlegame.com, quickly gained traction in performance marketing circles for its candid, hands-on account of ad fraud — not as abstract theory, but as a direct financial hit.

This isn’t an isolated incident. Commenters across Reddit and the blog’s thread corroborated similar losses — one developer recounted being banned by AdMob after buying Google Ads to drive traffic to their own app, highlighting a troubling misalignment in Google’s enforcement logic. Others described parallel issues on Reddit Ads, where support vanished mid-complaint, suggesting systemic tolerance rather than oversight failure. The pattern points to a broader tension: platforms profit from ad spend regardless of outcome, while advertisers bear full risk for invalid traffic.

The developer didn’t just document the problem — they offered concrete countermeasures. Most notably, they recommended using Google Ads’ built-in IP exclusion feature to block entire CIDR-like ranges (e.g., 194.82.210.*) tied to known bot infrastructure. One commenter noted maintaining a list of over 4,000 excluded networks in the US alone — a telling sign of how entrenched this issue has become. While Google claims recent AI upgrades improve fraud detection precision to 99.8%, real-world cases like this reveal persistent gaps between stated capabilities and on-the-ground enforcement.

What makes this case especially valuable is its transparency: no anonymized dashboards or vendor slides — just raw spend, install logs, IP lookups, and actionable steps. It underscores a hard truth for indie developers and small studios: performance marketing today demands not just creative targeting, but forensic-level traffic hygiene. Without proactive safeguards like IP exclusions, device fingerprinting, or third-party fraud detection tools, even modest ad budgets can vanish into bot-infested data centers.
