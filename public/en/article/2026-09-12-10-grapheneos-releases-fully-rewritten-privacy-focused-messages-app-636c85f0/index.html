GrapheneOS releases fully rewritten privacy-focused Messages app

News time: 2026-09-12T05:44:44.377441+00:00 · Source: hackernews

GrapheneOS has launched a ground-up rewrite of its default messaging application, now officially version 13 and available on GitHub. Unlike the previous iteration—built on Android’s legacy SMS framework—the new app is designed from scratch with modern privacy, security, and usability principles at its core. It supports RCS (Rich Communication Services) only when explicitly enabled by the user, avoids unnecessary permissions like location or contacts access by default, and enforces strict sandboxing to limit data exposure. The UI has also been streamlined: messages are grouped chronologically, delivery status is clearly indicated, and threading is handled more intuitively than in stock Android messaging apps.

This release reflects GrapheneOS’s broader philosophy: not just removing Google services, but actively rebuilding essential components to reduce attack surface and increase transparency. The old Messages app relied heavily on AOSP’s aging telephony stack, which introduced complexity and potential vulnerabilities—especially around carrier-specific SMS handling and MMS parsing. By replacing it with a minimal, auditable codebase written in Kotlin and leveraging modern Android APIs, the team significantly improves maintainability and long-term security posture.

The update arrives amid growing scrutiny of mobile communications privacy. With zero-day exploits targeting Android’s messaging subsystem appearing regularly in CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, hardened alternatives are no longer niche—they’re operational necessities for high-risk users like journalists and human rights defenders. While GrapheneOS remains limited to Pixel devices (and soon select Motorola models), its modular architecture means future versions could be adapted by other privacy-first projects—or even influence upstream Android development.

That said, adoption isn’t frictionless. As community comments highlight, users are still waiting for official Fairephone support, and questions remain about whether the new app can be sideloaded onto existing GrapheneOS installations or requires a full OS update. Some users also voiced frustration with the broader telephony experience—particularly the call log interface—which underscores that messaging is only one piece of a larger UX puzzle. Still, this release marks a concrete step toward making privacy-respecting communication tools both robust and usable.
