Signal to allow phone-number-free registration using zero-knowledge proofs

News time: 2026-09-14T06:13:36.844836+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Signal is preparing to eliminate its long-standing phone number requirement for account registration — a foundational privacy limitation since its launch. Instead of SMS-based verification, new users on Android will be able to register via a one-time purchase through Google Play Billing, backed by cryptographic zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs). This means Signal’s servers will verify that a user has completed a valid payment without learning which specific account or device corresponds to that transaction. The feature, codenamed "Signal Login" in the Android source code, is not yet live but has been confirmed through recent commits and technical analysis.

This shift addresses a core tension in secure messaging: how to prevent spam and abuse without sacrificing anonymity. Phone numbers have long served as both identity anchors and privacy leaks — linking accounts to real-world identifiers, enabling surveillance, SIM-swapping, and metadata correlation. By replacing them with a cryptographically verifiable, unlinkable payment event, Signal moves closer to true pseudonymity. Crucially, the ZKP design ensures that even Signal itself cannot connect a payment to a user’s public key, group memberships, or message history.

The implementation is tightly scoped: it applies only to new Android registrations and requires Google Play Billing, meaning it won’t be available on sideloaded APKs, F-Droid, or iOS at launch. Existing users retain their phone-number-bound identities but can now use Android tablets without SIM cards as first-class devices — a related but separate improvement. That change removes workarounds previously needed to run Signal on tablets, improving usability without altering identity models.

Critics rightly point out that while ZKPs are mathematically sound, real-world privacy depends on full system transparency. As one community member noted, Signal has not open-sourced its backend infrastructure automation — raising questions about auditability and resilience if compromised. Others emphasize that the announcement lacks sufficient technical detail to assess whether the ZKP construction actually achieves its stated privacy goals. Still, this represents one of the most ambitious production-grade uses of zero-knowledge cryptography in a mainstream privacy tool to date — not as a theoretical demo, but as an integral part of onboarding.
