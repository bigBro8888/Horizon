Hacker News developers share AI agents, meal planners, note-based music tools, and a decade-old voxel engine

News time: 2026-09-14T06:13:36.844945+00:00 · Source: hackernews

In a standout 'Ask HN: What are you working on?' thread from September 2026, over 350 developers shared unusually substantive personal projects — not just side hustles, but deeply technical, long-gestating tools reflecting real engineering priorities. The thread stands out for its density of thoughtful work: from Smalldocs, an open-source artifact layer designed specifically for coding agents to organize charts, diagrams, and spreadsheets in one place; to Meal Planner Tool, a pragmatic web app born from family kitchen logistics and now being polished for public use; to Tunesage, an experimental note-based AI music generator that treats melodies as symbolic sequences rather than raw audio waveforms.

One of the most striking entries is Bonsai — a voxel game engine under active development for nearly a decade. Its creator describes a recent major rewrite centered on signed distance fields (SDFs) and density-field representations, enabling precise, editable world geometry through parameterized projections into voxel grids. Unlike mainstream engines, Bonsai prioritizes procedural authoring and editor-first workflows — a niche but growing interest among indie game developers exploring alternatives to Unity or Unreal for stylized, scalable worlds. Another notable project is CurveFit.app, a weight-training tool applying Pareto frontier logic to balance rep ranges and load across movements — a rare example of domain-specific optimization applied to fitness software.

What makes this thread distinctive isn’t just variety, but depth: contributors openly discuss tradeoffs like open-core monetization (Smalldocs’ cloud library is the sole paid feature), early-launch regrets (Tunesage’s generic melodies), and architectural pivots (Bonsai’s SDF-based rewrite). Comments are concise and technically grounded — asking about local-first sync strategies, SDF rasterization performance, or how Meal Planner handles ingredient substitution — signaling peer-level dialogue rather than casual curiosity.

The thread reflects broader trends: the maturation of AI agent infrastructure beyond LLM wrappers; renewed interest in lightweight, purpose-built web apps; and sustained grassroots investment in alternative graphics and simulation paradigms. It also underscores how Hacker News continues to serve as a low-noise forum for engineers to surface unfinished, meaningful work — not just shipped products, but the messy, iterative labor behind them.
