Simon Willison names three blog posts that reshaped his engineering mindset

News time: 2026-09-15T06:08:49.806203+00:00 · Source: rss

In a reflective blog post published on September 14, 2026, Simon Willison — co-creator of Django and a widely respected voice in web development — shared the three blog posts that most profoundly shaped his approach to software engineering. Rather than citing academic papers or corporate engineering handbooks, Willison turned to long-form, practitioner-written blog posts: foundational, accessible, and deeply human accounts of real-world technical reasoning.

The first is Joel Spolsky’s 2002 classic The Law of Leaky Abstractions. Willison read it early in his career and credits it with instilling a lasting habit: never fully trusting abstractions. Whether it’s an ORM hiding SQL complexity, a cloud SDK masking network failures, or a frontend framework abstracting away browser inconsistencies, Spolsky’s insight reminds engineers that every abstraction eventually breaks down — and when it does, understanding the layer beneath becomes essential. That mindset shift helped Willison debug more effectively and design systems with appropriate fallbacks and observability.

The second is Will Larson’s 2018 essay Migrations: the sole scalable fix to tech debt. Unlike many who treat migrations as risky, one-off emergencies, Larson frames them as routine, learnable engineering skills — like testing or code review. Willison embraced this view, especially while scaling infrastructure at startups: replacing legacy databases, shifting from monoliths to services, or upgrading major dependencies weren’t exceptions but expected, scheduled work. This reframing reduces fear, improves planning, and turns tech debt from a looming crisis into manageable, incremental investment.

The third, Charity Majors’ The Engineer/Manager Pendulum, addressed a personal inflection point. As Willison considered stepping back from engineering management, he worried about career stagnation or loss of technical credibility. Majors’ argument — that moving fluidly between IC and leadership roles builds deeper empathy, broader context, and stronger outcomes — gave him permission to pivot without guilt. Her perspective reflects a growing cultural shift away from rigid career ladders toward dynamic, experience-rich engineering paths. Together, these three posts illustrate how informal, public writing continues to shape professional practice — not through authority, but through clarity, honesty, and lived insight.
