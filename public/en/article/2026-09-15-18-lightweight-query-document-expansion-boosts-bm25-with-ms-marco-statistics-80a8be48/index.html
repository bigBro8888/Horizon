Lightweight query-document expansion boosts BM25 with MS MARCO statistics

News time: 2026-09-15T06:08:50.692524+00:00 · Source: reddit

A new open-source technique called 'poor man’s DSSM' offers a surprisingly effective way to add semantic awareness to traditional full-text search—without neural networks. Developed by Reddit user /u/SpiritedTrip, the method builds count-based translation tables from supervised query-document pairs—such as those in the MS MARCO dataset or real-world click logs. Instead of training deep models, it simply counts how often document-side text units (e.g., words or wordpieces) co-occur with query-side units across relevant pairs. For each document unit, it retains the top-k most strongly associated query units and injects them directly into the inverted index during indexing—effectively expanding each document’s postings with semantically related terms.

This approach sits between classical term-matching and modern neural retrieval. Unlike BM25—which relies solely on lexical matching and term frequency—inverted index expansion here introduces weak but interpretable semantic signals. It’s not synonym replacement: a document containing 'car' might get expanded with 'automobile', 'vehicle', or even 'Tesla', depending on what queries historically led users to that document. The expansion is asymmetric and data-driven, grounded entirely in observed user behavior rather than linguistic rules or pretrained embeddings.

The implementation is deliberately minimal. The author packaged the precomputed expansion tables as a Hugging Face model repository, complete with a short demo script showing how to integrate them into a standard Lucene- or Whoosh-style indexer. Performance gains are modest but consistent—typically 2–4 percentage points improvement in MRR@10 over vanilla BM25 on MS MARCO dev sets—making it especially attractive for production systems where latency, cost, or interpretability constrain neural solutions.

Crucially, the method inherits a key limitation from its statistical roots: it captures only linear associations. Where DSSM uses deep neural networks to learn non-linear semantic mappings in high-dimensional space, this version treats co-occurrence as a direct, rank-based lookup. That makes it fast and debuggable—but unable to handle compositional meaning or rare long-tail queries without sufficient click history. Still, for many real-world search engines—especially those serving niche domains or constrained environments—it represents a pragmatic, low-risk upgrade path toward better relevance.
