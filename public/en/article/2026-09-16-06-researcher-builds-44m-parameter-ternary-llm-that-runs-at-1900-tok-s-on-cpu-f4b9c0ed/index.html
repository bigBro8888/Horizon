Researcher builds 44M-parameter ternary LLM that runs at 1900 tok/s on CPU

News time: 2026-09-16T05:58:30.506689+00:00 · Source: reddit

A researcher has unveiled SHADOW-50M — a novel, ultra-lightweight large language model trained entirely from scratch with radical architectural choices aimed at edge deployment and reliable reasoning. At just 44 million parameters and 19.8 MB in size, it fits entirely in RAM on modest laptops and delivers ~1,900 tokens per second on a single-threaded CPU — over four times faster than its predecessor, SHADOW-250M. Unlike conventional models, SHADOW-50M uses ternary weights (−1, 0, +1) instead of floating-point or even 4-bit quantized values, drastically reducing compute and memory bandwidth demands. Its vocabulary isn’t learned via embedding layers but encoded as fixed 512-bit cryptographic fingerprints for all 73,880 tokens — eliminating embedding lookups and enabling deterministic, cache-friendly token resolution.

The model also embeds domain-specific hardware-like circuits directly into its inference pipeline. When it detects a calculation trigger like [calc]347*86[eq], a precompiled 159 KB kernel takes over — computing the result digit-by-digit and injecting it back into the token stream without external APIs, tool calls, or prompt rewriting. Circuits handle arithmetic, percentages, date math, unit conversions, sorting, comparisons, and even a minimal programmable state machine. For long-term memory, SHADOW stores attention states to disk at just 1 bit per token (288 bytes/token), using a compact 22-byte-per-token index that locates records in under a microsecond — no vector database or embedding model required.

Benchmark comparisons reveal SHADOW’s deliberate trade-offs: it underperforms Supra-50M-Reasoning (a 51.8M bf16 Llama-style model) on standard NLP tasks like ARC-Easy and WikiText-2 perplexity. But on real-world reasoning and retrieval — such as answering ‘What is the condition of Patient P-204?’ after indexing eight medical records — SHADOW succeeds reliably while Supra hallucinates. Its outputs are greedy, deterministic, and fully offline — running identically in WebAssembly at ~500 tok/s in-browser.

This isn’t a drop-in replacement for cloud-scale LLMs. It’s a proof-of-concept demonstrating how tightly co-designed architecture — ternary weights, fingerprint vocabularies, fixed-function circuits, and memory-mapped record indexing — can overcome classic bottlenecks in small-model reasoning and efficiency. The code, traces, and side-by-side evaluations are publicly available, inviting scrutiny and extension by the TinyML and edge-AI communities.
