NeurIPS 2026 authors question fairness of multi-venue assignment

News time: 2026-09-16T05:58:30.506769+00:00 · Source: reddit

A growing concern has emerged among NeurIPS 2026 paper authors about how the conference is handling its new multi-venue format. For the first time, NeurIPS is holding simultaneous in-person events in Sydney, Atlanta, and Paris — a logistical shift intended to improve global accessibility. But early signals suggest the rollout may be straining community trust: Sydney passes sold out within minutes, while authors received venue preference forms without any guarantee they’d present at their top choice.

This uncertainty has sparked debate over equity and transparency. One author, posting on Reddit under the username /u/CantKillTheLifeless, questioned whether the designation of Sydney as the 'main' venue creates a two-tier experience — with premier talks, networking, and visibility concentrated there, while other locations become de facto 'runoffs'. The post asks whether geographic proximity, time zone, or submission timing might silently influence who gets assigned where — especially for researchers in Europe or Asia who face higher travel costs and visa hurdles.

The official NeurIPS blog confirms the three-site model and acknowledges registration complexity, but offers no public criteria for how authors are matched to venues. There’s no published policy on waitlists, reassignments, or fallback options if a preferred site fills up. That silence leaves room for speculation — and anxiety — particularly among early-career researchers whose first NeurIPS presentation could hinge on a lottery-like allocation rather than merit or need.

What’s at stake isn’t just convenience. Conference participation shapes career trajectories: invitations to workshops, informal collaborations, and even job opportunities often flow from hallway conversations and live Q&A sessions. If those interactions are unevenly distributed across venues — or if remote attendees receive lower-quality streaming, fewer live interactions, or delayed access to recordings — the multi-venue experiment risks deepening existing inequities in AI research participation. As NeurIPS grows, its ability to balance scale with fairness will define its credibility far beyond 2026.
