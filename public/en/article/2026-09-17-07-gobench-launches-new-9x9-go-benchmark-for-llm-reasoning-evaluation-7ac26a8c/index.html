GoBench launches new 9x9 Go benchmark for LLM reasoning evaluation

News time: 2026-09-17T06:07:11.540926+00:00 · Source: reddit

A new open-source benchmark called GoBench is pushing the frontier of how we evaluate large language models’ abstract reasoning — not through logic puzzles or coding tasks, but by having them play 9x9 Go against a calibrated ladder of KataGo opponents. Unlike generic benchmarks that rely on multiple-choice accuracy or synthetic prompts, GoBench measures real-time strategic decision-making: each LLM must generate valid Go moves in sequence, respond to opponent actions, and adapt over the course of a full game. Performance is scored using Elo ratings derived from win rates across hundreds of games — ranging from random-move baselines up to superhuman KataGo versions trained on millions of self-play games.

The benchmark’s design reflects deep domain awareness. By fixing the board size at 9x9 — small enough for fast evaluation yet rich enough to capture combinatorial depth — GoBench avoids the computational intractability of full 19x19 games while preserving core reasoning challenges like life-and-death reading, influence estimation, and long-horizon planning. Crucially, it correlates strongly with ARC-AGI 2 (r = 0.83), a leading human-aligned reasoning benchmark focused on generalization from minimal examples. This suggests GoBench isn’t measuring narrow Go knowledge, but rather transferable logical and spatial reasoning.

Early results reveal both promise and room for growth. GPT-6 Astra max scores 2500 Elo — solidly above amateur human players but far below KataGo’s 4400+ Elo ceiling. Even with coding tools and two hours of pre-evaluation preparation, Codex + Astra reaches only 3560 Elo. That gap — and the fact that no model has saturated the top tier — signals that GoBench remains highly discriminative and scalable for tracking progress. The live leaderboard, open-source code, and peer-reviewed paper make it immediately usable by researchers and developers alike.

What makes GoBench especially valuable is its grounding in a real, well-understood game with objective outcomes. Go has no hidden rules or subjective scoring — every move is verifiable, every win/loss unambiguous. As LLMs increasingly integrate tool use and multimodal reasoning, benchmarks like GoBench offer a rare combination of rigor, interpretability, and ecological validity. It doesn’t just ask what an LLM knows — it asks how it thinks, step by step, under constraint.
