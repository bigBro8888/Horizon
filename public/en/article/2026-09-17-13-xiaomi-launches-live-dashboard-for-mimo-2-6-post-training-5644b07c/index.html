Xiaomi launches live dashboard for Mimo 2.6 post-training

News time: 2026-09-17T06:07:11.316867+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Xiaomi has opened a new window into how it builds and refines its large language models — by launching a public, live dashboard for the reinforcement learning (RL) post-training phase of its Mimo 2.6 model. Unlike typical closed-loop AI development, where training metrics remain internal, Xiaomi now streams real-time reward curves, evaluation scores, and convergence signals directly to developers and researchers at mimo.xiaomi.com/rl/. This isn’t just telemetry: it’s an invitation to observe, understand, and even inform how the model evolves after initial pretraining.

The dashboard reflects Xiaomi’s growing commitment to transparency in industrial-scale AI. While OpenAI and Anthropic keep their fine-tuning pipelines tightly guarded, Xiaomi is treating RL alignment as a collaborative process — one that benefits from external scrutiny and community input. The move aligns with Xiaomi’s broader strategy of embedding Mimo across its ‘Human × Car × Home’ ecosystem, where reliability, cost efficiency, and iterative responsiveness matter more than theoretical benchmarks alone.

Early adopters are already putting Mimo 2.5 and early 2.6 variants to work. One software engineer reported using MiMo-V2.5 for daily development tasks, praising its intelligence-to-cost ratio — calling it comparable to late-2024 Anthropic models but at a fraction of the price. Others noted practical strengths: strong code reasoning, reasonable architectural suggestions, and fast iteration cycles. But they also flagged consistent limitations: occasional hallucinations, difficulty juggling multiple concurrent tasks, and context retention gaps — issues the live dashboard may help diagnose and prioritize.

What makes this especially notable is timing and openness. Xiaomi released Mimo-7B in April 2025, and the model is now available via API and vLLM-compatible deployments. The live RL dashboard doesn’t replace traditional evaluation, but it adds a layer of operational visibility previously reserved for internal engineering teams. For open-model ecosystems, it sets a new precedent: not just releasing weights, but revealing how those weights improve — day by day, reward by reward.
