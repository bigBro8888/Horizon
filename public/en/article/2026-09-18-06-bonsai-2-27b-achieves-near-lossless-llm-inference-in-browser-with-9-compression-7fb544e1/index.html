Bonsai 2 27B achieves near-lossless LLM inference in browser with 9× compression

News time: 2026-09-18T05:53:30.336347+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Prism ML has released Bonsai 2 27B — a highly optimized 27-billion-parameter large language model that delivers performance nearly indistinguishable from its full-precision counterpart, yet fits into just ~1.2 GB of storage. This is accomplished through a novel combination of ternary weight quantization (−1, 0, +1) and group-wise FP16 scaling, yielding an effective bit-width of only 1.76 bits per weight. The result is a model that runs efficiently not only on consumer laptops but even entirely within modern web browsers — a milestone previously reserved for models under 3B parameters.

Unlike standard quantization methods like Q2_K or Q3_K used in llama.cpp, Bonsai 2 employs a custom ternary scheme where most weights are reduced to three discrete values, drastically cutting memory footprint while preserving critical signal structure via carefully calibrated FP16 group scalars. Each weight group — typically 128 elements — gets its own FP16 scale factor, allowing fine-grained compensation for outliers without reintroducing full floating-point overhead. This approach avoids the sharp accuracy drop seen in aggressive binary or ultra-low-bit quantizations.

The model is distributed in GGUF format and requires Prism’s customized fork of llama.cpp to load and run — a detail emphasized by early adopters on Hacker News. Users report successful local inference on M-series Macs and even browser-based execution via Hugging Face Spaces, though some note degradation on long-context or reasoning-heavy tasks. Still, the fact that a 27B model can operate client-side without GPU acceleration marks a meaningful shift toward truly decentralized AI.

What makes Bonsai 2 especially notable is its pragmatic balance: it doesn’t chase theoretical minimalism (like 1-bit BitNet), nor does it rely on hardware-specific kernels or proprietary runtimes. Instead, it builds on widely adopted tooling — GGUF, llama.cpp, WebAssembly — and extends them with targeted, open innovations. That accessibility, combined with empirical near-lossless behavior across common benchmarks, positions it as a compelling option for developers building privacy-first or offline-capable LLM applications.
