Ignoring LLMs today is like ignoring Jurassic Park

News time: 2026-09-19T05:49:40.950842+00:00 · Source: rss

Simon Willison’s brief but arresting note from 18 September 2026 delivers a single, potent analogy: refusing to engage with large language models (LLMs) as a computer scientist today is akin to a geneticist turning away from a newly opened Jurassic Park. It’s not a technical analysis or a feature comparison — it’s rhetorical urgency distilled into two sentences.

The Jurassic Park reference isn’t whimsical; it’s deliberately loaded. In the original story, scientists resurrect extinct life not just as a curiosity, but as an irreversible, ecosystem-altering event — one demanding attention, responsibility, and rapid learning. Willison implies that LLMs have crossed a similar threshold: they are no longer speculative tools but live, deployed, rapidly evolving systems reshaping software development, education, research, and creative work.

What makes the analogy resonate is its emphasis on inattention as professional negligence. A geneticist who ignored Jurassic Park wouldn’t be exercising healthy skepticism — they’d be forfeiting their role in understanding, governing, and responding to a world-altering phenomenon. Likewise, Willison suggests that technologists who dismiss LLMs as hype, overhyped, or irrelevant risk falling behind not just in capability, but in ethical stewardship and practical relevance.

This note arrives amid accelerating real-world adoption: LLMs now power code autocompletion in major IDEs, underpin government chatbots, drive scientific literature synthesis, and serve as foundational infrastructure for startups. The field isn’t waiting for consensus — it’s iterating in public, shipping daily, and embedding itself in workflows. Willison’s point isn’t that everyone must build LLMs, but that everyone in computing must reckon with them — understand their limits, track their evolution, and participate in shaping how they’re used. That reckoning begins with attention — not awe, not fear, but informed, sustained engagement.
