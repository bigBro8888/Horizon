Samsung to more than double HBM4 and HBM4E production next year

News time: 2026-09-21T06:17:03.389330+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Samsung has announced plans to more than double its output of HBM4 and HBM4E high-bandwidth memory chips in 2025 — a decisive move aimed squarely at alleviating the most acute bottleneck in today’s AI hardware supply chain. Unlike logic chip fabrication, which faces well-documented constraints from EUV lithography shortages and export controls, memory — specifically HBM — has quietly emerged as the limiting factor for AI accelerator deployment, especially among Chinese vendors like Huawei.

This shift underscores a broader industry realization: while advanced logic nodes grab headlines, the real scaling wall lies in packaging, interconnects, and memory bandwidth. HBM4, standardized by JEDEC in April 2025, delivers up to 2 TB/s per stack using a 2048-bit interface and supports capacities up to 64 GB via dense 24Gb or 32Gb DRAM dies. Its enhanced variant, HBM4E, targets extreme computing workloads — such as frontier LLM training — with further optimizations in thermal resistance, power delivery, and signal integrity.

Samsung’s ramp isn’t just about volume; it reflects deep integration of process innovations. The company’s HBM4 uses 1c (one-chip) DRAM architecture, low-voltage TSV (Through-Silicon Via) I/O, and an advanced Power Distribution Network (PDN), yielding ~40% better energy efficiency and 30% improved heat dissipation over prior generations. These aren’t incremental tweaks — they’re foundational enablers for stacking more layers, sustaining higher bandwidth, and managing thermals in tightly packed AI accelerators.

The implications extend far beyond Samsung’s balance sheet. As community commenters observed, Huawei’s Ascend AI chips are reportedly constrained not by processor yield or lack of ASML tools, but by CXMT’s limited HBM capacity — highlighting how memory supply chains now dictate AI hardware timelines. Meanwhile, questions linger: Will even doubled HBM4 output satisfy AI’s exponential memory appetite? And what does this mean for consumer DRAM pricing, already under pressure from AI-driven demand diversion? For now, one thing is clear — the race for AI supremacy has pivoted from transistors to stacks.
