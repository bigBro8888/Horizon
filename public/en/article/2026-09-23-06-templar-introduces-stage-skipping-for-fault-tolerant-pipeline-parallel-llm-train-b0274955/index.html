Templar introduces stage skipping for fault-tolerant pipeline-parallel LLM training

News time: 2026-09-23T05:56:01.597156+00:00 · Source: reddit

Templar’s Crucible platform has introduced a novel technique called stage skipping to maintain training progress when individual pipeline stages fail during large-scale LLM pre-training. Unlike traditional fault-tolerance approaches that pause or restart training upon worker failure, Crucible allows healthy stages to continue forward and backward passes by dynamically bypassing the failed stage—effectively omitting its computation for several global steps. This keeps tokens flowing through the pipeline instead of stalling, preserving throughput and model convergence under realistic failure conditions.

Crucible combines stage skipping with two complementary techniques: SparseLoCo and pipeline compression. SparseLoCo is a communication-efficient algorithm that uses Top-k sparsification and 2-bit quantization to reduce inter-replica update traffic—critical when scaling across many replicas. Pipeline compression, meanwhile, reduces activation and gradient sizes exchanged between consecutive stages within a replica. Together, these methods lower bandwidth pressure while enabling graceful degradation during failures.

In simulations using a 178M-parameter model, eight data-parallel replicas, and four pipeline stages per replica, Crucible sustained validation loss within ~0.5% of the no-failure baseline—even when each replica had a 1% chance per global step of losing one stage for six consecutive steps. Notably, fixed projection layers shared across transformer blocks further improved robustness: they appear to align representations across stage boundaries, making skipped-stage bypasses less disruptive to learning dynamics. While this alignment effect remains a hypothesis, empirical results strongly support it.

Importantly, this work simulates learning resilience, not infrastructure recovery—it does not measure hardware replacement time or cloud cost savings. Rather, it demonstrates that training can remain stable even on volatile compute pools like spot instances or preemptible workers. For practitioners building production LLM training systems, stage skipping offers a pragmatic path toward higher resource utilization without sacrificing model quality.
