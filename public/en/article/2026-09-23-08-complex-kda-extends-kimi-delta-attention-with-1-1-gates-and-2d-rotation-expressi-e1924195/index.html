Complex KDA extends Kimi Delta Attention with [-1,1] gates and 2D rotation expressivity

News time: 2026-09-23T05:56:01.597016+00:00 · Source: reddit

Researchers have introduced Complex Kimi Delta Attention (CKDA), a theoretically grounded enhancement to the recently proposed Kimi Delta Attention (KDA) architecture. Unlike standard KDA—which builds on Gated DeltaNet (GDN) by refining head-wise decay into channel-wise gating—CKDA modifies two core parameters: it expands the diagonal gate’s output range from [0,1] to [-1,1], and lifts the delta rule learning rate from [0,1] to [0,2]. These seemingly small changes unlock expressive 2D orthogonal rotations in a single step, enabling CKDA to represent any orthogonal matrix that is the sum of a diagonal matrix and a rank-one update.

This expressivity isn’t just abstract: the authors connect it directly to symmetry groups central to structured reasoning—specifically S3 (the symmetric group on 3 elements) and S4—and even the alternating group A5. Their analysis shows CKDA can track these groups during training, meaning its internal dynamics respect their algebraic structure, though it cannot fully represent S5 due to dimensionality constraints. Such group-aware modeling is rare in linear attention and suggests CKDA may better capture compositional or relational patterns in sequential data.

Empirically, CKDA demonstrates stable training and competitive performance across diverse tasks. On audio continuation—where temporal structure and phase-sensitive transformations matter—it achieves promising results, likely benefiting from its ability to model signed, rotation-like updates. In language modeling, it matches standard KDA despite its more complex gating, confirming that the theoretical gains don’t come at a stability or efficiency cost. The architecture remains compatible with Kimi-Linear’s hybrid design, which interleaves KDA layers with full latent attention for balanced local-global processing.

What makes CKDA notable is its tight integration of abstract algebra and practical deep learning. Rather than treating attention as a black-box mixer, the work exposes how gate design choices map onto concrete mathematical objects—orthogonal matrices, symmetry groups, invariant manifolds. This bridges longstanding ideas from dynamical systems and Noether-inspired learning theory with modern sequence modeling. For practitioners, CKDA offers a drop-in upgrade path for KDA-based models; for theorists, it provides a testbed for studying how architectural priors shape learned representations.
