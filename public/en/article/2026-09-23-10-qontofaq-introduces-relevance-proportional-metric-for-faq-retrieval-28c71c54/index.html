QontoFAQ introduces relevance-proportional metric for FAQ retrieval

News time: 2026-09-23T05:56:01.597208+00:00 · Source: reddit

Information retrieval (IR) benchmarks have long struggled to reflect real-world usage—especially in customer-facing applications like FAQ search, where users expect the right answer, not just a list of vaguely relevant documents. Qonto, a European fintech company, has now released QontoFAQ: a new open benchmark designed specifically to evaluate how well embedding models retrieve answers to realistic product questions. Unlike traditional IR benchmarks that rely on binary relevance judgments or top-K precision, QontoFAQ uses a relevance-proportional metric—one that assigns partial credit based on how closely a retrieved document matches the user’s intent, acknowledging that some answers are more useful than others, even if not perfect.

The dataset behind QontoFAQ is curated from Qonto’s own public FAQ documentation, covering real user questions about banking features, account management, and compliance. Each query is paired with human-annotated relevance scores across multiple candidate answers—not just ‘relevant’ or ‘not relevant’, but graded on a scale reflecting practical utility. This mirrors how developers actually use retrieval in production systems, such as Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), where downstream LLMs depend on high-fidelity context. The benchmark also includes standardized evaluation code, baseline results for popular embedding models (e.g., OpenAI text-embedding-3-small, BGE-M3), and reproducible pipelines—all publicly available on GitHub.

What sets QontoFAQ apart is its grounding in applied needs. Rather than optimizing for leaderboard scores, the team started from a concrete engineering goal: “finding the article that answers a product question right.” That focus led them to rethink evaluation itself—moving away from metrics that reward volume or position alone (like Recall@10 or MRR) toward one that better reflects informational adequacy. Their relevance-proportional approach calculates score contributions weighted by annotation grades, making it sensitive to both ranking order and answer quality.

While still early, QontoFAQ offers a compelling alternative to widely used benchmarks like BEIR or MS MARCO, which often rely on sparse or synthetic relevance labels. Its emphasis on graded, domain-specific relevance—and its open, transparent design—makes it especially valuable for teams building financial, legal, or technical support tools where factual accuracy and user trust are non-negotiable.
