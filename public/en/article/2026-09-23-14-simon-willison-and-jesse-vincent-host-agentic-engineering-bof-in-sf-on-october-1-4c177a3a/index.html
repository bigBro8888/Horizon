Simon Willison and Jesse Vincent host agentic engineering BoF in SF on October 14

News time: 2026-09-23T05:56:01.182115+00:00 · Source: rss

On the evening of Wednesday, October 14, 2026, Simon Willison — known for his influential writing on web development and AI tooling — and Jesse Vincent, a veteran open-source engineer and co-creator of RT ticketing system, will host an informal Birds of a Feather (BoF) session in San Francisco. The event is explicitly not a conference talk or product launch. Instead, it’s designed as a low-pressure, high-curiosity gathering for people actively experimenting with coding agents: AI systems that write, test, debug, and refactor code autonomously or semi-autonomously.

The focus is squarely on early-stage, unpolished work — the kind rarely seen at industry conferences. Attendees are invited to share odd experiments, half-baked prototypes, or unfinished tools that don’t yet have a clear market fit. As Willison puts it: “This agentic AI stuff is weird! Let's celebrate and lean into that weirdness.” There will be no slides, no sales pitches, and no expectation of polished demos — just one flowing, collaborative conversation grounded in shared curiosity and mutual learning.

What makes this BoF distinctive is its emphasis on agentic engineering rather than just agent usage. Drawing from emerging definitions, agentic engineering treats AI agents not as black-box assistants but as programmable, composable components in a software development pipeline — orchestrated by humans who set goals, define constraints, validate outputs, and iterate on agent behavior. This framing shifts attention from ‘Can it code?’ to ‘How do we design, debug, and govern agent workflows reliably?’

The event reflects a broader trend: as foundation models mature, the frontier is moving from prompting to orchestration. Developers are increasingly asking how to chain agents, handle failures gracefully, manage state across steps, and integrate human-in-the-loop validation — questions that remain poorly documented and highly contextual. By creating space for candid discussion of what doesn’t work — and why — this BoF serves as a rare real-world feedback loop for the nascent discipline of agentic engineering.
