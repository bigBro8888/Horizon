TikTok creator spotlights AI scripts’ lack of voice and opinion

News time: 2026-09-23T05:56:01.182218+00:00 · Source: rss

A TikTok creator known as @therealcornpop has distilled a widely felt intuition into a crisp, memorable critique: AI-generated video scripts often fail not because of clumsy phrasing or clichéd structures—but because they sound fundamentally voiceless. In a viral observation shared on Simon Willison’s blog, the creator points to telltale rhetorical patterns—like overused framing devices (“It’s not X, it’s Y”), rigid adherence to the rule of three, or staccato bursts of punctuated fragments—that mimic depth without substance. But those are just surface symptoms.

What truly gives AI content away, according to @therealcornpop, is its emotional and intellectual emptiness: the absence of a coherent personal stance, the lack of conviction, and the erasure of subjective perspective. Unlike human creators who speak from experience, bias, passion, or even contradiction, AI scripts tend toward neutral, consensus-driven blandness—even when dressed up with dramatic pauses or emphatic punctuation. This isn’t about grammar or syntax; it’s about rhetorical presence—the sense that someone stands behind the words.

The insight lands with particular force in short-form video, where authenticity functions as both aesthetic and currency. On TikTok and YouTube Shorts, viewers rapidly assess credibility, relatability, and intent—not through credentials, but through tonal cues: hesitation, emphasis, irony, digression, or even self-correction. An AI script may tick every structural box, yet still feel hollow because it avoids risk, stakes, or specificity. As @therealcornpop puts it: “It’s the lack of anything”—a vacuum where voice and opinion should reside.

This observation doesn’t require technical expertise to grasp, yet it cuts deep into current debates about AI literacy and platform ethics. Educators, content moderators, and platform designers increasingly need accessible heuristics—not just detection tools—to recognize AI misuse in real time. And for creators, the takeaway is both cautionary and empowering: cultivating a distinctive voice isn’t just stylistic preference—it’s a functional defense against algorithmic homogenization and audience skepticism.
