Play poker and Diplomacy against frontier AI models with live chat

News time: 2026-09-23T05:56:01.596942+00:00 · Source: reddit

A new interactive platform lets users play classic social strategy games — including poker, Risk, and Diplomacy — not just against AI opponents, but with them: players can converse in natural language, negotiate alliances, bluff, revise plans mid-game, and observe how the AI adapts its behavior in response. Unlike traditional game AIs that rely on hardcoded rules or fixed policy networks, this system appears to leverage large language models (LLMs) to simulate human-like reasoning, deception, and real-time strategic recalibration — especially valuable in games where success hinges less on calculation and more on reading intent, building trust, and breaking it.

Diplomacy, for instance, is uniquely demanding: a seven-player board game set in pre-WWI Europe where victory depends entirely on negotiation, coalition-building, and betrayal — no dice, no randomness, just pure social maneuvering. For decades, it has served as a benchmark for multi-agent strategic reasoning. Similarly, poker introduces hidden information and probabilistic bluffing, while Risk layers territory control with diplomacy and risk assessment. The platform supports both solo play and multiplayer modes, letting humans team up or compete alongside AI agents that respond conversationally rather than through static move menus.

What makes this notable isn’t just the game selection, but the integration of LLM-driven dialogue as a first-class game mechanic. Recent research — such as the 2024 arXiv study 'The Emergence of Strategic Reasoning of Large Language Models' — confirms that while LLMs show emerging capabilities in simple game-theoretic settings, they still struggle with long-horizon planning, consistent identity maintenance, and robust counterfactual reasoning in adversarial multi-agent contexts. This platform offers a public-facing testbed for those limitations: users aren’t just observing AI moves — they’re probing them through speech, testing coherence, memory, and adaptability under pressure.

Still, the Reddit post gives no technical details: no model names, no architecture choices, no latency metrics, and no clarity on whether the AI generates full turn orders autonomously or relies on human-in-the-loop adjudication. It’s positioned as fun and accessible — not a research demo — which may limit its utility for developers but broaden its appeal to curious players. As LLMs increasingly enter collaborative and competitive social spaces, tools like this help bridge the gap between theoretical reasoning benchmarks and tangible, human-paced interaction.
