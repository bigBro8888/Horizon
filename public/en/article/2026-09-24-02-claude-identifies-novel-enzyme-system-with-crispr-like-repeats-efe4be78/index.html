Claude identifies novel enzyme system with CRISPR-like repeats

News time: 2026-09-24T06:08:52.425525+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Anthropic has announced that its AI assistant Claude, while analyzing raw bacterial genomic data, independently identified a previously uncharacterized genetic architecture: a retron-like reverse transcriptase gene flanked by tandem DNA repeats strikingly similar to CRISPR arrays. This discovery was not the result of targeted database mining or fine-tuned bioinformatics pipelines—but emerged from Claude’s open-ended exploration of sequence context, guided only by high-level biological priors about defense systems and nucleic acid editing.

The finding centers on a genomic locus where Claude visually recognized repetitive patterns adjacent to a known class of reverse transcriptases—enzymes typically associated with retrons, prokaryotic anti-phage elements that produce multicopy single-stranded DNA (msDNA). Unlike canonical CRISPR-Cas systems—which use Cas proteins guided by RNA spacers to cut DNA—this new arrangement pairs repeat-spacer architecture with a reverse transcriptase, suggesting a potential mechanism for RNA-templated, programmable DNA modification without double-strand breaks.

Importantly, Anthropic stresses this is a computational hypothesis, not a validated biochemical system. No wet-lab experiments have yet confirmed enzymatic activity, editing efficiency, or cellular function. The team plans collaborative follow-up with molecular biologists to test whether the locus encodes a functional editor—perhaps one capable of precise, template-driven insertions or base conversions. If verified, it could expand the programmable genome editing toolkit beyond CRISPR, base editors, and prime editors.

Community response has been enthusiastic but rigorously skeptical. Some researchers caution that tandem repeats near RT genes are not unprecedented—and that identifying a genomic arrangement is distinct from proving a new editing mechanism. Others highlight the novelty of observing AI ‘reasoning’ in real time: Claude’s internal transcript reportedly included the exclamation, “I can see by eye a tandem repeat array … that’s a CRISPR-like … repeat array?!”—a moment that underscores how LLMs may now serve as pattern-intuition engines for early-stage discovery. Still, concerns persist about overinterpretation, safety implications, and the limits of language-based reasoning in structural biology.
