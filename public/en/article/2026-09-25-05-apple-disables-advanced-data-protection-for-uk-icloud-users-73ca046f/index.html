Apple disables Advanced Data Protection for UK iCloud users

News time: 2026-09-25T06:08:33.874949+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Apple has withdrawn Advanced Data Protection (ADP) for iCloud users in the United Kingdom, reverting non-baseline data categories—including iCloud Backup, Photos, Notes, and iCloud Drive—to Standard Data Protection. Under this default setting, Apple holds the encryption keys and can comply with lawful requests from UK authorities. The move follows a legal demand that would have required Apple to modify ADP’s underlying security architecture—a change Apple declined to make. Instead of building a backdoor or weakening encryption globally, Apple chose to discontinue ADP entirely for UK users.

ADP, introduced in 2022, extends end-to-end encryption (E2EE) to 23 iCloud data categories—up from the baseline 14 (e.g., Keychain, Health, Messages) that are E2EE by default. For UK users, those 14 remain protected, but the additional nine categories now fall under Apple’s control. This creates what critics call a 'two-tier' encryption system: one level where users retain full cryptographic control, and another where Apple retains key access and legal responsiveness.

The decision highlights a growing tension between platform security commitments and national regulatory pressure. Unlike its 2016 public stand against the FBI over iPhone encryption, Apple did not litigate this UK request. Instead, it opted for a jurisdiction-specific feature rollback—a pragmatic but privacy-eroding compromise. Analysts note that while no user data was directly handed over, the structural shift lowers the barrier for future access and sets a precedent for other governments to demand similar concessions.

Critics argue this undermines trust in Apple’s privacy branding. As one commenter observed, once a company accepts regulatory entry through the front door—even via KYC prompts or age gates—it becomes harder to resist deeper intrusions later. The UK’s Online Safety Act and broader law enforcement powers appear to have nudged Apple toward a model where encryption strength depends on geography, not principle. For users who chose Apple for its privacy stance, the message is clear: your security posture is now subject to local law—not global design.
