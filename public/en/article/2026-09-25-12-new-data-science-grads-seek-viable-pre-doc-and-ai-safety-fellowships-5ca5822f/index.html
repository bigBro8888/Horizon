New data science grads seek viable pre-doc and AI safety fellowships

News time: 2026-09-25T06:08:34.108490+00:00 · Source: reddit

A recent data science graduate is navigating a confusing landscape of early-career research opportunities in AI—specifically, pre-doctoral residencies, industry research fellowships, and lesser-known programs that offer hands-on experience without requiring a PhD. The post, shared on r/MachineLearning, reflects growing uncertainty among new grads: many corporate AI residency pages (e.g., at major tech firms) haven’t been updated in months, raising concerns about program continuity or quiet discontinuation. Meanwhile, well-known pathways like the Allen Institute for Artificial Intelligence’s (Ai2) Predoctoral Young Investigators Program remain active and well-documented—but are relatively rare and highly competitive.

The poster explicitly names three high-profile options: Ai2’s pre-doc program, Anthropic’s Fellows Program for AI safety research, and the ML Alignment & Theory Scholars (MATS) fellowship. Each serves distinct goals—Ai2 emphasizes foundational research training with mentorship and real project involvement; Anthropic’s program focuses narrowly on safety-critical questions like agentic misalignment and jailbreak mitigation, offering six-month full-time funding and direct mentorship; MATS runs intensive 12-week cohorts pairing fellows with senior researchers across AI alignment, transparency, and security. All three are structured, time-bound, and application-driven—unlike traditional academic lab roles.

Beyond these, commenters surfaced several lower-profile but credible alternatives: the Center for Human-Compatible AI (CHAI) at UC Berkeley, the Decentralized AI Research (DAIR) Institute, and the Machine Learning Summer School (MLSS) Fellowships. These vary in scope—some are remote, some require affiliation with a university, and most emphasize open-source or public-interest AI work. Crucially, many are run by nonprofits or academic consortia rather than corporations, making them more resilient to market shifts.

What ties these programs together is their role as bridges: they fill the gap between undergraduate training and PhD admissions—or even serve as alternatives to grad school for those pursuing independent research careers. For candidates without publications or research labs on their CVs, such programs offer not just experience, but letters of recommendation, co-authored papers, and community access. As AI safety and alignment gain institutional attention, demand for these entry points is rising—even as corporate offerings become less transparent. The underlying message isn’t just ‘where to apply,’ but ‘how to build credibility when formal credentials are still in progress.’
