Tauon optimizer beats Muon and AdamW on GPT-Mini training

News time: 2026-09-27T06:35:07.344065+00:00 · Source: reddit

A new optimizer named Tauon has emerged from a solo developer’s experiment on Reddit, showing measurable improvements over two established baselines—Muon and AdamW—when training a small language model. On the TinyShakespeare dataset, Tauon achieved a lower final validation loss (~1.6) than Muon (~1.65) and AdamW (~1.8), while maintaining stable convergence across all 3000 training steps. In contrast, AdamW began diverging around step 1200, suggesting poorer generalization behavior under the same hyperparameters.

The design of Tauon builds on Muon’s core idea—using orthogonal matrix updates via polynomial spectral filtering—but introduces three key modifications: reducing the Newton-Schulz iteration count from three to two steps through coefficient scheduling; applying Discrete Cosine Transform type-2 (DCT-2) to compress the parameter matrix before orthogonalization; and leveraging polynomial approximations that trade off some theoretical rigor for faster per-step computation. These changes collectively cut step time by ~8.5% versus Muon (391.5 ms vs. 427.7 ms), bringing Tauon close to AdamW’s raw speed (382.9 ms) without sacrificing convergence quality.

The benchmark used GPT-Mini—a compact transformer with d_model=512 and six layers—trained entirely on Kaggle’s free T4 GPU, which limited runtime to just two hours. That constraint explains the narrow scope: no ablation studies, no theoretical analysis, and no testing on larger models or diverse tasks like vision or speech. Still, the implementation is open-source (PyPI and GitHub), inviting community replication and stress-testing. The author explicitly invites feedback and collaboration, acknowledging the preliminary nature of the results.

What makes Tauon notable isn’t just its empirical win on one setup—it reflects a growing trend in optimization research: moving beyond generic adaptive methods toward structured, spectrally-aware updates tailored to neural weight geometry. Unlike AdamW’s element-wise adaptation or even Muon’s full-matrix orthogonalization, Tauon attempts a middle path—compressing structure first (via DCT-2), then refining it efficiently. If validated at scale, this could influence how lightweight optimizers are designed for edge devices or rapid prototyping where both speed and stability matter.
