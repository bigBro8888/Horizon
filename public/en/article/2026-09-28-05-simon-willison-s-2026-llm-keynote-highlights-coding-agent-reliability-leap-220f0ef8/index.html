Simon Willison’s 2026 LLM keynote highlights coding agent reliability leap

News time: 2026-09-28T06:48:34.514489+00:00 · Source: rss

At the WeAreDevelopers World Congress North America in San Jose on September 25, 2026, Simon Willison delivered his highly anticipated annual keynote — '2026 in LLMs (so far)'. Known for distilling complex AI developments into clear, developer-centric insights, Willison used a chronological narrative to trace pivotal moments from early 2026 — and even back to November 2025 — framing them as inflection points rather than isolated releases.

The talk centered on a subtle but consequential shift: the transition of AI coding agents from experimental tools to reliable daily assistants. Willison pinpointed November 2025 as the unofficial start of 2026, citing the simultaneous release of Claude Opus 4.5 and GPT-5.1. Though marketed as incremental upgrades, these models crossed a practical threshold when integrated with their respective agent frameworks — Claude Code and Codex — moving from 'often make mistakes' to 'reliable enough for day-to-day use'. This wasn’t about raw benchmark scores; it was about real-world robustness in tasks like code generation, debugging, and iterative refinement.

Willison illustrated this evolution with his signature low-stakes, high-insight benchmark: asking models to generate an SVG of 'a pelican riding a bicycle'. Absurd by design, the prompt tests compositional reasoning, spatial understanding, and symbolic execution — all weak spots for earlier LLMs. His slides contrasted outputs from November 2025: both models produced broken bicycles and duck-like pelicans, confirming that visual-spatial reasoning remained immature. Yet the very fact that he still ran the test — and highlighted its continued failure — underscored how much else had improved: tool use, API orchestration, and long-horizon planning now worked well enough that developers could ignore the SVG flaws and ship real software.

Beyond benchmarks, Willison emphasized infrastructure shifts — notably the rise of lightweight, locally runnable coding agents powered by quantized 4-bit models fine-tuned for IDE integration. He also noted growing standardization around agent protocols like MCP (Model Communication Protocol), enabling interoperability between tools from different vendors. The closing message was pragmatic: 2026 isn’t about superintelligence, but about trust — the quiet, cumulative engineering that makes AI feel less like magic and more like a dependable colleague.
