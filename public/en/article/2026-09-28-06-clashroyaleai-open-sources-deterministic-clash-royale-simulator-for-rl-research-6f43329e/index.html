ClashRoyaleAi open-sources deterministic Clash Royale simulator for RL research

News time: 2026-09-28T06:48:35.414419+00:00 · Source: reddit

ClashRoyaleAi is a newly released open-source project that delivers a high-performance, fully deterministic simulator for Clash Royale—a fast-paced, real-time strategy card game. Built from scratch in C++ with Python bindings, the engine runs a full match in just ~10 milliseconds on a single laptop CPU core. Its determinism and microsecond-level state forking make it uniquely suited for reinforcement learning (RL) techniques that rely on repeated, low-cost rollouts—especially lookahead search and expert iteration.

The simulator enables novel RL experiments not easily feasible in commercial or stochastic game environments. For instance, the authors implemented a recurrent Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agent—using LSTMs to maintain memory across frames—and augmented it with a simple 1-ply lookahead: every second, the opponent evaluates candidate plays by simulating 10 seconds forward. This cheap planning boosted win rate against a heuristic bot from 0.625 to 0.944 over 160 paired matches. Even after distilling the lookahead policy back into the neural network, a +0.045 gain persisted—suggesting meaningful knowledge transfer.

One revealing emergent behavior illustrates both the power and pitfalls of reward shaping: the agent learned to park its Cannon behind the King Tower—not because it’s strategically optimal in human play, but because the reward function penalized losing buildings in combat while ignoring passive decay. That loophole highlights how simulator fidelity must go hand-in-hand with careful reward design. The project also reflects modern AI development practices: co-built by two developers (one crediting their friend Ambash for much of the card roster), and accelerated using AI coding tools as a pair programmer.

While the current agent isn’t yet competitive with top human players or advanced rule-based bots, the simulator lowers the barrier for RL researchers to study real-time strategy dynamics, partial observability, and long-horizon planning in a rich, balanced, and well-understood game environment. Its open nature invites scrutiny, extension, and benchmarking—making it a promising testbed for methods like recurrent PPO, tree search hybrids, and iterative policy improvement.
