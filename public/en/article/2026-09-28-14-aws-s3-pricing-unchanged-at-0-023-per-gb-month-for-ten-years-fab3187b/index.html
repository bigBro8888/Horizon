AWS S3 pricing unchanged at $0.023 per GB-month for ten years

News time: 2026-09-28T06:48:34.514509+00:00 · Source: rss

For over a decade, Amazon S3 has stood as the de facto standard for cloud object storage — simple, durable, and widely integrated. But a quiet shift has taken place: its price hasn’t moved since December 2016. That’s ten full years without a reduction, breaking a long-standing pattern of regular cuts that began with its 2006 launch at $0.150 per GB-month.

Before the freeze, S3 pricing dropped seven times in its first decade — sometimes by over 30% in a single announcement. The steepest cut came in April 2014, when AWS slashed the rate from $0.085 to $0.030 — a 65% drop in under two months. By late 2016, it settled at $0.023, where it remains today. No official explanation has been given for the halt, but the timing coincides with S3’s maturation: it had already achieved massive scale, near-universal adoption, and deep integration across AWS services and third-party tools.

This stagnation isn’t just a footnote in pricing history — it reflects broader dynamics in cloud economics. Early price reductions helped drive adoption and lock customers into the AWS ecosystem. Once entrenched, the incentive to cut further diminished, especially as margins on storage — already razor-thin — faced pressure from rising infrastructure costs and competition from newer, more specialized storage offerings. Meanwhile, alternatives like Backblaze B2 ($0.005/GB-month) and Wasabi ($0.0069/GB-month) have emerged, though they lack S3’s breadth of features, compliance certifications, and global reach.

What’s notable is how little attention this decade-long freeze has received. Unlike compute or database price changes — which often make headlines — storage pricing tends to fly under the radar, even though it compounds silently over time for large-scale users. For enterprises storing petabytes across years, a $0.023 rate may still be competitive in practice, but the absence of downward pressure signals a subtle but meaningful shift: S3 is no longer being priced to win new customers — it’s being priced to retain them.
