Qwen3-VL 8B beats GPT-5.6 on IRS tax forms but fails Indian date parsing

News time: 2026-09-29T06:51:52.966012+00:00 · Source: reddit

A hands-on benchmark conducted on a consumer laptop reveals surprising strengths and sharp limitations of Alibaba’s new multimodal model Qwen3-VL 8B Instruct. Running locally on an M5 MacBook with 24GB RAM—using Ollama and the Q4_K_M quantized version—the model was pitted against top cloud-based models: Claude Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5, and OpenAI’s GPT-5.6 Terra. The test set spanned 137 real-world messy documents: Indonesian and Malaysian receipts (CORD and SROIE), vintage scanned invoices from the 1980s–90s, freshly generated IRS tax forms (W-2s, not seen in any training data), synthetic Indian bank statements, and long CUAD legal contracts.

On overall accuracy—measured by fully correct document-level answers—Opus led at 89%, followed by Sonnet at 85%, while Qwen3-VL 8B and GPT-5.6 Terra tied near the bottom at 59% and 57%. But beneath that average lies a striking domain split: Qwen correctly parsed 21 of 32 IRS W-2 forms, vastly outperforming GPT-5.6 Terra’s 7/32. Yet on Indian bank statements, Qwen got every monetary value right—but misread all dd-mm-yyyy dates as mm-dd, yielding zero fully correct outputs. Similarly, on CUAD contracts, it succeeded on just 2 of 15, often hallucinating expiry dates or truncating due to token exhaustion.

The tester discovered a critical configuration pitfall: the default qwen3-vl:8b tag in Ollama loads the ‘thinking’ variant, which consumes all 4,096 context tokens on long contracts before returning any answer. Switching to :8b-instruct resolved this. Other unexpected findings included GPT-5.6 Terra’s aggressive spelling normalization (e.g., “Rachael” → “Rachel”)—a behavior absent in Qwen—and the futility of self-check prompts, which changed only 1 of 137 outputs. The author also flagged four likely errors in the widely used SROIE receipt benchmark’s official answer keys.

This isn’t just another leaderboard snapshot. It’s a granular, hardware-constrained, real-document stress test—one that exposes how multimodal reasoning breaks down not on complexity alone, but on cultural formatting conventions, OCR noise, and token management. The author plans to fine-tune the 8B model specifically on Indian date patterns and spelling variants, with results to be shared publicly. For developers building document AI tools, the takeaway is clear: local multimodal models are now viable for narrow, high-value tasks like US tax form parsing—but cross-regional robustness remains fragile, and prompt engineering alone won’t fix structural gaps.
