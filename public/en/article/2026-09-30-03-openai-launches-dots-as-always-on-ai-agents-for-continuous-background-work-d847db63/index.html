OpenAI launches Dots as always-on AI agents for continuous background work

News time: 2026-09-30T06:39:49.197869+00:00 · Source: hackernews

OpenAI has unveiled Dots — a new class of persistent, always-on AI agents designed to operate continuously in the background, not just respond to prompts. Unlike traditional chat interfaces or one-off API calls, Dots are meant to live alongside users: managing calendars, monitoring emails, updating spreadsheets, or coordinating across tools — all without requiring manual activation. Each Dot runs on its own cloud-based compute environment with browser access and long-term memory, enabling it to maintain context, learn from interactions, and act autonomously over time.

This marks a deliberate pivot from OpenAI’s earlier focus on conversational LLMs toward infrastructure-grade agent systems. Dots integrate natively with platforms like Gmail, Slack, Notion, and GitHub — suggesting an ambition to embed deeply into daily workflows rather than remain a standalone assistant. The system appears built for delegation: users assign high-level goals (e.g., 'keep my project timeline updated'), and Dots break them down, execute steps, and report back — much like a remote human teammate with domain-specific training.

Critically, Dots are not just smarter chatbots. They rely on orchestration layers beyond raw model inference: persistent state, tool calling with authentication, error recovery, and observability — features highlighted in industry analyses as essential for production-grade always-on agents. According to OpenAI’s documentation, each Dot gets its own isolated cloud computer, supporting both deterministic execution and long-running tasks that span hours or days.

The launch arrives amid growing skepticism about platform lock-in. As commenters on Hacker News note, switching away from a Dot becomes harder than swapping models — because migration would require transferring not just prompts or outputs, but integrations, memory, history, and custom tool bindings. That raises real questions about vendor dependence, especially as competitors like Anthropic (with Claude Code) and Meta (with Muse) pursue similar visions — yet with different distribution strategies and business models.
