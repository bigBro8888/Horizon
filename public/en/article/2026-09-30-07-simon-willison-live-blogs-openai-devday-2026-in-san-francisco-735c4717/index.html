Simon Willison is on the ground at Fort Mason in San Francisco, covering OpenAI DevDay 2026 as it unfolds. Known for his incisive, developer-first commentary and meticulous real-time reporting, Willison has established himself as one of the most trusted independent voices in the AI community — especially when it comes to parsing major platform announcements. His live blog serves not just as a chronological feed, but as a running technical annotation: clarifying jargon, contextualizing product updates against prior releases, and flagging implications for builders working with APIs, agents, or fine-tuned models.

This year’s event marks OpenAI’s third annual DevDay — a flagship gathering aimed squarely at developers, API users, and technical founders. Unlike OpenAI’s broader consumer-facing launches, DevDay focuses on infrastructure, tooling, and integration patterns: think new model versions, updated rate limits, improved function calling, enhanced vision or reasoning capabilities, and deeper SDK support. Willison’s presence in the ‘creator’ section signals access to both the main keynote and likely breakout sessions or hands-on demos — giving readers unusually rich, grounded coverage.

Willison notes he received a complimentary ticket and dedicated seating from OpenAI, underscoring the company’s recognition of his influence and credibility among technical audiences. That relationship doesn’t compromise his editorial independence — his past DevDay coverage (including the widely cited 2025 live blog) consistently balances enthusiasm with critical scrutiny, calling out documentation gaps, latency issues, or undocumented behavior. Readers rely on him not just to report what was announced, but to help answer what it means for shipping code tomorrow.

While the initial post contains no actual product news — it’s a meta-announcement of coverage — its significance lies in signaling the start of what’s expected to be a high-impact event. With growing adoption of OpenAI’s models across startups and enterprises, even incremental improvements in reliability, cost, or tooling can shift engineering priorities across thousands of teams. Willison’s live blog will serve as a primary real-time lens for that impact — free, open, and technically literate.
