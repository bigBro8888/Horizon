Halfspace launches experimental web IDE for SDF-based solid modeling

News time: 2026-10-01T07:09:55.647170+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Halfspace is a new, open, browser-based integrated development environment designed specifically for solid modeling using signed distance fields (SDFs). Unlike traditional CAD tools that rely on boundary representations (B-rep) or mesh editing, Halfspace treats 3D geometry as mathematical functions — where every point in space evaluates to a signed distance from the nearest surface. This functional approach enables real-time, interactive manipulation: users write concise expressions (e.g., sphere(1) ∪ cube(2)), instantly see rendered previews, and compose complex shapes through union, intersection, and subtraction — all with live feedback and no compilation step.

The tool emphasizes accessibility for both designers and engineers by removing steep learning curves associated with parametric CAD or low-level shader programming. Its clean interface runs entirely in the browser, requires no installation, and supports keyboard-driven editing — making it ideal for rapid prototyping, teaching computational design, or exploring geometric logic. Under the hood, Halfspace leverages sphere tracing — an efficient ray-marching technique tailored for SDFs — to render high-fidelity, anti-aliased previews without relying on polygonal approximations.

Halfspace builds on years of research by creator Matt Keeter, whose doctoral thesis explored functional approaches to implicit modeling and verified compilation of geometric programs. The project reflects a broader shift toward declarative, composable tools in computational design — where geometry emerges from code rather than manual manipulation. It also aligns with growing interest in "mathematical clay" paradigms, where infinite-resolution implicit representations replace fixed-resolution meshes.

While still experimental and not yet production-ready for mechanical engineering workflows, Halfspace invites comparison with complementary tools like sdf2stl (a WebGL-based SDF editor focused on 3D printing export) and even conceptual neighbors like Mu — a safe, time-travel-debuggable stack built from machine code upward. Its emergence signals renewed momentum behind functional, interpretable, and pedagogically transparent systems for digital fabrication and geometric computing.
