ORTUS AI open-sources RightWayUp for robust 360-degree image rotation detection

News time: 2026-10-01T07:09:56.013536+00:00 · Source: reddit

ORTUS AI, a company specializing in video analytics, has released RightWayUp — a novel deep learning model designed to estimate the precise rotational orientation of images across the full 360-degree circle. The model was born from a practical need: automatically detecting whether CCTV cameras had been installed upside-down, tilted, or rotated — a surprisingly common issue in real-world surveillance deployments. Existing off-the-shelf rotation estimation models either delivered insufficient accuracy on camera-like scenes, generated too many false positives, or came with restrictive licenses incompatible with commercial video analytics pipelines.

RightWayUp stands out for its calibrated abstention behavior: rather than forcing a guess, it confidently declines to predict when an image lacks clear orientation cues — such as sky/ground horizons, architectural lines, or text — which commonly occurs in close-ups, abstract scenes, or uniform textures. The team trained and rigorously validated the model on diverse, held-out test sets, including real CCTV frames and benchmark data. Across six scalable variants — from Pico (lightweight enough for browser inference) to Max — the largest version achieved 93.0% accuracy within ±10° on unseen real-world images, outperforming Woehrer 2026 (88.4%). On RotBench, RightWayUp achieved perfect 100% accuracy.

A striking technical finding emerged during evaluation: JPEG compression artifacts unintentionally leaked rotation information. When the COCO-based benchmark images were saved at JPEG quality 90, Woehrer 2026’s accuracy plummeted from 98.0% to just 30.2%, while RightWayUp remained stable — suggesting it learned true geometric structure instead of relying on compression grid artifacts. The team explicitly mitigated this bias during training, making RightWayUp more generalizable to real-world image pipelines where compression is ubiquitous.

Released under the permissive Apache-2.0 license, RightWayUp includes PyTorch code, pre-trained weights, CLI tools, and a live demo video. It supports 1°-resolution estimation and integrates cleanly into video analytics workflows — especially for automated camera health monitoring, dynamic reorientation correction, and metadata enrichment. The project also transparently acknowledges early use of Claude and Codex for engineering assistance, underscoring how LLMs can accelerate applied ML development without compromising model integrity.
