Isolation Forest achieves 94% recall using full training samples on CICIDS2017

News time: 2026-10-01T07:09:56.013625+00:00 · Source: reddit

A practitioner training Isolation Forest for network intrusion detection on the CICIDS2017 dataset discovered an unexpected performance peak: setting max_samples=1.0 — meaning each isolation tree is built using the entire benign training set — yielded ~94% recall and only ~7.6% false positive rate. This result directly contradicts the widely cited recommendation in the original Isolation Forest paper and scikit-learn documentation, which suggest small fixed subsample sizes like 256 to ensure computational efficiency and statistical independence between trees.

The experiment used a realistic cybersecurity setup: only benign traffic (70% of total) was used for training, while validation included both benign and attack samples to tune hyperparameters and thresholds. The author tested eight values of max_samples, ranging from 256 up to the full dataset size (800,000+ samples), and found consistent improvement as the value increased — with 1.0 delivering the best trade-off on the ROC curve. Training time increased by just 30 seconds compared to max_samples=200_000, making the gain highly practical.

This finding highlights a critical nuance often overlooked in practice: the standard advice to use small max_samples assumes anomalies may be present in training data, where subsampling helps avoid contamination and masking effects. But in pure unsupervised anomaly detection — especially in cybersecurity, where training is intentionally anomaly-free — larger or even full-sample trees can better capture the true structure of normal behavior in high-dimensional network traffic.

The result did not generalize to the newer CSE-CIC-IDS2018 dataset, where performance remained poor regardless of max_samples. This suggests dataset-specific characteristics — such as feature distribution shifts, labeling inconsistencies, or higher noise — strongly influence hyperparameter sensitivity. The author also raised broader questions about training methodology: whether including anomalies in training (a common but controversial practice) or using different splits affects robustness — particularly given known issues like swamping and masking in high-dimensional anomaly detection.
