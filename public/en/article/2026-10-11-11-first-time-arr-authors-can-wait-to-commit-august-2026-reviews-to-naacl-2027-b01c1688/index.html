First-time ARR authors can wait to commit August 2026 reviews to NAACL 2027

News time: 2026-10-11T07:08:28.942940+00:00 · Source: reddit

A researcher preparing for NAACL 2027 has asked a practical question that many early-career NLP authors face: if they’ve already received completed reviews for their paper in the ACL Rolling Review (ARR) August 2026 cycle, do they need to take any immediate action to reserve those reviews for NAACL 2027 — or can they simply wait until the official NAACL commitment portal opens?

This is not a theoretical concern. Since 2023, major ACL-affiliated conferences — including NAACL, ACL, and EACL — have fully adopted ARR as their primary submission and review backbone. Under this system, papers are reviewed once by ARR, and authors later ‘commit’ those reviewed manuscripts to a specific conference venue. The reviews remain valid for six months from completion, meaning an August 2026 review stays usable through at least February 2027.

The key procedural point is timing. According to the official NAACL 2027 call for papers, authors submit first to ARR, then — during a designated commitment window — log into the ACL Member Portal to formally designate their paper for NAACL (or another eligible venue like COLING). No pre-commitment, flagging, or reservation step exists in the ARR interface itself. There is no ‘hold’ button or advance registration for future venues. As long as the review remains within its six-month validity window and the paper meets NAACL’s formatting and scope requirements, authors may commit it when the portal opens — typically several months before the final decision deadline.

Importantly, authors must choose only one primary venue at commitment time, and that choice is binding: switching from EACL to NAACL (or vice versa) after commitment is not permitted. So while no action is needed now, careful planning is essential once the NAACL commitment period begins — especially since overlapping deadlines with other ARR-supported conferences (e.g., EACL in early 2027) require deliberate prioritization. For first-time submitters, this clarity reduces anxiety and avoids premature or irreversible steps.
