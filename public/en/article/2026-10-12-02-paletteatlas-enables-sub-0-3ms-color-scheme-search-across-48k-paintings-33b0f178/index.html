PaletteAtlas enables sub-0.3ms color-scheme search across 48k paintings

News time: 2026-10-12T07:18:49.567605+00:00 · Source: reddit

PaletteAtlas is a new open-source tool that lets users search over 48,695 public-domain paintings by color scheme — not by keywords or artist names, but by the actual visual harmony of hues, lightness, and saturation. At its core lies a clever fusion of perceptual color science, optimal transport theory, and high-performance approximate nearest-neighbor (ANN) indexing. Instead of treating each painting as a raw image, PaletteAtlas first extracts its dominant palette: exactly 128 colors, each assigned equal weight — turning each artwork into a discrete probability distribution over color space.

To compare palettes meaningfully, the system avoids brute-force Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD), which would take ~6 ms per pairwise comparison and be infeasible at scale. Instead, it uses sliced Wasserstein distance — a computationally tractable lower bound on EMD — computed in the OKLab color space, which closely matches human perceptual uniformity. The embedding process projects each 128-color palette onto eight evenly distributed directions on a Fibonacci hemisphere, sorts the projected values along each direction, then compresses them into 16 quantiles per direction. The final 128-dimensional vector is compared using L1 distance — effectively averaging 1D Wasserstein distances across projections.

This embedding achieves 78% recall@10 against ground-truth EMD — far surpassing baselines like soft color histograms (45%) or mean color (3%). To accelerate search, PaletteAtlas implements a custom HNSW index from scratch with M=16 and efConstruction=200. Crucially, the top layer replaces random nodes with 8 k-means landmarks — one representative painting per major color family — preserving search speed while adding semantic interpretability. On average, queries require just 2,558 distance computations and return results in under 0.25 ms, achieving perfect recall@10 (1.000) at ef=32.

The project also reveals deeper insights: the intrinsic dimension of the embedded space is only ~11, confirming that hierarchical navigation remains effective despite low dimensionality — a nuance explored in recent work by Munyampirwa et al. (2025). All code, documentation, and a live demo are publicly available, making PaletteAtlas both a practical tool for designers and curators and a methodological reference for researchers working at the intersection of color perception, optimal transport, and efficient similarity search.
