Jebadiah v2.1 releases open-weight precision models for logit-based decision scoring

News time: 2026-10-12T07:18:49.567765+00:00 · Source: reddit

Jebadiah v2.1 introduces a new class of open-weight AI models explicitly designed not to generate text—but to score predefined answer options directly from model logits. Unlike standard large language models that autoregressively produce tokens and require post-hoc parsing or sampling, these 'precision' models treat decision tasks as closed-set classification: given structured context and a question with fixed choices (e.g., 'A. Yes / B. No / C. Unclear'), the model outputs calibrated probabilities for each option by extracting and normalizing logits corresponding to those label tokens. This eliminates ambiguity from tokenization mismatches, sampling noise, or output format errors—making inference faster, more deterministic, and easier to integrate into agent pipelines.

The release includes two sizes: a 27B-parameter and a 9B-parameter variant, both built on Qwen architecture and released under Apache-2.0 license. On the Decision Index 0.3 benchmark—a public test suite evaluating reasoning across Knowledge, Language, Retrieval, Arts, and Tools domains—the models show consistent gains: the 27B version improves overall score from 55.11 to 57.03 (+1.92), while the 9B jumps from 44.09 to 47.20 (+3.11). Gains are especially strong in Knowledge, Language, and Retrieval subtasks, though both models regress slightly on Tools—driven almost entirely by a sharp drop on the When2Call task (−6.38 and −7.44 points respectively). The author notes these deltas lack statistical significance claims and that the root cause remains under investigation.

Crucially, the evaluation methodology is fully transparent and self-run: all scores use the official Decision Index 0.3 scorer without modification, and results—including private test set submissions—were submitted to the benchmark’s public leaderboard. A contamination check found zero exact text matches between benchmark questions and training data, though paraphrased overlap isn’t ruled out. Quantization fidelity was also measured: the 27B Q8_0 quantized version agrees with full-precision weights on 259 of 260 held-out questions; the 9B MLX 4-bit version agrees on 237/260. Code, weights, and per-question evaluation records are publicly available on GitHub and Hugging Face.

This work signals a deliberate pivot away from generative defaults toward purpose-built decision infrastructure—where reliability, speed, and interpretability matter more than fluency. As agents increasingly rely on fast, low-variance routing and classification decisions—not storytelling—the Jebadiah paradigm offers a lightweight, auditable alternative to repurposing massive LLMs for narrow choice tasks.
