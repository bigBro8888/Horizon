Beyond All Reason launches open alpha as Total Annihilation spiritual successor

News time: 2026-10-12T07:18:48.588349+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Beyond All Reason (BAR), a polished, open-source real-time strategy game inspired by the legendary 1997 title Total Annihilation, has entered open alpha — marking a major milestone for retro RTS revival. Developed on the Spring Engine, BAR isn’t a remake or remaster, but a ground-up reimagining that honors TA’s design philosophy while embracing modern capabilities: cross-platform support (Windows, Linux, macOS), scalable multiplayer up to 150 players, and battles involving tens of thousands of units — all running smoothly thanks to engine optimizations and modern rendering pipelines.

The project traces its roots to Balanced Annihilation, a long-running TA mod focused on competitive balance, which itself evolved into BAR to pursue broader technical ambition and legal clarity. Unlike commercial successors such as Supreme Commander (also designed by TA’s original lead, Chris Taylor), BAR is fully community-driven, open-source, and intentionally distinct in assets and codebase — avoiding copyright entanglements while preserving TA’s core feel: asymmetric factions (ARM vs CORE), physics-based weapon impacts, terrain deformation, and macro-scale warfare where economy, scouting, and late-game superweapons define victory.

What sets BAR apart isn’t just scale — it’s polish. Players praise its quality-of-life features rarely seen in niche RTS titles: intuitive unit queuing, smart pathfinding, dynamic camera controls, and deeply customizable UIs. A recent 150-player YouTube demonstration shows stable performance during massive air battles with over 64,000 units — a feat unthinkable on the original TA engine or even early versions of Spring. Behind the scenes, developers have replaced legacy tooling with modern C++17, Vulkan rendering, and asynchronous networking, enabling consistent 60 FPS gameplay even in endgame scenarios running at 4× real-time speed against AI.

BAR also reflects a broader cultural shift: the preservation and evolution of classic game design through open collaboration. Its GitHub repository hosts over 2,000 commits from dozens of contributors; its Discord server sustains active discussion on balancing, modding, and engine improvements. While not officially endorsed by TA’s original creators, BAR enjoys tacit respect from veteran fans — and even draws comparisons to other ambitious revivals like Planetary Annihilation, whose team included former TA developers. For players who cut their teeth on 90s RTS epics, BAR isn’t nostalgia — it’s a living continuation.
