Looped Diffusion Transformer reuses transformer blocks per denoising step

News time: 2026-10-13T07:40:21.142593+00:00 · Source: reddit

A new architectural approach to text-to-image diffusion models—called the Looped Diffusion Transformer (Looped-DiT)—challenges the conventional wisdom that bigger models always yield better results. Instead of scaling up parameter count, Looped-DiT runs a shared set of transformer blocks multiple times within each denoising step, effectively making the model deeper at inference time without increasing its size. This looping is not trivial: early attempts failed due to unstable feature updates, but the authors solved this with two key innovations—deep supervision applied at every internal loop iteration, and self-modulating attention, which dynamically adjusts attention weights based on intermediate features to maintain stability and expressivity.

The paper (arXiv 2609.40305, released 30 September 2026) reports striking efficiency gains: a 260-million-parameter Looped-DiT outperforms a non-looped model 6.5× larger on standard image generation benchmarks—including FID, CLIP score, and human preference metrics—while using only 4.9× less inference compute. Crucially, loop depth—the number of times the shared blocks are reused per step—is fully configurable at sampling time, just like adjusting the number of denoising steps. This means users can trade off latency for quality on the fly: on an Apple M5 Pro, generating one image took ~23 seconds at loop depth 1 and ~45 seconds at depth 4, with diminishing returns beyond depth 3.

The open implementation (MIT-licensed, hosted on GitHub and Hugging Face by OpenSenseNova) enables immediate experimentation. A Reddit user tested it locally using PyTorch MPS backend, fixed Euler sampling (100 steps, CFG=6.0), and five diverse prompts—confirming visual quality improves sharply from depth 1 to 3, then plateaus between depths 3 and 4. The model was trained at depth 4, yet generalizes seamlessly to other depths without retraining—a strong sign of architectural robustness.

Beyond raw speed or size, Looped-DiT introduces a new design axis for generative models: computational depth per step. Unlike traditional models where depth is fixed at training time, Looped-DiT decouples model capacity from inference budget, enabling adaptive deployment across devices—from mobile chips to datacenter GPUs. It also echoes trends in efficient AI, such as recurrent-depth language models (e.g., RWKV, Mamba), suggesting a broader shift toward dynamic, compute-aware architectures in generative modeling.
