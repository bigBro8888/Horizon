U-Lens enables single-pass, label-free uncertainty diagnosis in reasoning LMs

News time: 2026-10-13T07:40:21.142825+00:00 · Source: reddit

When deploying reasoning-focused language models—like Qwen3.5 or Gemma 4—in real-world applications, developers often need to know how confident the model is in its answer. But existing methods are costly: multi-step sampling (e.g., 5–10 forward passes), external judge models, or supervised fine-tuning on labeled correctness data. U-Space introduces U-Lens, a lightweight, interpretable framework that delivers uncertainty signals in just one forward pass—no labels, no training, no resampling.

U-Lens works by projecting the final-layer hidden state (at end-of-thought) onto four precomputed, human-interpretable directions in a low-dimensional ‘U-Space’. These directions correspond to distinct uncertainty types: ambiguity (e.g., vague phrasing), missing information (e.g., insufficient facts), conflicting evidence (e.g., contradictory premises), and general uncertainty (low-confidence reasoning). The score combines this directional alignment with mean token entropy across reasoning tokens—both quantities already computed during standard inference.

Crucially, U-Lens relies on a Jacobian lens (J-Lens), a linear probe introduced by Anthropic that maps internal activations to their downstream verbalizable effects. Fortunately, over 40 pre-fitted J-Lenses—including for Gemma 2/3/4, Qwen3.5, Llama 3.1/3.3, and Magistral—are publicly available on Hugging Face. For unsupported models, fitting a new J-Lens takes ~100 unlabeled prompts and only backward passes—no parameter updates. Once the J-Lens is in hand, building the 4-vector U-Space basis requires only CPU computation and a few hundred unembedding rows.

In evaluations across three models (Gemma 4, Qwen3.5, Magistral) and four benchmarks—including TriviaQA—the U-Lens consistently outperformed multi-pass baselines (5- and 10-pass) and supervised uncertainty estimators. Its simplicity, speed, and interpretability make it especially valuable for production pipelines: routing low-confidence answers to larger models or humans, triggering retrieval when ‘missing info’ activates, prompting clarifying questions upon ‘ambiguity’, or flagging contradictions in RAG systems. While still research code—not yet a pip-installable library—the authors provide a ready-to-run smoke test and welcome integration help.
