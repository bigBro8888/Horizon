NeurIPS Paris pre-event launches workshop on physics-constrained ML models

News time: 2026-10-13T07:40:21.142987+00:00 · Source: reddit

A dedicated workshop titled 'Constrained Learning for Scientific Modelling' (CL4SM) will take place on December 8, 2026, at the Palais des Congrès de Paris — just before the main NeurIPS 2026 conference. Organized as part of the ELLIs UnConference, CL4SM invites researchers to submit work that ensures machine learning models obey fundamental physical principles: conservation of mass and energy, symmetry invariance, thermodynamic consistency, stability, and adherence to boundary conditions. The call highlights a growing pain point in scientific AI — models that fit observational data well but produce physically nonsensical outputs, such as negative concentrations or unbounded drift in dynamical simulations.

The workshop explicitly bridges theory and practice across multiple technical approaches. It welcomes contributions on soft constraint methods — like penalty terms or augmented loss functions — as well as hard enforcement strategies, including structure-preserving neural network architectures designed from first principles. Also in scope are differentiable optimization layers, projection-based correction mechanisms, and probabilistic frameworks that encode physical uncertainty in a scientifically coherent way. Topics span applications in climate modeling, fluid dynamics, energy systems, and industrial engineering — domains where model trustworthiness directly impacts safety, policy, and design decisions.

Submissions fall into three categories: ongoing research (including early-stage ideas and negative results), recently published papers from top venues like NeurIPS, ICML, JMLR, or IEEE TPAMI, and educational resources such as tutorials, open-source libraries, and benchmark datasets. All submissions are limited to two pages using the PMLR template and undergo single-blind review by the program committee. Crucially, the session is non-archival — meaning accepted work retains full publication rights elsewhere.

This initiative reflects a maturing phase in scientific machine learning. As physics-informed ML moves beyond proof-of-concept PINNs toward production-grade simulation tools, rigorous constraint integration is no longer optional — it’s foundational. CL4SM doesn’t just solicit incremental improvements; it calls for rethinking how ML layers interact with governing equations, symmetries, and domain-specific guarantees. For practitioners building surrogate models for high-stakes systems — from nuclear fusion control to atmospheric chemistry — this workshop signals both urgency and opportunity.
