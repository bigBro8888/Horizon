School builds AI system to generate exam questions from past papers

News time: 2026-10-13T07:40:21.143177+00:00 · Source: reddit

A high school developer has built a working prototype that uses large language models (LLMs) to generate new exam questions—tailored to the school’s specific curriculum—by analyzing its own archive of past papers and mark schemes. The system isn’t just prompting an LLM with raw text; it layers in structured guardrails: learning objectives from the school’s existing curriculum database guide question scope, and every generated item must pass human approval by a curriculum administrator before teachers can use it. This human-in-the-loop design reflects a pragmatic, safety-first approach to AI in assessment—one that avoids blind automation while still scaling content creation.

The pipeline starts with PDFs of past exams and answer keys. Because many science papers contain equations, subscripts, tables, and diagrams, standard PDF text extraction fails badly—dropping deltas (Δ), splitting chemical subscripts like H₂O into ‘H2O’, or scrambling table columns. To work around this, the developer currently feeds entire PDFs directly to the LLM, bypassing fragile text-layer parsing. But they’re seeking better alternatives: tools like Docling or Marker, which combine layout-aware OCR with semantic structure recovery, could preserve scientific notation and tabular integrity more reliably.

For question generation, a smaller, faster model drafts new items—MCQs, short answers, and lab-based scenarios—while a separate, stronger model acts as an automated grader, answering them “blind” (without seeing the answer key). MCQ answer keys are then validated programmatically against the original mark scheme—not trusted to the model—achieving ~80% pass rates. Structured questions remain trickier: early versions invented chemically implausible lab setups, but upgrading to a mid-size model significantly improved fidelity.

Beyond technical hurdles, the developer raises four strategic questions relevant to any edtech team building AI-assisted assessment: How to handle PDF parsing at scale? When does vector search (e.g., pgvector) become necessary for de-duplication and retrieval? What’s the real cost-quality tradeoff when generating thousands of questions? And most fundamentally—how do you measure whether an AI-generated question is pedagogically sound, not just factually correct? Their stack—NestJS, Postgres per tenant, self-hosted VPS—keeps sensitive student and exam data on-premises, sending only anonymized curriculum text and draft questions to external LLM APIs. It’s a thoughtful, grounded implementation where AI augments, rather than replaces, educator judgment.
