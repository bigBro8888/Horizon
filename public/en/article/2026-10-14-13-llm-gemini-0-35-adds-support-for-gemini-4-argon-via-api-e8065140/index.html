llm-gemini 0.35 adds support for Gemini 4 Argon via API

News time: 2026-10-14T06:06:19.192438+00:00 · Source: rss

Simon Willison has released llm-gemini 0.35, a CLI plugin that extends his popular LLM command-line tool to support Google’s newly launched frontier model, Gemini 4 Argon. The update arrives just days after Google made Argon publicly available — first to select cyber defenders via its Fairwind Program, then broadly to developers and enterprises. As a lightweight, open-source bridge to the Gemini API, llm-gemini lets users invoke Argon directly from the terminal or Python scripts, without building custom integrations.

Gemini 4 Argon is positioned as Google’s most capable reasoning model to date, optimized for long-horizon tasks in software engineering, legal analysis, finance, and cybersecurity. Unlike earlier Gemini versions, Argon emphasizes sustained logical depth and cache-aware inference — features reflected in its benchmark cost breakdown: $815 for reasoning, $897 for cache writes, and only $118 for non-cached input tokens. This architecture helps explain why Artificial Analysis’ total-cost benchmark ranks Argon at $2,407 — cheaper than GPT-6 Astra ($5,324), Claude Opus 5.5 ($8,708), and even GLM-5.3 ($2,503).

Pricing is time-sensitive: Argon launches at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens — half the current rate of Claude Opus 5.5 and one-fifth that of Claude Fable 5.1. But Google plans to double its price on December 1st, aligning it with Opus 5.5. Still, even post-increase, Argon remains more cost-efficient than GPT-6 Astra and several other top-tier models when measured by end-to-end benchmark cost.

The release also includes playful, SVG-based illustrations — dubbed 'low, medium and high pelicans' — generated by Argon itself. One 'high' pelican image (a stylish bicycle-riding bird with scarf and fierce expression) reportedly cost $0.17166, underscoring how token efficiency and multimodal fidelity intersect in real usage. While the blog post contains fragmented asides and placeholder-like phrasing, the technical substance — timely API integration, transparent pricing context, and empirical cost data — makes this a meaningful update for CLI-first AI practitioners.
