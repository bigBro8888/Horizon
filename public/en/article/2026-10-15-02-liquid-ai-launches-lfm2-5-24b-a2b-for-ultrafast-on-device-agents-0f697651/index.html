Liquid AI launches LFM2.5-24B-A2B for ultrafast on-device agents

News time: 2026-10-15T05:52:01.997551+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Liquid AI has unveiled LFM2.5-24B-A2B — its largest and most capable model to date — explicitly engineered for real-time, privacy-preserving agent workloads running directly on devices. Unlike conventional dense models of similar scale, this Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture activates only about 2 billion parameters per inference step out of its total 24 billion, enabling dramatic efficiency gains without sacrificing reasoning depth. The result is unprecedented throughput: up to 21,000 output tokens per second on a single NVIDIA B300 GPU — more than 1.7× faster than comparable MoEs in on-device settings.

The model is part of the expanding LFM2.5 family, which now spans two orders of magnitude in size — from the lightweight 230-million-parameter LFM2.5-230M to this flagship 24B variant. Its design prioritizes automation-specific intelligence: it excels on AutomationBench, a benchmark focused on real-world agent tasks like tool use, multi-step planning, and API orchestration — not just generic language fluency. This signals a strategic pivot toward purpose-built AI infrastructure for autonomous agents, rather than general-purpose foundation models.

What makes LFM2.5-24B-A2B especially notable is its hardware adaptability. It delivers 184 tokens per second even on an Apple M5 Max chip — a rare feat for a model of this capacity — suggesting strong cross-platform optimization across consumer-grade silicon. Community adoption is already underway: quantized versions are available via Unsloth, and integrations with Ollama and LM Studio make local deployment straightforward for developers. Still, licensing remains ambiguous — the model carries a non-commercial clause that some users find legally unclear.

Beyond raw speed, the release reflects a broader industry shift: moving beyond scaling laws alone toward structured efficiency. Rather than chasing ever-larger dense models, Liquid AI leverages sparse activation, hybrid routing, and hardware-aware compilation to compress compute cost while preserving capability. For developers building private, low-latency agents — whether for edge robotics, personal assistants, or enterprise automation — LFM2.5-24B-A2B sets a new practical benchmark for what’s possible on-device today.
