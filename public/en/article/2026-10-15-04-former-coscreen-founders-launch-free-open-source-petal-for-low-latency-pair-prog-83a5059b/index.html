Former CoScreen founders launch free open-source Petal for low-latency pair programming

News time: 2026-10-15T05:52:01.997641+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Jason Thomas, co-founder of the beloved but discontinued pair programming tool CoScreen, has unveiled Petal — a free, open-source successor designed to revive the magic of real-time, multi-cursor collaborative coding. Announced on Hacker News with deep personal resonance, Petal is built by four former CoScreen engineers who spent two months crafting what they call a ‘love letter’ to the developer community — one that breaks the industry’s grim pattern of acquisition and shutdown.

The story begins with ScreenHero: launched in 2013, it pioneered low-latency screen sharing with independent cursors, voice chat, and seamless IDE integration — until Slack acquired and shuttered it in 2017. CoScreen emerged in 2019 as its spiritual successor, emphasizing frictionless window-sharing across devices and persistent virtual rooms. After Datadog acquired the team in 2022, CoScreen continued as a standalone product for years — until its official sunsetting on July 31, 2024. Petal arrives not as a commercial pivot, but as a deliberate rejection of that cycle: no monetization, no corporate ownership, and full self-hosting capability from day one.

Technically, Petal mirrors CoScreen’s core UX: shared application windows appear as native, movable desktop windows (not tiled video grids), each participant gets a labeled cursor, and clicking into any shared window grants immediate keyboard and scroll control. It supports macOS and Windows natively, with Linux support actively in development. Media routing uses LiveKit for WebRTC-based real-time video and audio, but users can swap in their own LiveKit server or even alternative backends via configuration. The web app offers zero-config access, while advanced teams can keep all traffic internal using Docker Compose.

Remarkably, the first functional version of Petal was built in under eight weeks — a stark contrast to CoScreen’s six-year journey. The team credits modern AI-assisted development tools like Fable and Astra for accelerating UI and infrastructure work, though they stress that real-time window capture and synchronization remain among the most demanding systems programming challenges. They’ve pledged a forthcoming technical deep dive on what accelerated development — and what didn’t.
