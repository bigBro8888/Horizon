Mamba-3, RWKV-7 Goose, and BDH lead post-Transformer architecture shift in 2026

News time: 2026-10-15T05:52:02.419220+00:00 · Source: reddit

A comprehensive technical survey published on Reddit’s r/MachineLearning maps the rapidly diversifying landscape of post-Transformer neural architectures — not as replacements, but as purpose-built alternatives addressing core bottlenecks in reasoning, memory efficiency, and energy-constrained inference. The analysis covers nine prominent models: Mamba-3 (with complex-valued state and MIMO enhancements), RWKV-7 'Goose' (a fully parallelizable RNN), Dragon Hatchling (BDH) from Pathway, xLSTM from NXAI, Titans from Google Research, and hybrid variants like Zamba2, Jamba 2, and Nemotron-H. Rather than chasing scale alone, these designs prioritize how intelligence emerges — through selective state updates, dynamic Hebbian synapses, exponential gating, or surprise-driven memory framing.

The survey organizes models along two key dimensions: their core computational mechanism and their test-time memory behavior. For instance, Mamba-3 and BDH both use linear attention variants (via Selective State Space Models and local neuron graphs, respectively), yet differ sharply in memory dynamics — Mamba evolves a fixed-size state vector, while BDH implements biologically grounded, plastic synapses that adapt during inference. RWKV-7 ‘Goose’ stands out for eliminating the KV cache entirely, relying instead on constant-memory recurrent evolution — a trait shared with xLSTM’s matrix-based memory and Titans’ neural long-term memory updated only when prediction error exceeds a threshold.

Crucially, the comparison reveals significant architectural overlap: most hybrids (e.g., Falcon-H1, Zamba2, Jamba 2) mix Mamba-style SSM layers with traditional attention in fixed ratios — often 7:1 or 3:1 — suggesting industry consensus is forming around modular composition, not monolithic paradigms. Even hardware-aware designs like LFM2.5 (found via hardware-in-the-loop search) and StripedHyena 2 (using three distinct convolutional operators) reflect a broader trend: co-designing algorithms with silicon constraints in mind.

What unites these efforts is a shared rejection of the ‘bigger is always better’ scaling dogma. As the author notes, the next frontier lies in extracting more reasoning fidelity per watt and per millisecond — especially for real-time, on-device, or multi-step planning tasks. This shift signals maturation: instead of optimizing one architecture across all use cases, developers now select or compose mechanisms like building blocks — selective SSMs for long contexts, delta-rule updates for fast adaptation, or coupled recurrent modules (as in HRM-Text) for hierarchical planning. The result isn’t a single successor to the Transformer, but a richer, more specialized ecosystem.
