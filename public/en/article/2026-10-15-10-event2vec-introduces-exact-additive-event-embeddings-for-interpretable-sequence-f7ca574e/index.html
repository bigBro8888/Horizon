Event2Vec introduces exact additive event embeddings for interpretable sequence modeling

News time: 2026-10-15T05:52:02.419311+00:00 · Source: reddit

Event2Vec is a new open-source library that rethinks how discrete event sequences—like user clicks, medical codes, or system logs—are embedded into vector space. Unlike black-box sequence models such as LSTMs or Transformers, Event2Vec enforces a deliberately simple arithmetic structure: the representation of a history up to time t is defined as the exact sum of individual event vectors, hₜ = hₜ₋₁ + eₓₜ, with optional norm clipping. This design ensures that any linear downstream score—say, a risk or churn prediction—decomposes cleanly into per-event contributions: score = w·hₜ + b = Σᵢ (eᵢ·w) + b. That means analysts can directly attribute model output to specific events, enabling root-cause analysis without post-hoc approximation.

The method is trained self-supervisedly using next-event prediction (p(xₜ₊₁ | hₜ)) plus a reconstruction loss that encourages hₜ − hₜ₋₁ to recover eₓₜ. No labels are required. Crucially, while standard Euclidean embeddings are commutative (e₁ + e₂ = e₂ + e₁), Event2Vec’s training procedure breaks symmetry by learning from ordered sequences—so although addition is mathematically commutative in Euclidean space, the learned vectors reflect temporal order through distributional bias and initialization. To go further, the library also supports non-commutative geometries: hyperbolic space (via Möbius addition), κ-stereographic space (a unified curvature parameterization), spherical space, SO(n) rotations, and even the Heisenberg group—where order affects not just the endpoint but also a signed-area coordinate capturing sequence geometry.

The motivation came from real-world debugging at Berkeley Lab’s Advanced Light Source, where researchers needed to trace fault sequences in particle accelerator logs. Naive reuse of Word2Vec failed because it ignored directionality and order-sensitive causality. Event2Vec bridges the gap between symbolic interpretability and neural scalability—offering scikit-learn–style APIs (fit, transform, most_similar), PyTorch backend support, and cross-platform hardware compatibility (CPU/CUDA/MPS). Its documentation includes interactive visualizations, and the code is MIT-licensed.

What sets Event2Vec apart isn’t just simplicity—it’s theoretical fidelity to additive decomposition. Most embedding methods approximate linearity or rely on attention-weighted sums that don’t decompose exactly. Event2Vec guarantees it. That makes it especially valuable for high-stakes domains like healthcare or infrastructure monitoring, where understanding why a model flagged a sequence matters as much as accuracy.
