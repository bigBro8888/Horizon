Aggressive consistency scheduling blurs temporal boundaries in AST-CRNN noise classifier

News time: 2026-10-15T05:52:02.419526+00:00 · Source: reddit

A researcher developing a binary noise classification system has hit a subtle but critical roadblock: while overall detection accuracy remains acceptable, the model consistently misplaces the precise onset and offset times of noise events. The architecture combines an Audio Spectrogram Transformer (AST) backbone—first frozen, then fine-tuned—with a bidirectional CRNN head and Mean Teacher semi-supervised learning in stage two. Though this pipeline leverages strong audio representations and label-efficient training, evaluation reveals that most errors are not missed detections, but timing mismatches—events predicted too early, too late, or smeared across frames.

The issue worsened sharply during semi-supervised training. The researcher attributes the degradation to an overly aggressive consistency weight schedule in the Mean Teacher framework: the λ(t) coefficient, which scales the importance of teacher-student output agreement, ramped up too quickly and too high. As a result, the student model was forced to conform rigidly to a smoothed teacher prediction—even when those predictions lacked sharp temporal structure. This eroded crisp event boundaries early in training, with measured boundary variation exceeding the intended 14% tolerance.

Current metrics reflect the trade-off: an Event F1 score of just 0.284 and Dice score of 0.635 yield a combined score of only 0.919—well below the target of 1.5. Input preprocessing uses 5-second audio windows and batch size 8, standard for many SED tasks, but may interact poorly with aggressive consistency regularization. The researcher explicitly seeks tuning guidance—not architectural replacement—preferring to retain the AST-CRNN-Mean Teacher stack while fixing its temporal fidelity.

This case highlights a real tension in weakly supervised sound event detection: consistency regularization improves label efficiency but risks sacrificing temporal precision when misaligned with model capacity and scheduler design. Unlike image segmentation, where spatial smoothing is often tolerable, audio event detection demands millisecond-level alignment for practical applications like industrial monitoring or assistive listening. The fix likely lies not in abandoning Mean Teacher, but in calibrating its ramp-up curve, adding temporal-aware losses, or decoupling consistency enforcement across frequency vs. time dimensions.
