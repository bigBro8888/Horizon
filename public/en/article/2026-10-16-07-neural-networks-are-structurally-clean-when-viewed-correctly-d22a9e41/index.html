Neural networks are structurally clean when viewed correctly

News time: 2026-10-16T05:48:26.194772+00:00 · Source: hackernews

A provocative new essay titled Neural networks are clean challenges a widespread assumption in AI research: that neural networks are inherently messy, entangled, and inscrutable. Instead, the author argues that what appears as ‘polysemanticity’—a single neuron responding to multiple unrelated concepts like cats and cars—is not evidence of fundamental disorder, but rather a symptom of mismatched abstractions. When we reframe neurons not as detectors of high-level human categories, but as implementers of simpler, geometric or functional primitives—like edge detectors, curve templates, or logical gates—their behavior becomes surprisingly consistent and interpretable.

This perspective draws on years of mechanistic interpretability work, including early efforts at OpenAI where the author co-authored foundational studies. Those projects initially led to pessimism: networks seemed too tangled to reverse-engineer. But newer analyses—especially of transformer attention patterns, MLP feature circuits, and activation sparsity—suggest that many apparent complexities dissolve under better conceptual lenses. For example, a neuron previously labeled ‘cat-and-car’ may instead encode a specific V-shaped contour common to both objects’ silhouettes—a far cleaner, more generalizable role.

The claim isn’t that all neurons are monosemantic or that interpretability is trivial. Rather, it’s that polysemanticity is often structured, not random—and that parameter overloading (where one weight serves multiple functions) reflects efficient circuit design, not irreducible confusion. The post catalogs repeated failures of older interpretability tools—not because networks are chaotic, but because those tools imposed flawed assumptions about what counts as a ‘feature’ or a ‘concept’.

If correct, this reframing could accelerate alignment research. Instead of treating neural nets as black boxes requiring brute-force probing, researchers might systematically map their computational ‘grammar’: how basic features compose into higher-level representations, how circuits reuse components across tasks, and how training dynamics stabilize clean functional roles. As one commenter put it, it’s less about ‘sequencing the AI genome’ and more about finally reading the genetic code with the right codon table.
