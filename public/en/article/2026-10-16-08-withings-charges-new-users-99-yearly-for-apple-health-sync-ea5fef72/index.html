Withings charges new users $99 yearly for Apple Health sync

News time: 2026-10-16T05:48:26.194858+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Withings has quietly introduced a $99-per-year subscription fee for Apple Health synchronization — but only for users who create new accounts after a recent policy change. Existing users retain free access to this feature, creating a sharp divide between legacy and new customers. The move affects all Withings devices — including smart scales, blood pressure monitors, and activity trackers — that previously advertised seamless Apple Health integration as a core selling point.

The backlash erupted almost immediately on Withings’ official support community, where users labeled the change ‘unacceptable’ and accused the company of bait-and-switch marketing. Many report purchasing devices specifically because Apple Health sync was prominently featured in product listings, packaging, and promotional materials — yet now find the functionality locked behind a steep paywall. Legal concerns have surfaced too: one commenter cited Apple’s App Store Review Guidelines (Section 4.10), which prohibit developers from charging for access to built-in OS capabilities like HealthKit — a rule Apple itself enforces against third-party apps.

Customer service experiences further fuel frustration. A widely shared anecdote describes a multi-day support loop where Withings agents repeatedly asked the user to re-try basic troubleshooting steps — despite those steps having been documented and confirmed as ineffective in the initial request. This history compounds perceptions of neglect and erodes trust, especially when paired with opaque communication about the new pricing model.

While Withings positions the fee as part of a broader ‘premium health insights’ tier, critics argue it contradicts the company’s stated mission of delivering ‘clinically validated health devices embedded in beautifully designed, easy-to-use products.’ For consumers managing chronic conditions or tracking long-term wellness metrics, losing frictionless, on-device health data flow isn’t just inconvenient — it undermines interoperability, a foundational principle of modern digital health. The episode serves as a cautionary case study in how hardware-first health tech brands risk alienating users when subscription models override functional expectations.
