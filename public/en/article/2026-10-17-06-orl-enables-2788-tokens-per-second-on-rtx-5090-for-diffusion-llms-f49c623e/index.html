ORL enables 2788 tokens per second on RTX 5090 for diffusion LLMs

News time: 2026-10-17T05:33:28.244784+00:00 · Source: reddit

A new open-source framework called ORL (On-Policy Residual Learning) has dramatically accelerated diffusion-based language models (dLLMs), achieving up to 2,788 tokens per second on a single (hypothetical) RTX 5090 GPU — and 7,708 tokens/s across eight NVIDIA B200 GPUs. Unlike traditional autoregressive LLMs that generate text token-by-token, dLLMs refine entire sequences iteratively through denoising steps — a paradigm long held back by slow convergence and high computational cost. ORL tackles this bottleneck head-on with two novel ideas: first, on-policy residual learning, where the model refines its own prior predictions as residuals instead of restarting from scratch at each step; second, adjacent entropy contraction, a training objective that explicitly encourages each denoising step to reduce uncertainty relative to the previous one — while gating loss to avoid over-correcting confident predictions.

The performance gains are backed by rigorous benchmarking across three distinct dLLM families. On QwenDLM-4B, ORL boosts end-to-end throughput by 6.9× and lifts HumanEval pass@1 from 82.9% to 89.6%. For the lightweight LLaDA-2.1-mini, just 4.2 million self-generated tokens and 36 H100-hours of post-training yield up to 3× higher throughput. Even on the massive DiffusionGemma 26B MoE — already highly parallel — ORL delivers an extra 1.2× tokens per forward pass. Crucially, these speedups come without quality trade-offs: in head-to-head comparisons on a single H100, QwenDLM-4B + ORL hits 1,298 tokens/s, outpacing DFlash (746 tokens/s) and EAGLE3 (405 tokens/s) at matched accuracy.

To unlock this performance, the team built dlmrt — a custom inference engine optimized exclusively for low-latency, single-request diffusion decoding. Instead of launching each denoising step from CPU (with costly synchronization), dlmrt captures the full denoising loop in a CUDA graph and runs it entirely on GPU. It also supports NVFP4 quantization on Blackwell GPUs — including consumer-grade RTX 5090 hardware. In benchmarks, dlmrt runs 1.2–1.6× faster than SGLang or vLLM for identical models and settings. Training is similarly accelerated: co-designed kernels for residual-state capture, specialized attention, and compute-balanced packing deliver up to 5.2× faster training steps.

Everything — models, training code, evaluation pipelines, and dlmrt — is fully open source. The paper is on arXiv, interactive demos are live, and GitHub repos host production-ready implementations. While the RTX 5090 remains unreleased (as noted by community commenters), the reported numbers reflect realistic extrapolations using Blackwell architecture assumptions — and the real-world impact lies in proving that diffusion LLMs can now rival or exceed speculative decoding in both speed and fidelity.
