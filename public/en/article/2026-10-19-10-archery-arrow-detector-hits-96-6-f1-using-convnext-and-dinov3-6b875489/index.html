Archery arrow detector hits 96.6% F1 using ConvNeXt and DINOv3

News time: 2026-10-19T06:08:52.621327+00:00 · Source: reddit

A solo practitioner has built a highly accurate computer vision system for detecting arrow impacts on archery targets — achieving a 96.6% F1 score on held-out test data. The solution combines modern self-supervised pretraining with careful domain-specific engineering: it uses a ConvNeXt backbone pretrained on Meta’s DINOv3, trained on a hybrid dataset of 40,000 synthetic Blender-rendered scenes and 8,000 hand-labeled real-world images. Unlike many academic benchmarks, this work confronts real deployment constraints — from the tedium of labeling thousands of real arrows (20 hours of manual labor) to correcting for non-planar paper warping after perspective rectification.

The pipeline is split into two stages: registration and detection. First, a registration network identifies the target’s center, concentric scoring rings, and orientation using semantic segmentation. Then, a cropped region around the expected impact zone is fed to a detection network that outputs a heatmap of likely arrow positions. To boost robustness, the model runs inference on both the original and horizontally mirrored image, then fuses the two heatmaps in the decoder — a technique that helps resolve ambiguity near symmetry axes and improves localization precision. This mirrored fusion outperformed sliding-window baselines.

After detecting candidate impacts, the system applies a homography transform to normalize perspective distortion and map coordinates into a canonical circular target layout. But because real targets warp unevenly (e.g., due to humidity or mounting), a second correction step compares the detected ring heatmap against ideal geometric rings and applies a radial offset — effectively compensating for physical deformation beyond what homography can fix. The final metric — 80.5% whole-image correctness — means no false positives and no missed arrows across entire test frames, a stringent requirement for automated scoring systems.

What stands out is not just performance, but methodological transparency: ablations show ConvNeXt+DINOv3 clearly surpasses ImageNet-pretrained ResNets and pure Vision Transformers on this task; heavy augmentation expands the 48k-image dataset to ~1M training samples; and every design choice — from mirroring to radial correction — addresses a concrete failure mode observed in practice. It’s a rare example of production-grade thinking applied to a niche but high-stakes vision problem.
