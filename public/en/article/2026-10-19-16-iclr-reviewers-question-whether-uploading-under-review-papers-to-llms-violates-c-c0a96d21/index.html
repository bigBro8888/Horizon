ICLR reviewers question whether uploading under-review papers to LLMs violates confidentiality

News time: 2026-10-19T06:08:52.621432+00:00 · Source: reddit

A reviewer participating in the International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR) has raised a timely and ethically charged question on Reddit: Is it permissible to upload confidential, under-review manuscripts to commercial large language models (LLMs) for assistance during peer review? The post—submitted by u/howtorewriteaname—reflects growing uncertainty among academic reviewers who want to leverage AI tools responsibly but lack clear policy guidance. While the reviewer acknowledges that AI use must be disclosed and avoids relying on LLMs for substantive technical evaluation, they hesitate to feed full papers into external APIs, fearing potential breaches of confidentiality or dual-use restrictions.

ICLR’s official guidelines emphasize strict confidentiality and prohibit dual submissions from the moment of submission through decision notification—but they were written before widespread LLM adoption and contain no explicit language about AI-assisted review. This silence creates a gray zone: uploading a paper to a commercial LLM may expose proprietary content to third-party servers, potentially violating data sovereignty expectations and institutional ethics requirements. As noted in recent analyses, such practices could also introduce subtle biases, undermine reviewer accountability, or conflict with publisher policies requiring human judgment as the final arbiter.

The concern is not hypothetical. A June 2026 arXiv survey highlights that AI-assisted review tools must comply with institutional ethics, publisher policies, and applicable law—yet few venues have updated their frameworks accordingly. Meanwhile, Pangram’s analysis of ICLR 2025 reviews estimated that 21% showed strong indicators of AI generation, underscoring how quickly norms are shifting—even without formal rules. This gap between practice and policy places individual reviewers at risk: if an LLM leaks or retains sensitive content, responsibility falls squarely on the human reviewer under ICLR’s Code of Ethics.

Ultimately, this question reflects a broader tension in scholarly communication: how to harness AI’s analytical power while preserving trust, transparency, and intellectual ownership. Until official guidance emerges—such as explicit clauses in future ICLR reviewer handbooks or integration of AI-use protocols into submission systems—reviewers are left to navigate uncharted terrain using personal judgment, professional caution, and informal community consensus.
