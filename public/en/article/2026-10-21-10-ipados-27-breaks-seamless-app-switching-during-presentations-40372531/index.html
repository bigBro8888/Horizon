iPadOS 27 breaks seamless app switching during presentations

News time: 2026-10-21T05:59:49.235149+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Apple’s latest iPadOS 27 update has introduced a frustrating regression for professional presenters: the loss of flexible screen-sharing control during live slide deck presentations. Previously, on iPadOS 26, users could run a Keynote or PowerPoint presentation on an external display while simultaneously preparing or launching another app—like a live coding environment, browser demo, or spreadsheet—for on-the-fly screen sharing. The workflow involved swiping up to access the Dock, tapping the desired app, and having it instantly take over the external output without exiting the presentation entirely.

This capability was never officially documented as a feature—but it was widely relied upon by educators, consultants, and developers who use iPads in hybrid or remote settings. It mirrored the behavior of macOS with multiple displays: one screen showing slides, another showing live content, all under user control. With iPadOS 27, that flow no longer works. Attempting to switch apps mid-presentation forces the system to exit presentation mode entirely—killing the slideshow and requiring a full restart to resume.

The change appears tied to how iPadOS now handles external display ownership. Instead of allowing dynamic delegation of the external screen to different apps, the OS now locks that output to the presenting app until the presentation ends. There’s no prompt, no toggle, and no setting to restore the prior behavior—even in Settings or Accessibility menus. Apple has not acknowledged the issue publicly, nor offered a workaround in its official support documentation.

For many professionals, this isn’t just an inconvenience—it undermines a core value proposition of the iPad as a portable presentation tool. Unlike laptops, iPads lack physical ports for secondary monitors, so AirPlay-based external display usage is central to their utility in conference rooms or classrooms. When the software removes agency from the presenter at a critical moment—like pivoting from a static slide to a live demo—the device feels less like a professional instrument and more like a curated media player. As one commenter put it: 'On a real computer, it’s just an extra screen—you put what you want on it.'

The backlash reflects deeper concerns about Apple’s shifting design priorities: diminishing user control, inconsistent QA across major releases, and a growing gap between marketing claims ('powerful enough for pros') and real-world workflows. iPadOS 27 was billed as a quality-focused update, yet early adopters report cascading bugs—from phantom HomePod remote clicks to unstable AirPlay handoffs—suggesting systemic testing gaps.
