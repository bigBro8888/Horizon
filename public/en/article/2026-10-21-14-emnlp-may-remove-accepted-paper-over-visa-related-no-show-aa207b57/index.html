EMNLP may remove accepted paper over visa-related no-show

News time: 2026-10-21T05:59:49.598401+00:00 · Source: reddit

A researcher recently posted on Reddit expressing deep concern that their EMNLP-accepted paper—months of work and their first major publication—may be excluded from the official proceedings because they cannot attend the conference in person due to visa denial. The authors had secured a poster slot and proactively contacted the EMNLP organizing team, hoping to switch to a virtual presentation option. However, they were informed that virtual participation is no longer available for their submission. Despite also reaching out to program chairs and other organizers, replies have not arrived in time, likely due to the proximity of the conference date.

This situation highlights a persistent tension in top-tier NLP conferences like EMNLP, ACL, and NAACL: strict no-show policies designed to maintain program integrity versus real-world barriers faced by researchers from visa-restricted countries. While many conferences officially state that failure to present—even with valid reasons—can lead to removal from the proceedings, enforcement varies. Some past EMNLP and ACL editions have allowed exceptions when authors provided documentation and communicated early; others have upheld the policy rigidly, especially when deadlines for camera-ready submissions or registration passed.

For early-career researchers, especially those without institutional support or prior conference experience, such uncertainty carries high stakes. A missing publication in a flagship venue like EMNLP can delay graduation, hinder job applications, and weaken grant proposals. The emotional weight is palpable in the original post: the paper represents over a year’s effort, and its potential removal feels like a professional setback unrelated to scientific merit.

The broader community has long debated how to balance inclusivity and logistical feasibility. Proposals include expanding virtual attendance options, decoupling publication from mandatory presentation, and creating formal appeal pathways for documented visa failures. Until such policies are standardized, affected authors are advised to document all communications, submit proof of visa denial (e.g., embassy rejection letters), and request written confirmation of any accommodation—or lack thereof—from the organizers. Even if the paper is ultimately withdrawn, retaining a preprint and citing it as ‘accepted to EMNLP 2024 (not presented)’ remains common practice among researchers navigating similar hurdles.
