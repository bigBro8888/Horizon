New Mexico wins $25 billion penalty against Meta over Cambridge Analytica

News time: 2026-10-22T06:05:39.885709+00:00 · Source: hackernews

In a landmark enforcement action, the New Mexico Department of Justice has secured a $25 billion penalty against Meta Platforms Inc. — the parent company of Facebook — for its role in the Cambridge Analytica data scandal. The penalty stems from a state-led lawsuit alleging that Meta knowingly permitted the unauthorized harvesting and misuse of millions of users’ personal data, violating New Mexico’s Unfair Practices Act and consumer protection laws. Attorney General Hector Balderas announced the judgment as a historic victory for digital privacy rights and state-level accountability.

Unlike the multistate settlement finalized earlier this year — which included 47 states and yielded just $459 million specifically for Cambridge Analytica-related claims — New Mexico opted to pursue independent legal action. That decision appears to have paid off dramatically: the $25 billion figure dwarfs not only the multistate payout but also exceeds Meta’s entire net income for fiscal year 2025 ($22.1 billion), raising questions about enforceability and likely triggering an immediate appeal. Legal experts note that while the penalty is unprecedented in scale for a state enforcement action, it functions more as a statutory maximum or negotiating lever than a guaranteed collection amount.

Deposition testimony from Mark Zuckerberg and Sheryl Sandberg — recently made public by the New Mexico AG’s office — played a pivotal role in the case. Video recordings show both executives struggling to recall basic facts about Facebook’s data-sharing policies and oversight mechanisms during questioning. Critics argue these depositions exposed systemic negligence at the highest levels, reinforcing arguments that corporate liability should extend beyond fines to include personal accountability for executives.

The timing of the announcement — just days before the November 3 gubernatorial election — has drawn scrutiny. While the lawsuit was originally filed under former Attorney General Balderas (who left office in December 2025), the final judgment was issued under his successor, who is now running for reelection. Observers note that the win bolsters the incumbent’s platform on tech accountability, even as skeptics warn that political optics shouldn’t overshadow judicial rigor. Ultimately, this case signals a growing willingness among state attorneys general to challenge Big Tech with aggressive, high-stakes litigation — especially where federal regulators have moved slowly or settled lightly.
