Panache reimagines Battle Drones as a French Belle Époque steampunk RTS

News time: 2026-10-22T06:05:39.459157+00:00 · Source: rss

Just one day after Simon Willison open-sourced his real-time strategy game Battle Drones under the Apache 2.0 license, a vibrant community fork emerged: Panache, a fully French-language, mobile- and browser-compatible RTS set in an alternate-history Belle Époque France circa 1900.

Unlike the original’s near-future drone warfare aesthetic, Panache swaps sleek robotics for brass-and-steam grandeur — think armored steam wagons (Fardiers à vapeur), dirigibles, marching fortress-mechs named Gargantua, and automata wearing regulation képis. The visual overhaul is comprehensive: buildings gleam in gold and red, biplanes share skies with airships, and the flagship Goliath mech has been transformed from a blue sci-fi stomper into an ornate, gear-laden French imperial war machine.

The adaptation goes beyond skin-deep changes. A newly commissioned animated intro welcomes players, and the soundtrack has been reorchestrated with distinctly French musical motifs — waltzes, accordion flourishes, and period-appropriate instrumentation — reinforcing the setting’s cultural texture. Though gameplay mechanics remain identical to Battle Drones (including its audio-based multiplayer system), the shift in tone and context creates a strikingly different emotional experience: less techno-thriller, more Jules Verne meets Toulouse-Lautrec.

This rapid, high-fidelity fork exemplifies how permissive licensing and modular game architecture can catalyze cross-cultural creativity. Developed by the GitHub user @menufactory43, Panache doesn’t just translate — it transplants the game into a rich historical imaginary, leveraging steampunk not as generic retro-futurism but as a lens for French national identity, technological optimism, and Belle Époque elegance. You can play it instantly in any modern browser or on mobile at panache.menufactory43.github.io — no install, no account, just a click and the whir of steam valves.
