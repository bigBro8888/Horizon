eMudhra exits global SSL/TLS certificate business after trust failure

News time: 2026-10-23T06:00:37.664798+00:00 · Source: hackernews

Indian cybersecurity firm eMudhra has announced it will fully withdraw from the global SSL/TLS certificate market — a move triggered by a critical operational failure that undermined confidence in its Certificate Authority (CA) infrastructure. The company confirmed the exit in an official press release, citing the need to “reassess its strategic direction” following intense scrutiny from browser vendors and the broader security community. While eMudhra continues to operate its domestic digital identity services in India — including Aadhaar-based authentication and e-signature platforms — its global CA operations, which issued publicly trusted TLS certificates for websites worldwide, are being wound down immediately.

The incident appears to involve a serious breach of CA operational requirements, likely tied to private key handling or misissuance. Though eMudhra has not publicly disclosed full technical details, browser vendors Chrome and Mozilla launched formal investigations, with Mozilla filing a detailed public inquiry on Bugzilla (Bug #2083449). The Chrome team reportedly submitted a rigorous 27-question battery to eMudhra — a rare and severe step indicating deep concern over process integrity and cryptographic hygiene.

What makes this especially consequential is how foundational CAs are to internet trust. Every time a user visits an HTTPS site, their browser validates the site’s certificate against a chain anchored in a root CA — like eMudhra’s — embedded in the browser’s trust store. If that root is compromised or mismanaged, attackers could forge certificates for any domain, enabling undetectable man-in-the-middle attacks. Browser vendors don’t take such risks lightly: past CA failures (e.g., DigiNotar, Symantec) led to mass distrust and root certificate removals — actions that break encryption for millions of users overnight.

eMudhra’s exit avoids that nuclear option but signals a loss of confidence too severe to recover from in the global PKI ecosystem. Its certificates remain technically valid until expiration, but no new ones will be issued, and major browsers may accelerate deprecation timelines. For enterprises and developers relying on eMudhra-issued certs, migration to alternative CAs — such as Let’s Encrypt, Sectigo, or DigiCert — is now urgent. The episode also reignites debate about CA centralization, audit rigor, and whether the current web trust model can scale without recurring fragility.
