Open-XTTS-Trainer enables full end-to-end training of XTTS v2

News time: 2026-10-23T06:00:38.024647+00:00 · Source: reddit

When Coqui AI abruptly shut down its open-source TTS initiative, the widely adopted XTTS v2 model was left without official training code for two of its three core components: the Discrete VAE (DVAE) audio tokenizer and the Latent HiFi-GAN vocoder. While fine-tuning the GPT backbone remained possible, researchers and developers struggled to adapt XTTS v2 to new languages—especially those with non-Latin scripts like Arabic, Hindi, or Thai—due to hardcoded vocabularies and brittle dependencies. This gap stalled multilingual deployment and hindered reproducible research.

Enter Open-XTTS-Trainer, an open-source, modular training suite built by developer Amit Bhakt. Released under the Mozilla Public License 2.0, it provides verified, GPU-tested training pipelines for all three XTTS v2 stages: DVAE tokenization, GPT-based text modeling with dynamic vocabulary extension, and latent-conditioned HiFi-GAN vocoding. Crucially, it solves the ‘tokenizer wall’ by scanning raw text corpora to auto-discover Unicode glyphs, then safely appends them to the tokenizer and resizes the GPT embedding matrix—without corrupting pretrained weights.

The suite is dependency-robust: it runs natively on modern PyTorch versions (3.10–3.13, PyTorch 2.4+), eliminating legacy blockers like torchcodec. Benchmarks on an NVIDIA T4 GPU using LJSpeech and JFK speech data confirm convergence: DVAE mel reconstruction error dropped 67% (8.14 → 2.71), HiFi-GAN mel loss fell 67% (190.83 → 63.49), and discriminator losses stabilized cleanly—indicating stable adversarial training. A one-click Google Colab demo lowers the barrier to entry for newcomers and educators alike.

Beyond technical completeness, Open-XTTS-Trainer restores agency to the open TTS community. It transforms XTTS v2 from a static inference model into a truly adaptable, trainable system—enabling zero-shot TTS for underserved languages, domain-specific voice cloning, and pedagogical experimentation. With GitHub, Hugging Face, and Colab support—all under an OSI-approved license—it sets a new standard for transparency and maintainability in open speech synthesis.
