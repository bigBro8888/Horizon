WhissleAI shrinks steerable TTS to 11.84 MB with int4 quantization

News time: 2026-10-23T06:00:38.024751+00:00 · Source: reddit

WhissleAI has released SteerTTS-edge, a fully self-contained, ultra-compact English text-to-speech system designed for on-device deployment. At just 11.84 MB — including model weights, 200 preloaded voices, a lightweight text frontend, and an embedded Python runtime — it represents one of the smallest fully steerable TTS systems publicly available. Unlike cloud-dependent alternatives, SteerTTS-edge runs entirely offline on consumer-grade CPUs, enabling real-time voice synthesis in resource-constrained environments like mobile apps, embedded assistants, or privacy-sensitive applications.

The engineering breakthrough lies in how WhissleAI preserved controllability while aggressively shrinking the model. The acoustic model — built on a hybrid architecture combining attention and bidirectional Mamba-3 layers — contains only 9.25 million parameters and was trained using quantization-aware training (QAT) to int4 precision. Its companion vocoder is a distilled 6.2-million-parameter variant of Vocos, also int4 QAT-trained. Crucially, the text frontend avoids heavy dependencies: it uses pure NumPy for grapheme-to-phoneme conversion and prosody parsing, eliminating NLTK, torchaudio, g2p_en, and num2words at runtime.

Quality benchmarks show remarkable fidelity retention. Using the Open TTS Leaderboard protocol, SteerTTS-edge matches the original v1’s word error rate (WER) exactly at 0.96%, confirming unchanged intelligibility. Speech naturalness, measured by UTMOS, dips only slightly — from 4.20 to 4.11 — with most of that loss attributable to the int4 vocoder. All 12 numeric steering controls (e.g., pitch, rate, breathiness, pause duration) retain ≥95% of their fp32 effect, and inline markup like <slow> or [pause 400ms] works seamlessly. On a loaded 2018 Threadripper CPU, inference achieves 19× real-time speed (RTF 0.053) and delivers first audio in ~120 ms with sentence-level streaming.

Still, trade-offs exist. The edge version supports English only and ships with just 200 voices (down from 2,825), excludes voice cloning and free-text prompting, and dequantizes weights to fp32 at load — meaning size reduction doesn’t translate to faster inference unless custom int4 kernels are added later. The team explicitly invites feedback on vocoder quality and requests input on target runtimes: ONNX, mobile SDKs, or WebAssembly. This isn’t just a smaller model — it’s a deliberate step toward making expressive, user-controllable speech synthesis truly portable.
