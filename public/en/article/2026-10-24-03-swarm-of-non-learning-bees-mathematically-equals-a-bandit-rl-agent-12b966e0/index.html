Swarm of non-learning bees mathematically equals a bandit RL agent

News time: 2026-10-24T05:46:59.825718+00:00 · Source: reddit

A new theoretical paper proves something striking: a hive of honeybees—each incapable of individual learning, relying only on blind imitation—collectively implements the exact same mathematical update rule as a single reinforcement learning (RL) agent solving a multi-armed bandit problem. This isn’t an analogy or approximation; it’s a rigorous, closed-form equivalence derived from first principles in evolutionary dynamics.

The work centers on natural collective decision-making, like how honeybee swarms choose a new nest site. Scouts perform the waggle dance to advertise candidate locations, and uncommitted bees randomly follow dances—without evaluating quality or comparing options. Crucially, higher-quality sites elicit longer, more persistent dances, increasing their chance of being copied. The paper models this as a population-level process: the fraction of bees committed to each option evolves over time, and that evolution matches precisely the policy update of a novel RL algorithm the authors name Maynard-Cross Learning (MCL).

MCL emerges not from design but from aggregation: each bee acts as a single, noisy sample from the environment. With N bees, the effective learning rate becomes α = 1/N—meaning the swarm behaves like N parallel rollouts feeding into one shared policy. The derivation bridges two long-separated fields: swarm intelligence (SI), rooted in biology and evolutionary game theory, and RL, grounded in sequential decision theory. By mapping local imitation rules—like ‘copy the highest-payoff neighbor’ or ‘follow the first dance you see’—onto known bandit algorithms (e.g., Cross Learning), the authors show SI isn’t just inspired by learning—it is learning, at the macro scale.

This has concrete implications beyond theory. In AI, it suggests population-based training methods—such as evolutionary strategies or ensemble distillation—can be reinterpreted and optimized through an RL lens. In robotics, decentralized swarm controllers could inherit convergence guarantees from bandit theory. And for biologists, it transforms the ‘hive mind’ metaphor from poetic shorthand into a testable, quantitative framework: collective cognition need not require neural complexity, only structured social feedback. As the authors put it, the superorganism isn’t like a brain—it computes like one.
