Critical CVEs surged 25x since 2020 amid CNA expansion and AI-assisted discovery

News time: 2026-10-24T05:46:59.598622+00:00 · Source: hackernews

A new CVE Tracker dashboard at cve.0xosprey.com reveals a dramatic acceleration in vulnerability disclosures — especially those labeled 'critical' — with data showing critical CVEs increased roughly 25-fold since 2020, far outpacing the overall 5x rise in total CVEs. This isn’t just noise: the trend holds even when filtering for severity, suggesting a real shift in how vulnerabilities are identified, classified, and published.

The surge coincides with structural changes in the CVE ecosystem. Major open-source entities like GitHub and the Linux Kernel became official CVE Numbering Authorities (CNAs) in mid-2024 and mid-2025 respectively. As CNAs, they gained authority to assign CVE IDs directly — often via automated tooling — without routing through MITRE or waiting for manual review. This lowered the barrier to entry: one developer reported receiving a CVE for a beta Obsidian plugin over a path-traversal regex — a finding that likely wouldn’t have qualified just two years ago.

Another key driver appears to be AI-powered vulnerability discovery. Commenters note that while many feared AI would introduce more bugs into code, it’s instead proving highly effective at uncovering latent flaws in decades-old software — from legacy libraries to widely used open-source components. This aligns with observed spikes in CVEs tied to older, unmaintained projects where AI scanning tools are now systematically auditing large codebases at scale.

Yet the rise raises urgent questions about signal versus noise. With more CVEs comes greater pressure on downstream consumers — security teams, package maintainers, and DevOps engineers — to triage, patch, and verify fixes. If the bar for CVE assignment has meaningfully dropped, organizations risk wasting resources on low-risk issues while missing truly exploitable flaws. The tracker itself highlights this tension: users report UI bugs (like non-functional log-scale toggles), underscoring how quickly infrastructure struggles to keep pace with data volume.

Ultimately, the CVE explosion reflects not worsening software hygiene alone, but a confluence of decentralization, automation, and new discovery capabilities. It’s less a sign that code is getting dramatically worse — and more evidence that we’re finally seeing it more clearly.
