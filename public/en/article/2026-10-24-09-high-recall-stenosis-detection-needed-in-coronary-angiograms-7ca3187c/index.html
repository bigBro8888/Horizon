High-recall stenosis detection needed in coronary angiograms

News time: 2026-10-24T05:46:59.826130+00:00 · Source: reddit

A machine learning researcher is developing an AI system to detect potential coronary artery stenoses—narrowings that restrict blood flow—in X-ray coronary angiograms. Unlike typical object-detection tasks where precision and recall are balanced, this project explicitly prioritizes high recall: missing a true stenosis could delay life-saving intervention, while extra false positives can be filtered out by clinicians during review. The researcher reports early experiments with YOLO yielded promising results on one internal dataset but failed dramatically on another—largely due to inconsistent annotation practices: one used fixed-size bounding boxes centered on lesion points, the other fitted boxes tightly to lesion contours. This subtle difference turned out to represent two distinct detection tasks, exposing how annotation heterogeneity undermines model generalization.

To improve robustness, the researcher proposes shifting from generic object detection to a vessel-centered geometric approach—leveraging existing vessel segmentation and centerline data to compute radius profiles along the artery, flagging local narrowing as candidate stenoses. A secondary classifier could then refine these candidates. This aligns with established clinical methodology, where stenosis quantification relies on measuring luminal diameter changes relative to reference segments upstream and downstream.

But the most persistent challenge isn’t modeling—it’s evaluation. Clinical ground truth labels are inherently incomplete: radiologists mark only lesions they deem clinically significant or visible under current viewing conditions—not every anatomical narrowing. Computing recall against such sparse labels artificially inflates performance metrics. There’s no consensus on how to estimate true recall when the reference standard itself is partial and subjective.

The post invites expert input on prior work using vessel-centric modeling, public datasets beyond ARCADE (e.g., CATHETER, CORONA), and principled frameworks for evaluating AI under incomplete supervision. It also signals openness to paradigm shifts—acknowledging that framing stenosis detection as a standard bounding-box task may misalign with both clinical workflow and vascular anatomy.
