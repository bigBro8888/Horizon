Student shares study notes on Transformers and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo

News time: 2026-10-24T05:46:59.826063+00:00 · Source: reddit

A Reddit user going by /u/aybehrouz has published a set of personal study notes covering two foundational yet conceptually distinct topics in modern machine learning: the Transformer architecture and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC). The notes are not research papers or software releases, but rather pedagogical materials—carefully structured explanations intended to clarify core ideas for learners. The Transformer section draws directly from the seminal 2017 paper Attention Is All You Need, focusing on self-attention mechanisms, query-key-value computation, and the overall encoder-decoder flow. Meanwhile, the HMC notes explain how Hamiltonian dynamics—borrowed from physics—are used to improve sampling efficiency in Bayesian inference, especially when dealing with high-dimensional posterior distributions.

Unlike typical conference submissions or GitHub repos with benchmarks, these notes prioritize conceptual clarity over novelty or implementation. They include intuitive analogies—like comparing HMC’s proposal step to a frictionless hockey puck sliding across an energy landscape—and visualizable derivations of attention weights. The author explicitly invites feedback: corrections, suggestions for deeper coverage (e.g., variants like Nystromformer or Riemannian HMC), or even requests for worked examples. This openness reflects a growing culture of collaborative, low-barrier knowledge sharing in ML communities.

Both topics sit at important intersections: Transformers define the backbone of nearly all large language models, while HMC remains a gold-standard sampler for probabilistic programming and uncertainty-aware modeling—areas gaining renewed interest amid calls for more robust, interpretable AI. Yet they rarely appear together in introductory curricula. By bridging them in one accessible document, the notes serve as a rare dual-entry point—helping Bayesian newcomers grasp why attention isn’t just ‘magic’, and helping deep learning practitioners see how MCMC methods tackle problems gradients alone cannot solve.

The notes are hosted on Zenodo (a trusted open-research repository), with DOIs assigned for both parts—ensuring citability and long-term accessibility. While they lack empirical validation or code, their value lies in distillation: transforming dense technical literature into digestible, self-contained narratives. For students juggling theory-heavy coursework or self-taught engineers filling gaps in fundamentals, such resources fill a quiet but critical need—one that formal education often overlooks.
